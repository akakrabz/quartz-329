**7.9 Junction with an intrinsic layer** (page 07)
- Source: the old Summer 2020 HE1 #2a line → the required `Summer 2019 HE1 #1a style (oppositely charged slabs separated by a gap, $\epsilon_0$ kept symbolic) and Summer 2020 HE1 #2a (V inside a slab), extended to the junction of course notes Lecture 7, Example 4.`
- Problem found: two features were exactly those of Summer 2019 HE1 (conflict) #1a: the n slab (+4 C/m³ on 1 < z < 2 m) and the gap (−1 < z < 1 m). The p slab's −4 C/m² also matched. As a result, ε₀E_z at z = 0 and 1.5 m (−4 and −2) equalled that key's values.
- Change: every charge density × 1.5. ρ: −2 → −3 C/m³ on −3 < z < −1 m, and +4 → +6 C/m³ on 1 < z < 2 m. The geometry, the gap and part (d) are unchanged.
- Re-solved:
  - ε₀E_z: −2(z+3), −4, 4(z−2) → −3(z+3), −6, 6(z−2).
  - ε₀V: (z+3)², 4(z+2), 14 − 2(z−2)², 14 → (3/2)(z+3)², 6(z+2), 21 − 3(z−2)², 21.
  - V(−1), V(0), V(1): 4, 8, 12 → 6, 12, 18 (all /ε₀). V(0) ≈ 9.04×10¹¹ → 1.36×10¹² V.
  - Curvature: +2 and −4 → +3 and −6 (/ε₀).
  - Built-in potential: 14/ε₀ ≈ 1.58×10¹² → 21/ε₀ ≈ 2.37×10¹² V.
  - E_max: 4/ε₀ ≈ 4.52×10¹¹ → 6/ε₀ ≈ 6.78×10¹¹ V/m.
  - Without the intrinsic layer: 6/ε₀ ≈ 6.78×10¹¹ → 9/ε₀ ≈ 1.02×10¹² V. The difference 8/ε₀ → 12/ε₀ (= E_max·g). Area-rule check: 14 → 21.
- Sources paragraph: names both exams.
