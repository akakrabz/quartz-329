## Findings and changes
1. **Blocker, 17.10(c) Answer line.** It said "tangential B/μ0 jumps by 30x̂ − 100ŷ A/m". That vector is n̂×(B1−B2)/μ0, the total (free + bound) sheet current. The jump itself is (B1−B2)/μ0 = −100x̂ − 30ŷ A/m, i.e. B1−B2 = −1.26e-4 x̂ − 3.77e-5 ŷ T, rotated 90° from what was stated. Changes:
   - Body: now states B1−B2 = −100μ0x̂ − 30μ0ŷ = −1.26e-4 x̂ − 3.77e-5 ŷ T, then n̂×(B1−B2)/μ0 = 30x̂ − 100ŷ A/m, which is 20.9 × |J_s2| = 5 A/m.
   - Answer line: corrected to match.
   - (d): its back-reference now says "the n̂×(B1−B2)/μ0 found in (c)".
2. **Major, 17.12(d).** The parenthetical said B_z is continuous because "the current sits on the side wall, not on the face, so nothing makes B_z jump there". That implies a current on the face could make normal B jump, which it cannot. Replaced with: normal B is continuous across any surface (∇·B = 0), a surface current only makes the tangential components jump, and the on-axis formula is continuous at z = ℓ/2.
3. **Minor, 17.2 distractor (e).** "'Non-magnetic' means χm = 0" is not how the term is used. Reworded: it is everyday shorthand for μ ≈ μ0, not a class; only the vacuum has χm = 0, and the signs of the small susceptibilities classify P, Q and R.
4. **Minor, notation (SPEC §7).** The sheet formula used **K** in 17.6(d) and 17.10(d). Changed to $\tfrac{\mu_0}{2}\mathbf{J}_s\times\hat{n}$.
