> [!key] Propagation constant and intrinsic impedance of a conducting medium
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \gamma = \sqrt{j\omega\mu(\sigma + j\omega\epsilon)} = \alpha + j\beta\ \ [\text{m}^{-1}],\\[6pt]
> \eta = \sqrt{\frac{j\omega\mu}{\sigma + j\omega\epsilon}} = \lvert\eta\rvert e^{j\tau}\ \ [\Omega],\\[6pt]
> \gamma\eta = j\omega\mu,\qquad \frac{\gamma}{\eta} = \sigma + j\omega\epsilon .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> Roots with positive real part ($\sigma = 0$: $\gamma = +j\beta$): $\alpha\ge0$, $\beta>0$, $0\le\tau<45^\circ$; $\alpha = \tau = 0$ only if $\sigma = 0$. **Attenuation constant** $\alpha$ in nepers per metre (Np/m), **phase constant** $\beta$ in rad/m; both 1/m. Backwards (notes' margin; real $\sigma$, $\epsilon$):
> $$
> \mu = \frac{\gamma\eta}{j\omega},\qquad \sigma = \text{Re}\Big\{\frac\gamma\eta\Big\},\qquad \epsilon = \frac1\omega\,\text{Im}\Big\{\frac\gamma\eta\Big\} .
> $$

> [!key] An x-polarized plane wave in a conducting medium
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \tilde{\mathbf{E}} = \hat x\,E_0e^{\mp\gamma z},\qquad \tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \pm\hat y\,\frac{E_0}{\eta}\,e^{\mp\gamma z},\\[6pt]
> \mathbf{E} = \hat x\,\lvert E_0\rvert\,e^{\mp\alpha z}\cos(\omega t\mp\beta z + \angle E_0),\\[4pt]
> \mathbf{H} = \pm\hat y\,\frac{\lvert E_0\rvert}{\lvert\eta\rvert}\,e^{\mp\alpha z}\cos(\omega t\mp\beta z + \angle E_0 - \tau).
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> Upper signs: toward $+z$; lower: toward $-z$. Either way the wave **decays in the direction it travels**. Direction rule with complex $\eta$:
> $$
> \tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \frac1\eta\,\hat u\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}},\qquad \tilde{\mathbf{E}} = \eta\,\tilde{\mathbf{H}}\times\hat u .
> $$
> Dividing by $\eta$ divides by $\lvert\eta\rvert$ *and* subtracts $\tau$.

**Physics.** With $\tilde{\mathbf{J}} = \sigma\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$ ($\tilde\rho = 0$, [[concepts/phasor-maxwell-equations|Maxwell's equations in phasor form]]) only Ampère changes, $\nabla\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \sigma\tilde{\mathbf{E}} + j\omega\epsilon\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = (\sigma + j\omega\epsilon)\,\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$, so every lossless result carries over with $j\omega\epsilon\to\sigma + j\omega\epsilon$:

$$
\begin{gathered}
\gamma^2 = -\omega^2\mu\epsilon = (j\omega\mu)(j\omega\epsilon)\\[4pt]
\longrightarrow\quad \gamma^2 = (j\omega\mu)(\sigma + j\omega\epsilon),\\[8pt]
\eta = \sqrt{\frac{j\omega\mu}{j\omega\epsilon}}\quad\longrightarrow\quad \eta = \sqrt{\frac{j\omega\mu}{\sigma + j\omega\epsilon}} .
\end{gathered}
$$

Two readings of $\sigma + j\omega\epsilon$ (complex conductivity or complex permittivity $\epsilon_c = \epsilon - j\sigma/\omega$), the crossover frequency and the time-domain damped wave equation: [[concepts/phasor-maxwell-equations|Maxwell's equations in phasor form]]; the ratio $\sigma/\omega\epsilon$ is the [[concepts/loss-tangent|loss tangent]].

**Reading the damped wave.** $e^{\mp\gamma z} = e^{\mp\alpha z}e^{\mp j\beta z}$ decays and turns $2\pi$ per wavelength.
- **β sets the phase:** $\lambda = 2\pi/\beta$, $v_p = \omega/\beta = \lambda f$, with $\beta$ no longer $\propto\omega$: $v_p$ is not constant, nor $\lambda\propto1/f$. $v_p$ is the speed of the **nodes** (zero crossings), which decay cannot move; $\lambda$ is twice the node spacing. Frequency-dependent $v_p$ spreads pulses ([[concepts/dispersion-relation|Dispersion relation and phase velocity]]; [[concepts/group-velocity|Group velocity]]).
- **α sets the decay.** The **penetration depth**
$$
\delta\equiv\frac1\alpha = \frac{1}{\text{Re}\{\sqrt{j\omega\mu(\sigma + j\omega\epsilon)}\}}
$$
is where the amplitude is down by $e$, to 37 % (the [[concepts/skin-depth|skin depth]] when small); the power, $\propto e^{-2\alpha z}$, is at 13.5 %. One neper is a factor $e$: $20\log_{10}e = 8.686$ dB.
- **τ sets the timing:** $\mathbf{H}$ peaks $\tau/\omega$ after $\mathbf{E}$; in a snapshot it sits $\tau/\beta$ behind, on the source side ([[concepts/intrinsic-impedance|Intrinsic impedance]]).
- **Damped in space, steady in time:** at a fixed point the field is a steady sinusoid (the source feeds it); the amplitude decays from point to point.
- **Power:** $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^*\}$, for the $+z$ wave $\hat z\,(\lvert E_0\rvert^2/2\lvert\eta\rvert)\cos\tau\,e^{-2\alpha z}$, still along $\hat u$ ($\cos\tau>0$); the lost power is the $\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}>0$ Joule term of [[concepts/poynting-theorem|Poynting's theorem]].

> [!key] The general formulas (real, positive σ, ε, μ)
> Squaring $\gamma = \alpha + j\beta$ (Rao §5.3):
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> (\alpha + j\beta)^2 = \alpha^2 - \beta^2 + 2j\alpha\beta = -\omega^2\mu\epsilon + j\omega\mu\sigma\\[4pt]
> \Longrightarrow\quad \beta^2 - \alpha^2 = \omega^2\mu\epsilon,\qquad 2\alpha\beta = \omega\mu\sigma .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> With $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = \lvert\gamma\rvert^2 = \lvert j\omega\mu\rvert\,\lvert\sigma + j\omega\epsilon\rvert = \omega^2\mu\epsilon\sqrt{1 + (\sigma/\omega\epsilon)^2}$, add and subtract:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \alpha = \omega\sqrt{\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}}\left[\sqrt{1 + \Big(\frac{\sigma}{\omega\epsilon}\Big)^2} - 1\right]^{1/2},\\[8pt]
> \beta = \omega\sqrt{\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}}\left[\sqrt{1 + \Big(\frac{\sigma}{\omega\epsilon}\Big)^2} + 1\right]^{1/2},\\[8pt]
> \lvert\eta\rvert = \frac{\sqrt{\mu/\epsilon}}{\big[1 + (\sigma/\omega\epsilon)^2\big]^{1/4}},\\[8pt]
> \tau = \frac12\tan^{-1}\frac{\sigma}{\omega\epsilon},\qquad \angle\gamma = 90^\circ - \tau,\\[8pt]
> \frac\alpha\beta = \tan\tau,\qquad \frac{\sigma}{\omega\epsilon} = \tan2\tau = \frac{2\alpha\beta}{\beta^2 - \alpha^2} .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> Also $\lvert\gamma\rvert\lvert\eta\rvert = \omega\mu$. The notes take $\alpha$, $\beta$ as Re and Im of the root (the calculator route). **Angles:** $j\omega\mu$ is at 90°, $\sigma + j\omega\epsilon$ at $90^\circ - \tan^{-1}(\sigma/\omega\epsilon)$; $\eta$ (root of the ratio) takes half the difference, $\tau$; $\gamma$ (root of the product) half the sum, $90^\circ - \tau$. Hence $\alpha/\beta = \cot\angle\gamma = \tan\tau\le1$: **β ≥ α** (plasma excepted). Beyond the scales $\omega\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}$ and $\sqrt{\mu/\epsilon}$, only $\sigma/\omega\epsilon$ matters.

> [!recipe] Decoding a damped wave (the exam skill)
> 1. **Read off.** Opposite signs on the $t$ and position terms: travel toward $+$ that axis; same signs: toward $-$. $\omega$: coefficient of $t$; $\beta$: of position in the cosine; $\alpha$: in the exponential, which must decay along the travel. $\gamma = \alpha + j\beta$.
> 2. **η** $= j\omega\mu/\gamma$ with the medium's $\mu$: $\lvert\eta\rvert = \omega\mu/\lvert\gamma\rvert$, $\tau = 90^\circ - \angle\gamma = \tan^{-1}(\alpha/\beta)$.
> 3. **The other field.** $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \hat u\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}}/\eta$ (divide by $\lvert\eta\rvert$, subtract $\tau$) or $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = \eta\,\tilde{\mathbf{H}}\times\hat u$ (multiply, add $\tau$). Do the cross product; never guess.
> 4. **The medium.** $\gamma/\eta = \gamma^2/j\omega\mu = \sigma + j\omega\epsilon$: $\sigma = 2\alpha\beta/\omega\mu$, $\epsilon = (\beta^2 - \alpha^2)/\omega^2\mu$.
> 5. **The regime.** $\sigma/\omega\epsilon = 2\alpha\beta/(\beta^2 - \alpha^2) = \tan2\tau$.
> 6. **Sanity checks.** $\beta\ge\alpha$; $0\le\tau<45^\circ$; $\lvert\gamma\rvert\lvert\eta\rvert = \omega\mu$; $\epsilon\ge\epsilon_0$ for ordinary matter.

**Reading the regime from a wave** (limits on [[concepts/loss-tangent|Loss tangent]], [[concepts/skin-depth|Skin depth]]): $\beta\ge\alpha$, so "$\beta$ much smaller than $\alpha$" is impossible. Decay per wavelength $e^{-\alpha\lambda} = e^{-2\pi\tan\tau}$: $e^{-2\pi}\approx0.19\,\%$ in a good conductor, $\approx1 - \pi\sigma/\omega\epsilon$ in an imperfect dielectric. $\lambda/\delta = 2\pi\alpha/\beta = 2\pi\tan\tau$, so $\lambda<2\pi\delta$ always; good conductor $\lambda\approx2\pi\delta$, imperfect dielectric $\delta\gg\lambda$. "$\lambda$ longer than $\delta$" only means $\sigma/\omega\epsilon>0.33$, which includes the in-between range: not proof of a good conductor.

**Examples.**

> [!example] Example — decode $\mathbf{E} = \hat y\,6e^{-x}\cos(3\times10^8t - 3x)$ V/m in a non-magnetic medium
> Find $\gamma$, $\eta$, $\mathbf{H}$, $\sigma$, $\epsilon$, $\delta$, $\lambda$, $v_p$ ($\epsilon_0 = 10^{-9}/36\pi$ F/m).
> - **Read off.** Opposite signs: toward $+x$, with $e^{-x}$ decaying that way ✓. $\omega = 3\times10^8$ rad/s ($f = 47.7$ MHz), $\alpha = 1$ Np/m, $\beta = 3$ rad/m, $\gamma = 1 + j3 = \sqrt{10}\,e^{j71.6^\circ}$ m⁻¹.
> - **η.** $\omega\mu_0 = 3\times10^8\times4\pi\times10^{-7} = 120\pi$ Ω/m:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \eta = \frac{j120\pi}{1 + j3} = \frac{120\pi\,j(1 - j3)}{10} = 12\pi(3 + j)\\[6pt]
> = 113.1 + j37.7 = 119.2\,e^{j18.4^\circ}\ \Omega .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> Check: $\tan^{-1}(1/3) = 18.4^\circ$ ✓.
> - **H.** $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \hat x\times\hat y\,(6/\eta)\,e^{-\gamma x} = \hat z\,(6/119.2)\,e^{-j18.4^\circ}e^{-\gamma x}$ A/m: $\mathbf{H} = \hat z\,50.3\,e^{-x}\cos(3\times10^8t - 3x - 18.4^\circ)$ mA/m. Check: $\hat y\times\hat z = +\hat x$ ✓.
> - **The medium.** $\gamma^2 = -8 + j6$, $\gamma/\eta = (-8 + j6)/(j120\pi) = (6 + j8)/120\pi$: $\sigma = 6/120\pi = 1/20\pi = 15.9$ mS/m; $\omega\epsilon = 8/120\pi$ gives $\epsilon = 7.07\times10^{-11}$ F/m $= 8\epsilon_0$. $\sigma/\omega\epsilon = 3/4$: comparable currents.
> - **Lengths, speed.** $\delta = 1$ m, $\lambda = 2.09$ m, $v_p = 1.0\times10^8$ m/s, below the $c/\sqrt8 = 1.06\times10^8$ m/s of a lossless $\epsilon_r = 8$.
> - **Cross-check.** $\omega\sqrt{\mu\epsilon} = 2\sqrt2$ rad/m, $\sqrt{1 + (3/4)^2} = 5/4$: $\alpha = 2\sqrt{5/4 - 1} = 1$ ✓, $\beta = 2\sqrt{5/4 + 1} = 3$ ✓.

> [!example] Example — a medium at the crossover, where neither shortcut works
> $\mu = \mu_0$, $\epsilon = 4\epsilon_0$, $\sigma = 0.04$ S/m, $f = 180$ MHz: $\omega\epsilon = 2\pi(1.8\times10^8)(4\times10^{-9}/36\pi) = 0.04$ S/m, $\sigma/\omega\epsilon = 1$ (0.9986 with the exact $\epsilon_0$).
> - **Exact.** $\omega\sqrt{\mu\epsilon} = 2.4\pi = 7.540$ rad/m: $\alpha = (7.540/\sqrt2)\sqrt{\sqrt2 - 1} = 3.43$ Np/m, $\beta = (7.540/\sqrt2)\sqrt{\sqrt2 + 1} = 8.28$ rad/m, $\lvert\eta\rvert = 60\pi/2^{1/4} = 158.5\ \Omega$, $\tau = \tfrac12\tan^{-1}1 = 22.5^\circ$; $\delta = 0.291$ m, $\lambda = 0.758$ m, $v_p = 1.37\times10^8$ m/s.
> - **Imperfect-dielectric shortcut.** $\alpha\approx(\sigma/2)\sqrt{\mu/\epsilon} = 0.02\times60\pi = 3.77$ (10 % high), $\beta\approx7.54$ (9 % low), $\tau\approx0.5$ rad $= 28.6^\circ$.
> - **Good-conductor shortcut.** $\alpha\approx\beta\approx\sqrt{\omega\mu\sigma/2} = 5.33$: $\alpha$ 55 % high, $\beta$ 36 % low, $\tau = 45^\circ$.
>
> Near $\sigma = \omega\epsilon$, use the exact formulas.

- Lossless, non-magnetic, $\epsilon_r = 4$, 100 MHz: $\gamma = j\,4\pi/3 = j4.19$ m⁻¹, $\lambda = 1.5$ m.
- Toward $-x$: $e^{+\gamma x}$; $\gamma = 1 + j2$ m⁻¹ gives $\gamma^2 = -3 + j4$, a 3-4-5 triangle; $\mu = 3\mu_0$ ([[problems/decoding-a-damped-wave|worked problem]]).
- Plasma, $10^{12}$ m⁻³, $f_p\approx9$ MHz: real $\gamma$ below (0.156 m⁻¹ at 5 MHz: no oscillation), imaginary above ($j0.375$ m⁻¹ at 20 MHz) ([[concepts/conductivity-and-susceptibility-models|frequency-dependent σ]]).
- **Transmission-line twin** (the site's own): a coax with the lossy filling has $\mathcal{L} = (\mu/2\pi)\ln(b/a)$, $\mathcal{C} = 2\pi\epsilon/\ln(b/a)$, $\mathcal{G} = 2\pi\sigma/\ln(b/a)$. With $\mathcal{L}\mathcal{C} = \mu\epsilon$ ([[concepts/transmission-line-parameters|Transmission-line parameters 𝓛, 𝓒, 𝓖]]) and $\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{C} = \sigma/\epsilon$, its $\sqrt{j\omega\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G} + j\omega\mathcal{C})}$ is exactly $\gamma$, and $\sqrt{j\omega\mathcal{L}/(\mathcal{G} + j\omega\mathcal{C})} = \eta\ln(b/a)/2\pi$: the same problem. General line: $\gamma = \sqrt{(\mathcal{R} + j\omega\mathcal{L})(\mathcal{G} + j\omega\mathcal{C})}$.

> [!trap] Sign and phase traps with damped waves
> - **Which exponential.** $e^{-\gamma z} = e^{-\alpha z}e^{-j\beta z}$: toward $+z$; $e^{+\gamma z}$: toward $-z$. $e^{-\alpha z}\cos(\omega t + \beta z)$ would grow along its travel: impossible in a passive medium. $-\gamma$ is no new wave.
> - **The phase of H.** $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}/\eta$ subtracts $\tau$, $\eta\tilde{\mathbf{H}}$ adds it. With real $\mu$, $\epsilon$, $\sigma$ (every medium here), $\mathbf{H}$ lags $\mathbf{E}$.
> - **β is never smaller than α.** Real $\sigma$ puts $\angle\gamma$ between 45° and 90°; $\beta = \alpha$ only in the good-conductor limit. Exception: a plasma (imaginary $\sigma$), $\beta = 0$ below its plasma frequency; there $\alpha>0$ is not loss ($\eta$ imaginary, no average power enters; the wave reflects).
> - **Nepers and radians.** $\alpha$ multiplies distance in an exponential, $\beta$ in a cosine; both 1/m, not interchangeable. 1 Np = 8.686 dB; power decays as $e^{-2\alpha z}$, twice as fast as the field.
> - **Degrees and radians in one argument.** In $\cos(\omega t - 4x + 60^\circ)$, $\beta x$ is radians, the constant degrees: convert before adding $\tau$.
> - **What μ does.** $\mu$ enters $\eta = j\omega\mu/\gamma$, $\sigma = 2\alpha\beta/\omega\mu$ and $\epsilon = (\beta^2 - \alpha^2)/\omega^2\mu$: $\mu_0$ for a magnetic medium spoils all three, but not $\sigma/\omega\epsilon$, so the regime verdict survives (the [[problems/decoding-a-damped-wave|worked problem]], $\mu = 3\mu_0$, shows the factor of 3).

> [!exam] On exams
> One of the most reliably examined parts of Exam 3.
> 1. **Decode a damped wave** (recipe). SP17 Exam 3 #3: $\mathbf{E} = 3e^{-3x}\cos(8\pi10^6t - 4x + 60^\circ)\,\hat z$ V/m, non-magnetic: every constant ($\gamma$ in both forms, $\lambda$, $f$, $v_p$, $\lvert\eta\rvert$, $\tau$), then $\mathbf{H}$ with direction. Summer 2018 HE3 #1b: time-domain $\mathbf{H}$ ($\mu_0$) to $\epsilon$ and both phasors. SP19 Exam 3 #1: phasor $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$, $\mu = 2\mu_0$, to $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}$, $\sigma/\omega\epsilon$, regime.
> 2. **γ and η from one field.** SP18 Exam 3 #2a–c, lossless: $\gamma = j\beta$, $\eta = \omega\mu/\beta$ from $\eta\gamma = j\omega\mu$.
> 3. **Regime reasoning.** Summer 2020 HE3 #3a: can $\beta\ll\alpha$ at high frequency? Summer 2019 HE3 conflict #3b: $\delta\gg\lambda$, inequality on $\omega$.
> 4. **Given aids.** Stems print $\gamma\eta = j\omega\mu$, $\gamma/\eta = \sigma + j\omega\epsilon$, sometimes $\gamma$, $\eta$. Numbers land on 3-4-5 and 30-60-90 triangles ($\tan^{-1}(4/3) = 53^\circ$, $1 + j\sqrt3 = 2e^{j60^\circ}$).
> 5. **Recurring traps.** $\mu\neq\mu_0$; the sign of $\tau$ in $\mathbf{H}$; Np/m against rad/m; 60° against $\pi/3$; travel along $x$ or $y$ (use $\hat u\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$).
