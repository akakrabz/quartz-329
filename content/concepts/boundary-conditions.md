> [!key] The four boundary conditions ($\hat n$ from medium 2 into medium 1; the notes' $\pm$ superscripts mean the same)
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \hat n\cdot(\mathbf{D}_1-\mathbf{D}_2) = \rho_s,\qquad \hat n\cdot(\mathbf{B}_1-\mathbf{B}_2) = 0,\\[4pt]
> \hat n\times(\mathbf{E}_1-\mathbf{E}_2) = 0,\qquad \hat n\times(\mathbf{H}_1-\mathbf{H}_2) = \mathbf{J}_s .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> **Normal $\mathbf{D}$ jumps by the free surface charge; tangential $\mathbf{E}$ is continuous; normal $\mathbf{B}$ is continuous; tangential $\mathbf{H}$ jumps by the surface current.** Components: $D_{1n} - D_{2n} = \rho_s$ [C/m²]; $E_{1t} = E_{2t}$; $B_{1n} = B_{2n}$; $H_{1t} - H_{2t} = J_s$ [A/m]. *Nothing* is said about $D_t$, $E_n$, $H_n$ or $B_t$ (they jump at a material interface even with $\rho_s = 0$, $\mathbf{J}_s = 0$). Valid for time-varying fields too.

**Physics — where fields jump.** Differential laws need differentiable fields; at a **surface charge** they fail: the infinite sheet ([[concepts/symmetric-charge-distributions|Symmetric charge distributions]]), $\mathbf{E} = \hat{z}\dfrac{\rho_s}{2\epsilon_0}\text{sgn}(z)$, flips its $z$-component across the sheet. Add the smooth field $\mathbf{E}_0$ of the other sources:

$$
\mathbf{E} = \hat{z}\frac{\rho_s}{2\epsilon_0}\text{sgn}(z) + \mathbf{E}_0 .
$$

At $z=0^\pm$ the smooth part is the same, so **tangential** $E_x, E_y$ are continuous while the **normal component** jumps: $E_z(0^+) - E_z(0^-) = \rho_s/\epsilon_0$, i.e. $D_z(0^+) - D_z(0^-) = \rho_s$. Neither orientation nor flatness was used (a curved surface is flat on a small scale): this holds at *any* charged surface. The integral laws hold for **every** closed surface and path, straddling or not: apply them to a straddling shape and squash it flat; the magnetic conditions come free.

> [!derivation]- Pillbox and loop, step by step
> **Conventions.** Medium 1 on one side, medium 2 on the other; $\hat{n}$ is the unit normal **pointing from medium 2 into medium 1**; subscripts $n$, $t$ = normal, tangential components.
>
> **Gauss's law on a pillbox.** Caps of area $A$, one in each medium, side wall of height $h$. As $h\to0$ the side flux vanishes (finite field, vanishing area) and so does the enclosed *volume* charge; the *surface* charge $\rho_sA$ stays. Outward normals $+\hat{n}$ on the medium-1 cap, $-\hat{n}$ on the medium-2 cap:
> $$
> \oint\mathbf{D}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{D}_1\cdot\hat{n}A - \mathbf{D}_2\cdot\hat{n}A = \rho_sA \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \hat{n}\cdot(\mathbf{D}_1-\mathbf{D}_2) = \rho_s .
> $$
> The same pillbox with $\oint\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0$ gives $\hat{n}\cdot(\mathbf{B}_1-\mathbf{B}_2) = 0$ (no magnetic charge, no jump).
>
> **Faraday's law on a loop.** Rectangle of length $L$ along the surface, height $h$ across, one long edge in each medium. As $h\to0$ the short edges give nothing and the magnetic flux ($\propto Lh$) vanishes even if $\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t\neq0$; the long edges run in opposite directions:
> $$
> \oint\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = E_{1t}L - E_{2t}L = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad E_{1t} = E_{2t},\ \text{i.e.}\ \hat{n}\times(\mathbf{E}_1-\mathbf{E}_2) = 0 .
> $$
> ($\hat{n}\times$ picks out the tangential part, rotated by 90° — zero is zero.)
>
> **Ampère's law on the same loop.** $\oint\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = I_{\text{enc}} + \frac{d}{dt}\int\mathbf{D}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$. The displacement term dies with the area (width $w\to0$), as the $\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t$ flux did, but a **surface current** $\mathbf{J}_s$ [A/m] *in* the interface threads the loop with $I_{\text{enc}} = J_sL$ (component of $\mathbf{J}_s$ perpendicular to the loop). Hence $H_{1t} - H_{2t} = J_s$, or $\hat{n}\times(\mathbf{H}_1-\mathbf{H}_2) = \mathbf{J}_s$ (the [[concepts/current-sheets-and-slabs|current sheet]] in action). These four are the key box.
>
> The time-derivative terms vanish with the *area*, not because fields are static, so all four hold for time-varying fields (Lecture 16's "same for dynamics"; justified fully in Lecture 19).

> [!recipe] How to remember them
> In each differential Maxwell equation replace $\nabla\to\hat{n}$, each field by (field in 1) − (field in 2), $\rho\to\rho_s$, $\mathbf{J}\to\mathbf{J}_s$, $\partial/\partial t\to0$: $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{D}=\rho$ becomes $\hat{n}\cdot(\mathbf{D}_1-\mathbf{D}_2)=\rho_s$, $\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t$ becomes $\hat{n}\times(\mathbf{E}_1-\mathbf{E}_2)=0$, etc. Divergence equations constrain normal components, curl equations tangential ones. A *result* of squashing the integral laws, not a substitute.

**The normal vector is half the problem.** Every sign error here is an $\hat{n}$ error. *You* choose which side is medium 1; $\hat{n}$ must point into it, and flips when you re-choose at the next interface. Draw $\hat{n}$ at every interface before writing an equation. The conditions fix **differences**, never levels; levels come from a measured field, symmetry, superposition, or a conductor's potential. $\rho_s$ and $\mathbf{J}_s$ are **free** charge and current; bound charge sits inside $\mathbf{P}$ ($\hat{n}\cdot(\mathbf{P}_1-\mathbf{P}_2) = -\rho_{sb}$, [[concepts/polarization|Polarization P and bound charge]]), bound current inside $\mathbf{M}$.

**Special cases.**

> [!note] Field lines bend at a dielectric interface (Lecture 9 §2)
> Two perfect dielectrics ($\epsilon_1\neq\epsilon_2$), no free charge; split each field into normal and tangential parts:
> $$
> E_{2t} = E_{1t},\qquad \epsilon_2E_{2n} = \epsilon_1E_{1n}\ \Rightarrow\ E_{2n} = \frac{\epsilon_1}{\epsilon_2}E_{1n},
> \qquad\qquad \frac{\tan\theta_1}{\tan\theta_2} = \frac{\epsilon_1}{\epsilon_2}
> $$
> (angles from the normal). Entering the higher-$\epsilon$ medium the normal component shrinks: the line tilts toward the surface. $\mathbf{D}$, parallel to $\mathbf{E}$, bends by the same angle with roles swapped: normal part continuous, tangential part scaling with $\epsilon$. With free surface charge, $D_{1n} - D_{2n} = \rho_s$ replaces the second condition. Bound surface charge (with $\rho_s = 0$): $\rho_{sb} = P_{2n} - P_{1n} = \epsilon_0(E_{1n} - E_{2n})$. With $\sigma = 0$, $\mathbf{J}_s = 0$ too: $D_n$, $E_t$, $B_n$, $H_t$ all continuous.

> [!note] Magnetic boundary conditions (Lecture 17 §6; $\hat n$ from medium 2 into medium 1)
> $$
> \hat n\cdot(\mathbf{B}_1-\mathbf{B}_2) = 0\ \Longrightarrow\ \mu_1H_{1n} = \mu_2H_{2n},\qquad\qquad \hat n\times(\mathbf{H}_1-\mathbf{H}_2) = \mathbf{J}_s\ \ (\text{free}).
> $$
> Without free surface current $\mathbf{H}_t$ (so $\mathbf{B}_t/\mu$) is continuous and $B$- and $H$-lines **refract** alike:
> $$
> \frac{\tan\theta_1}{\tan\theta_2} = \frac{\mu_1}{\mu_2}\qquad(\text{angles from the normal}).
> $$
> Tangential $\mathbf{B}$ jumps by the bound current, $\hat n\times(\mathbf{B}_1-\mathbf{B}_2)/\mu_0 = \mathbf{J}_s + \hat n\times(\mathbf{M}_1-\mathbf{M}_2)$; normal $\mathbf{H}$ jumps too, $H_{1n}-H_{2n} = -(M_{1n}-M_{2n})$ — "$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{H} = 0$" fails here, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$ never.

**Surface of a perfect conductor** (medium 2, $\sigma\to\infty$, $\mathbf{E} = 0$ inside): Faraday gives $\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t = -\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = 0$ inside, so no *time-varying* $\mathbf{B}$ either — the class's reason for $\mathbf{H} = 0$ inside. Then $E_{1t} = 0$ and $B_{1n} = 0$: $\mathbf{E}$ meets the surface *normally* and ends on $\rho_s = \hat n\cdot\mathbf{D}_1$ — **its normal $\mathbf{D}$ *is* the surface charge** ([[1-electrostatics/07-poisson-and-laplace|Lecture 7]] reads plate charges this way); $\mathbf{H}$ runs *tangentially*, carried by $\mathbf{J}_s = \hat n\times\mathbf{H}_1$, i.e. $\mathbf{H}_{1t} = \mathbf{J}_s\times\hat n$. (A typed slide's "$D_{1n} = \rho$" means $\rho_s$.) More: [[concepts/conductors|Conductors in electrostatics]].

**Planar problems.** A sheet $\rho_s$ steps $D_n$ by $\rho_s$ along $\hat{n}$; a current sheet steps $H_t$ by $J_s$ (Lecture 13's $\pm\tfrac12\mathbf{J}_s\times\hat n$); everything else passes.

**Examples.**

> [!example] Notes Example 2 — a vacuum gap bounded by two charged planes
> Vacuum; $\mathbf{D} = 0$ measured for $x<0$. Planes $x=0$ and $x=5$ m carry $\rho_s = 2$ C/m² and an unknown $\rho_{s0}$; no other charges.
>
> *At $x=0$:* gap $0<x<5$ = medium 1, $x<0$ = medium 2, $\hat{n} = +\hat{x}$. $\hat{x}\cdot(\mathbf{D}_1 - 0) = 2$, so $D_{1x} = 2$; no tangential field on the left, $E_t$ continuous, $\mathbf{D} = \epsilon_0\mathbf{E}$ both sides: $\mathbf{D}_1 = 2\hat{x}$ C/m² in the gap.
>
> *At $x=5$:* now the gap is side "2" and $x>5$ medium 1 ($\hat{n} = +\hat{x}$), or keep the gap as medium 1 with $\hat{n} = -\hat{x}$; either way $D_x(x>5) - 2 = \rho_{s0}$. The jump alone cannot fix $\rho_{s0}$; superposition does: the sheets' fields must cancel for $x<0$, sheet 1 giving $-\tfrac{2}{2}\hat{x}$ there and sheet 2 $-\tfrac{\rho_{s0}}{2}\hat{x}$, so $\rho_{s0} = -2$ C/m² and $\mathbf{D} = 0$ for $x>5$ too.
>
> Result: $\mathbf{D} = 0,\ 2\hat{x},\ 0$ C/m² — a parallel-plate capacitor's field.

> [!example] Notes Example 3 — tangential and normal bookkeeping together
> $\mathbf{D} = 3\hat{y}$ C/m² for $x<0$; sheets $\rho_s = 2$ at $x=0$ and $-6$ C/m² at $x=5$ m; $\mathbf{D}$ uniform in each region (vacuum). *Tangential:* $\mathbf{E} = 3\hat{y}/\epsilon_0$ is parallel to both planes and never jumps, $\mathbf{D} = \epsilon_0\mathbf{E}$, so $D_y = 3$ everywhere (from distant charges). *Normal:* $D_x$ starts at 0, rises by $2$ at $x=0$, by $-6$ at $x=5$:
> $$
> \mathbf{D} = \begin{cases}3\hat{y}, & x<0\\ 2\hat{x}+3\hat{y}, & 0<x<5\text{ m}\\ -4\hat{x}+3\hat{y}, & x>5\text{ m}\end{cases}\quad[\text{C/m}^2].
> $$

> [!example] Slides, Lecture 6 challenge — a preview of dielectrics
> Medium 1 ($\epsilon_1 = \epsilon_0$, above) meets medium 2 ($\epsilon_2 = 2\epsilon_0$, below) on a plane with free charge $\rho_s = 3$ C/m². Below, $\mathbf{D}_2 = 3\hat{x} + 2\hat{y}$ C/m², $\hat{y}$ normal. Find $\mathbf{D}_1$ ($\mathbf{D} = \epsilon\mathbf{E}$ in each medium, [[concepts/permittivity|Permittivity, susceptibility, and D = εE]]).
> - Tangential: $E_{1t} = E_{2t}$ ⇒ $D_{1t}/\epsilon_0 = D_{2t}/(2\epsilon_0) = 3/(2\epsilon_0)$ ⇒ $D_{1t} = \tfrac32$. **Tangential $\mathbf{D}$ is not continuous** — it scales with $\epsilon$.
> - Normal ($\hat{n} = \hat{y}$ from 2 into 1): $D_{1n} - D_{2n} = \rho_s$ ⇒ $D_{1n} = 2 + 3 = 5$.
>
> $\mathbf{D}_1 = \tfrac32\hat{x} + 5\hat{y}$ C/m². The slide's wrong options are the classic errors: $3\hat{x}+2\hat{y}$ (everything continuous), $3\hat{x}+5\hat{y}$ (tangential $\mathbf{D}$ continuous), $\tfrac32\hat{x} - 1\hat{y}$ ($\hat{n}$ flipped).

> [!example] Slides, Lecture 16 — two exercises at a perfect conductor
> *Surface charge from $\mathbf{D}$.* (a) $\mathbf{D} = D_0\langle1,-2,2\rangle$ pointing *away* from the surface: $\hat n$ (out of the metal) is $\langle1,-2,2\rangle/3$, so $\rho_s = \hat n\cdot\mathbf{D} = D_0(1+4+4)/3 = 3D_0$. (b) $\mathbf{D} = D_0\langle1,0,\sqrt3\rangle$ pointing *toward* the surface: $\hat n = -\langle1,0,\sqrt3\rangle/2$, $\rho_s = -2D_0$ — negative charge, lines ending on it. Shortcut: pointing away, $\rho_s = \lvert\mathbf{D}\rvert$; in, $\rho_s = -\lvert\mathbf{D}\rvert$.
>
> *Which arrows are possible just outside a perfect conductor?* Oblique: neither $\mathbf{E}$ nor $\mathbf{H}$. Tangential: $\mathbf{H}$ only ($B_{1n} = 0$, $\hat n\times\mathbf{H}_1 = \mathbf{J}_s$). Normal: $\mathbf{E}$ only ($E_{1t} = 0$, $\hat n\cdot\mathbf{D}_1 = \rho_s$). Answer (e) in class.

> [!trap] Ways to go wrong
> - **Mixing up what is continuous.** $E_t$ and $D_n$, not $E_n$ and $D_t$; $B_n$ and $H_t$, not $H_n$ and $B_t$. Test: at a charged conductor normal $\mathbf{E}$ jumps (nonzero outside, zero inside).
> - **Losing $\hat{n}$.** Swapping labels *and* $\hat{n}$ changes nothing; swapping one flips the sign of $\rho_s$.
> - **Differential laws across a boundary.** At a jump the derivatives are δ-functions: pillbox or thin loop, shrink, read off the jump (or write the sheet as $\rho_s\,\delta$).
> - **Inverting the ratio.** $E_{2n} = (\epsilon_1/\epsilon_2)E_{1n}$: the higher-$\epsilon$ side has the smaller $E_n$; each side's own $\epsilon$ in $\mathbf{D} = \epsilon\mathbf{E}$, never $\epsilon_0$ for a dielectric.
> - **Units.** $\rho_s$, $\mathbf{D}$ in C/m²; $\mathbf{J}_s$ in A/m (current per unit *width*, not $I/S$), $\mathbf{H}$ in A/m. A stray $\epsilon_0$ in a jump condition means $\mathbf{E}$ written where $\mathbf{D}$ belongs.

Course-material slips (missing minus in the $\mathbf{P}$ condition; $D_1-D_2=\rho$): [[0-toolkit/04-errata-in-the-course-materials|errata]].

> [!exam] On exams
> FA26 problem 3 is Lecture 9 §2 verbatim: $\mathbf{E}_2$ from $\mathbf{E}_1$ ($E_t$ continuous, $E_{2n} = (\epsilon_1/\epsilon_2)E_{1n}$), then $\mathbf{D}_{1,2} = \epsilon_{1,2}\mathbf{E}_{1,2}$, then $\mathbf{P}_{1,2} = \mathbf{D} - \epsilon_0\mathbf{E}$ — [[problems/fields-across-a-dielectric-interface|Worked problem — Fields across a dielectric interface]]; commonest error, confusing $\mathbf{D}$ with $\mathbf{E}$. Two-plane examples: HW3 #6 ($\rho = \rho_s\,\delta(y)$, read off the jump). Magnetic versions: $B_{1n} = B_{2n}$, $H_{1t} = H_{2t}$, convert with $\mu$ region by region. Surface currents and perfect conductors: the same rubric with four conditions, $\hat n$ drawn first; every later reflection problem is condition-matching at an interface.
