> [!key] Poynting's theorem (point form)
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\tfrac12\epsilon\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{E} + \tfrac12\mu\,\mathbf{H}\cdot\mathbf{H}\Big)\\
> {} + \nabla\cdot(\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}) + \mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E} = 0 ,
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> or, with $w\equiv\tfrac12\epsilon E^2 + \tfrac12\mu H^2$ [J/m³] and $\mathbf{S}\equiv\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}$ [W/m²],
> $$
> \frac{\partial w}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot\mathbf{S} + \mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E} = 0\qquad[\text{W/m}^3].
> $$
> Valid at every point and instant in a linear, isotropic medium with $\epsilon$, $\mu$ constant in time (variable in space; unstated in the notes). $\mathbf{J}$ is the free current; bound sources sit inside $\epsilon$, $\mu$.
>
> **Integral form:**
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> -\frac{dW}{dt} = \oint_A\mathbf{S}\cdot d\mathbf{A} + \int_V\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}\,dV,\\[4pt]
> W = \int_V\Big(\tfrac12\epsilon E^2 + \tfrac12\mu H^2\Big)dV .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> Outward $d\mathbf{A}$ (not to clash with $\mathbf{S}$). Rate of decrease of stored energy in $V$ = outflow through $A$ + power handed to charges inside; a source inside supplies $-\int\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}\,dV$ to storage, outflow and resistive regions.

**Physics.** Field energy sits at $\tfrac12\epsilon E^2$ ([[concepts/electrostatic-energy|Electrostatic energy]]) and $\tfrac12\mu H^2$ ([[concepts/magnetic-energy|Magnetic energy]]) per m³; resistors turn it into heat; a radiating sheet, whose $\mathbf{E}$ opposes $\mathbf{J}_s$, feeds it into two outgoing waves. Like charge ([[concepts/continuity-equation|Continuity equation (charge conservation)]]), field energy obeys a continuity law, plus an exchange term (heat, chemistry).

> [!derivation]- From the curl equations to the theorem, the vector identity, and the same result in one dimension
> **Step 1:** dot Faraday's law with $\mathbf{H}$ and Ampère–Maxwell with $\mathbf{E}$ (every term W/m³):
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \mathbf{H}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{E}) = -\mathbf{H}\cdot\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t},\\[4pt]
> \mathbf{E}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{H}) = \mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{J} + \mathbf{E}\cdot\frac{\partial\mathbf{D}}{\partial t}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> **Step 2:** subtract the second from the first, using $\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B}) = \mathbf{B}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{A}) - \mathbf{A}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})$ ([[0-toolkit/02-vector-calculus-cheatsheet#identities-valid-for-any-smooth-fields|toolkit]]):
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &\underbrace{\mathbf{H}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{E}) - \mathbf{E}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{H})}_{\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H})}\\
> &\qquad = -\mathbf{E}\cdot\frac{\partial\mathbf{D}}{\partial t} - \mathbf{H}\cdot\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t} - \mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> The first two terms on the right are a time derivative if $\mathbf{D} = \epsilon\mathbf{E}$, $\mathbf{B} = \mu\mathbf{H}$ with $\epsilon$, $\mu$ constant in time, since $\partial(\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{E})/\partial t = 2\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\partial\mathbf{E}/\partial t$:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \mathbf{E}\cdot\frac{\partial\mathbf{D}}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\tfrac12\epsilon\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{E}\Big),\\[4pt]
> \mathbf{H}\cdot\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\tfrac12\mu\,\mathbf{H}\cdot\mathbf{H}\Big).
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> **The identity**, in components:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \nabla\cdot(\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}) &= \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(E_yH_z - E_zH_y)\\
> &\quad + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(E_zH_x - E_xH_z)\\
> &\quad + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(E_xH_y - E_yH_x).
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> The product rule splits each product; the six pieces with $\mathbf{E}$ differentiated give
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &H_x\Big(\frac{\partial E_z}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial E_y}{\partial z}\Big) + H_y\Big(\frac{\partial E_x}{\partial z} - \frac{\partial E_z}{\partial x}\Big)\\
> &\quad + H_z\Big(\frac{\partial E_y}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial E_x}{\partial y}\Big) = \mathbf{H}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{E}).
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> The other six, each bracket reversed, give $-\mathbf{E}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{H})$.
>
> **1-D check (Rao's route).** $\mathbf{E} = \hat x\,E_x(z,t)$, $\mathbf{H} = \hat y\,H_y(z,t)$ ([[concepts/plane-waves|Plane waves]]), current $J_x$: $\partial E_x/\partial z = -\mu\,\partial H_y/\partial t$, $-\partial H_y/\partial z = J_x + \epsilon\,\partial E_x/\partial t$. With $S_z = E_xH_y$,
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \frac{\partial S_z}{\partial z} &= H_y\frac{\partial E_x}{\partial z} + E_x\frac{\partial H_y}{\partial z}\\
> &= -\mu H_y\frac{\partial H_y}{\partial t} - E_x\Big(J_x + \epsilon\frac{\partial E_x}{\partial t}\Big)\\
> &= -\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\tfrac12\mu H_y^2 + \tfrac12\epsilon E_x^2\Big) - J_xE_x ,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> the theorem.
>
> **Integral form:** integrate over $V$; [[concepts/divergence-theorem|divergence theorem]] on $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{S}$.

**The three terms** (W/m³) sum to zero:

- **$\partial w/\partial t$: stored energy changing.** $w_e = \tfrac12\epsilon E^2$, $w_m = \tfrac12\mu H^2$ [J/m³], valid for any time-varying field.
- **$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{S}$: energy flowing away** from the point; $\mathbf{S}$ is the [[concepts/poynting-vector|Poynting vector]], power per unit perpendicular area.
- **$\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}$: energy handed to charges.** The force density $\rho(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B})$ works at $\rho\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}$; the magnetic part does none ([[concepts/lorentz-force|Lorentz force]]). The notes' **Joule heating**, non-zero only where there is current. Ohmic, $\mathbf{J} = \sigma\mathbf{E}$ ([[concepts/conductors|Conductors in electrostatics]]):
$$
\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E} = \sigma E^2 = \frac{J^2}{\sigma}\ge0\qquad[\text{W/m}^3],
$$
point-form $I^2R$, passed to the lattice in Drude collisions ([[concepts/conductivity-and-susceptibility-models|Lorentz–Drude models of σ and χe]]); kinetic in a vacuum beam, chemical in a charging battery.
- **Negative $\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}$ is a source:** something else drives current against $\mathbf{E}$, feeding the field $-\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}$ per m³: a battery's current runs from − to + against the terminal charges' field ([[concepts/electrostatic-potential|Electrostatic potential V]]); a radiating sheet has $\mathbf{E} = -\tfrac\eta2\mathbf{J}_s$ ([[concepts/current-sheet-radiation|Radiation from a current sheet]]). Notes' analogy: passive sign convention, $vi>0$ absorbs, $vi<0$ supplies.

**Poynting's theorem next to the continuity equation.**

| | charge ([[concepts/continuity-equation\|continuity]]) | electromagnetic energy |
|---|---|---|
| density | $\rho$ [C/m³] | $w = \tfrac12\epsilon E^2 + \tfrac12\mu H^2$ [J/m³] |
| flux density | $\mathbf{J}$ [A/m² = (C/s)/m²] | $\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}$ [W/m² = (J/s)/m²] |
| point form | $\partial\rho/\partial t + \nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$ | $\partial w/\partial t + \nabla\cdot\mathbf{S} = -\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}$ |
| exchange term | none: charge is never created or destroyed | $\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}$: to heat, motion or chemistry ($>0$), or from them ($<0$) |
| density moving at a speed | $\mathbf{J} = \rho\,\mathbf{u}$ | $\mathbf{S} = v\,w\,\hat u$ for one plane wave |

| situation | $\partial w/\partial t$ | $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{S}$ | $\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}$ | what happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| plane wave, lossless medium | oscillates | $-\partial w/\partial t$ | 0 | transport at the wave speed |
| leaky capacitor discharging | $-\sigma E^2$ | 0 | $\sigma E^2$ | stored energy → heat in place |
| wire, steady current | 0 | $-\sigma E^2$ | $\sigma E^2$ | energy flows in, becomes heat |
| battery; radiating sheet | 0 (on average) | positive | negative | driven charges feed the field, which flows away |

> [!recipe] Using the integral form
> 1. **Surface:** pillbox (sheet), cylinder (wire), sphere (antenna), box beyond a wavefront.
> 2. **Outflow:** $\mathbf{S}\cdot d\mathbf{A}$ per face, outward; $\mathbf{S} = 0$ or tangential gives nothing.
> 3. **Exchange:** $\int\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}\,dV$ ($\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E}$ × area for a sheet).
> 4. **Storage:** $dW/dt$; zero if steady, zero on average if periodic.
> 5. **Check:** the sum is zero; the skipped term follows.

**A wire is heated through its surface.** Radius $a$, length $\ell$, conductivity $\sigma$, steady $I$ along $+\hat z$: $\partial w/\partial t = 0$, so $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{S} = -\sigma E^2<0$: energy converges on the wire.

- **E** $= \hat z\,I/(\sigma\pi a^2)$ inside, continuous (tangential) just outside ([[concepts/boundary-conditions|Boundary conditions at an interface]]); surface charges set it up and add a radial outside part ([[1-electrostatics/11-lorentz-drude-models-for-conductivity-and-susceptibility#6-what-drives-the-current-in-a-wire-a-footnote-worth-a-section|Lecture 11 §6]]).
- **H** $= \hat\phi\,I\rho/(2\pi a^2)$ inside, $\hat\phi\,I/(2\pi\rho)$ outside.
- **S** at the surface: $\hat z\times\hat\phi = -\hat\rho$, so $\mathbf{S} = -\hat\rho\,E_zH_\phi(a)$, radially **inward**:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_zH_\phi(a)\cdot2\pi a\ell &= \frac{I}{\sigma\pi a^2}\cdot\frac{I}{2\pi a}\cdot2\pi a\ell\\
&= I^2\,\frac{\ell}{\sigma\pi a^2} = I^2R,
\end{aligned}
$$

$R = \ell/(\sigma\pi a^2)$ ([[concepts/conductance|Conductance and the leaky capacitor]]); the end faces get nothing ($S_z = 0$). Inside, $S_\rho = -E_z\,I\rho/(2\pi a^2)$ carries $I^2R\,(\rho/a)^2$ across radius $\rho$: the heat produced within it. $\mathbf{S}$ lines start in the battery ($\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}<0$), run alongside the wires (radial $\mathbf{E}$ with $\mathbf{H}$) and enter the resistor through its sides.

**Examples.**

> [!example] Example — a leaky capacitor empties without any energy flow
> $\epsilon_r = 4$, $\sigma = 10^{-5}$ S/m, charged to $E_0 = 10$ kV/m (10 V across 1 mm), disconnected: $E = E_0e^{-t/\tau}$, $\tau = \epsilon/\sigma = 3.54$ µs. Conduction $\sigma\mathbf{E}$ and displacement $\epsilon\,\partial\mathbf{E}/\partial t = -(\epsilon/\tau)\mathbf{E} = -\sigma\mathbf{E}$ cancel: $\mathbf{H} = 0$, $\mathbf{S} = 0$ (edges ignored), $\partial w/\partial t = -\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}$, heat in place. $t = 0$: $w = \tfrac12\epsilon E_0^2 = 1.77$ mJ/m³, $\sigma E_0^2 = 1000$ W/m³ $= 2w/\tau$ ($w\propto e^{-2t/\tau}$); energy halves every $(\tau/2)\ln2 = 1.23$ µs, charge every 2.45 µs.

> [!example] Example — DC power travels in a coax's dielectric, not its copper
> Perfect conductors, radii $a$, $b$; inner at $V$, carrying $I$ along $+\hat z$. Between them $\mathbf{E} = \hat\rho\,V/\big(\rho\ln(b/a)\big)$ ([[concepts/capacitance|Capacitance]]), $\mathbf{H} = \hat\phi\,I/(2\pi\rho)$, $\hat\rho\times\hat\phi = \hat z$:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \mathbf{S} = \hat z\,\frac{VI}{2\pi\rho^2\ln(b/a)},\\[4pt]
> \int_a^bS_z\,2\pi\rho\,d\rho = \frac{VI}{\ln(b/a)}\int_a^b\frac{d\rho}{\rho} = VI .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> All of $VI$ flows in the dielectric toward the load, mostly near the inner conductor ($S\propto1/\rho^2$); the copper ($\mathbf{E} = 0$, $\mathbf{S} = 0$) guides the energy, does not carry it (Unit 4).

> [!example] Example — a 100 W heater wire
> Nichrome, resistivity $1.10\ \mu\Omega\cdot$m ($\sigma\approx9.1\times10^5$ S/m), $a = 0.25$ mm, $\ell = 2$ m, $I = 3$ A: $R = 11.2\ \Omega$, $P = I^2R = 101$ W; $E = 16.8$ V/m ($E\ell = IR = 33.6$ V), $H(a) = I/(2\pi a) = 1.91$ kA/m; $\lvert\mathbf{S}\rvert = EH = 32.1$ kW/m² inward (24 suns); × side area $2\pi a\ell = 3.14\times10^{-3}$ m² $= 101$ W ✓.

**The books balance at every instant at a radiating sheet.** Notes' Example 1 ([[concepts/current-sheet-radiation|fields derived there]]): $\mathbf{J}_s = \hat x\,2\cos(\omega t)$ A/m on $z = 0$ in free space radiates $\mathbf{E}(z,t) = -\hat x\,\eta_0\cos(\omega t\mp\beta z)$ V/m, $\mathbf{H}(z,t) = \mp\hat y\,\cos(\omega t\mp\beta z)$ A/m ($z\gtrless0$), $\mathbf{S} = \pm\hat z\,\eta_0\cos^2(\omega t\mp\beta z)$ W/m². On the sheet $\mathbf{E}(0,t) = -\hat x\,\eta_0\cos\omega t$ (continuous):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E} &= \big(\hat x\,2\cos\omega t\big)\cdot\big(-\hat x\,\eta_0\cos\omega t\big)\\
&= -2\eta_0\cos^2(\omega t)\ \ \text{W/m}^2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

Power per unit *area* ([A/m]×[V/m]: $\int\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}\,dz$ with $\mathbf{J} = \mathbf{J}_s\,\delta(z)$), negative except at the zeros of $\cos\omega t$: a source (the notes' $\pm\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E}$: power absorbed or injected per unit area). Pillbox of face area $A$ hugging the sheet: outflow $\mathbf{S}(0^+)\cdot\hat z\,A + \mathbf{S}(0^-)\cdot(-\hat z)\,A = 2\eta_0\cos^2\omega t\,A$, no storage at zero height, $\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E}\,A = -2\eta_0\cos^2\omega t\,A$: sum zero.

**Poynting's theorem across a current sheet.**

$$
\hat n\cdot(\mathbf{S}_1 - \mathbf{S}_2) = -\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E},
$$
$\hat n$ from side 2 into side 1: the jump in normal Poynting flux is the power per area the sheet injects. The [[concepts/boundary-conditions|boundary-condition]] pattern ($\nabla\to\hat n$, field → jump, $\mathbf{J}\to\mathbf{J}_s$, no time derivative: a thin pillbox stores nothing). Here $\hat n = \hat z$: $\eta_0\cos^2\omega t - (-\eta_0\cos^2\omega t) = 2\eta_0\cos^2\omega t$ ✓.

**Away from the sheet the books balance only on average.** Slab $\lvert z\rvert<d$: energy enters at $2\eta_0\cos^2\omega t$ per m², leaves at $2\eta_0\cos^2(\omega t - \beta d)$ (delay $d/c$); the difference is stored:

$$
\frac{dW}{dt} = 2\eta_0\big[\cos^2\omega t - \cos^2(\omega t - \beta d)\big],
$$

per m² of sheet; for $\beta d = \pi/2$, $2\eta_0\cos2\omega t$: the slab fills and drains twice per cycle, averaging zero. Hence the notes turn to [[concepts/time-average-power|time averages]]: in periodic steady state $\langle\partial w/\partial t\rangle = 0$, and the sheet's books read $\eta_0/2 + \eta_0/2 = \eta_0\approx377$ W/m² for every $d$.

> [!trap]
> - Outward flux plus $\int\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}\,dV$ = rate of *decrease* of $W$.
> - Exams call even a negative $\langle\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle$ (a source) Joule heating; $\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E}$ is W/m², $\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}$ W/m³.
> - Only $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{S}$, hence $\oint\mathbf{S}\cdot d\mathbf{A}$, is fixed: a charge beside a bar magnet has $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}\neq0$, but $\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = 0$ and $\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J} = 0$ give $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{S} = 0$: closed loops carrying nothing.
> - Hysteresis ([[concepts/magnetic-materials|Dia-, para- and ferromagnetism]]): $\mathbf{H}\cdot\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t$ is not a stored-energy rate; each cycle loses the loop area as heat.
