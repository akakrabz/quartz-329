> [!key] The line parameters, and what they multiply to
> | | parallel plates | coax |
> |---|---|---|
> | $\mathcal{C}$ | $\epsilon W/d$ | $2\pi\epsilon/\ln(b/a)$ |
> | $\mathcal{G}$ | $\sigma W/d$ | $2\pi\sigma/\ln(b/a)$ |
> | $\mathcal{L}$ | $\mu d/W$ | $(\mu/2\pi)\ln(b/a)$ |
>
> $$
> \mathcal{L}\mathcal{C} = \mu\epsilon,\qquad \frac{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{C}} = \frac\sigma\epsilon,\qquad \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}\mathcal{C}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}}\ (= c\ \text{in vacuum}).
> $$
> Both relations hold for *any* two-conductor line filled with one homogeneous medium: all three parameters share one geometric factor. Plates: width $W$, separation $d$; coax: inner radius $a$, outer inner radius $b$. Units F/m, S/m, H/m.

**Physics.** $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{G}$ come from Unit 1 ([[concepts/capacitance|Capacitance]], [[1-electrostatics/10-capacitance-and-conductance#3-the-coaxial-cable|Lecture 10 §3]]; [[concepts/conductance|Conductance and the leaky capacitor]], [[1-electrostatics/10-capacitance-and-conductance#6-conductance-the-leaky-capacitor|§6]]); $\mathcal{L}$ comes from the Ampère chain of [[concepts/inductance|Inductance]] applied to a line *shorted at the far end*, so the current goes down one conductor and back along the other and the flux is trapped between them. In both geometries the geometric factor of $\mathcal{L}$ is the **arithmetic inverse** of that of $\mathcal{C}$: $\mathcal{L}\propto\mu$, $\mathcal{C}\propto\epsilon$ (the notes swap them: [[0-toolkit/04-errata-in-the-course-materials|errata]]). This is the *external* inductance (flux inside the inner conductor ignored) — the right idealization at the frequencies where cables matter. The slides' $v_p = 1/\sqrt{\mathcal{LC}}$ (beside $c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}$) is the signal speed on the line: a voltage step launched on a coax travels at the speed of light in its dielectric, whatever the radii. Unit 4 builds the [[concepts/telegraphers-equations|Telegrapher's equations]] from $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{C}$ and the [[concepts/characteristic-impedance|Characteristic impedance]] $Z_0 = \sqrt{\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{C}}$ from their ratio ([[4-transmission-lines/27-guided-tem-waves-on-transmission-lines|Lecture 27]]).

**Shorted coax** (course notes, Lecture 15). Inner radius $a$, outer inner radius $b$, length $\ell\gg b$, shorted at the far end: $I$ goes down the inner conductor and back along the inside of the outer one. Ampère for $a<r<b$: $B_\phi = \dfrac{\mu I}{2\pi r}$; for $r>b$ the currents cancel, $\mathbf{B} = 0$ — flux confined to the cable. The linked flux threads the rectangle $a<r<b$, $0<z<\ell$ (normal $\hat\phi$, along $\mathbf{B}$):

$$
\Psi = \int_S\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \ell\int_a^b\frac{\mu I}{2\pi r}dr = \frac{\mu\ell I}{2\pi}\ln\frac ba,
$$

so $L = \Psi/I$ and, per unit length,

$$
\begin{gathered}
L = \frac{\mu\ell}{2\pi}\ln\frac ba,\qquad \mathcal{L}\equiv\frac L\ell = \frac{\mu}{2\pi}\ln\frac ba\ [\text{H/m}]\\[4pt]
\text{vs.}\qquad \mathcal{C} = \frac{2\pi\epsilon}{\ln(b/a)}\ [\text{F/m}]\ (\text{Lecture 10}).
\end{gathered}
$$

**Shorted parallel plates**, width $W$, separation $d$: surface current $I/W$ on each plate makes $H = I/W$ between them (the sheet rule, twice — [[concepts/current-sheets-and-slabs|Current sheets and slabs]]), so $\Psi = \mu\dfrac IW d\ell$ and $\mathcal{L} = \mu\dfrac dW$, beside $\mathcal{C} = \epsilon\dfrac Wd$.

**Examples.** *Slide challenge.* Increase the plate separation $d$: $\mathcal{L}$ rises while $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{G}$ fall — the same current links more flux, the same charge gives a bigger voltage drop. *Site's own.* An air coax with $b/a = e\approx2.72$: $\mathcal{L} = \mu_0/2\pi = 0.2\ \mu$H/m, $\mathcal{C} = 2\pi\epsilon_0 = 55.6$ pF/m, $\mathcal{LC} = 1.11\times10^{-17}\ \text{s}^2/\text{m}^2 = \mu_0\epsilon_0$ ✓, speed $3\times10^8$ m/s, $Z_0 = \sqrt{\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{C}} = 60\ \Omega$; with polyethylene ($\epsilon_r = 2.25$) the speed drops to $2\times10^8$ m/s and $Z_0$ to 40 Ω. [[problems/coax-inductance-and-the-lc-product|Worked problem — Inductance of a coax, and the 𝓛𝓒 product]] runs the table for a coax with numbers and the 50 Ω look-ahead. The energy route checks $\mathcal{L}$: $\tfrac12\mathcal{L}I^2 = \int\tfrac12\mu H^2\,dS$ per metre ([[concepts/magnetic-energy|Magnetic energy]]).

> [!trap]
> - $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{C}$ have *inverse* geometric factors; if your coax numbers fail $\mathcal{LC} = \mu\epsilon$, the chain has an error.
> - The coax flux surface is the $r$–$z$ rectangle between the conductors (normal $\hat\phi$), not a circle.
> - $\mathcal{LC} = \mu\epsilon$ needs one homogeneous medium between the conductors; a layered filling breaks it (then $\mathcal{C}$ is a series/parallel combination and $\mathcal{L}$ is not).
> - Script letters mean per metre: $\mathcal{L}$ [H/m], not $L$ [H].

> [!exam] On exams
> "Find $\mathcal{L}$, $\mathcal{C}$, $\mathcal{G}$ of this line and check $\mathcal{LC} = \mu\epsilon$": run the Ampère chain for $\mathcal{L}$ (loop, $\mathbf{B} = \mu\mathbf{H}$, the rectangle, divide by $\ell$) and quote $\mathcal{C}$, $\mathcal{G}$ from Unit 1 with the same geometric factor; state units. Qualitative variants ask which parameter rises when a dimension changes — reason from the geometric factor.
