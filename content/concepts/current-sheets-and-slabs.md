> [!key] Fields of an infinite current sheet and of a current slab
> $$
> \mathbf{H} = \hat y\,\frac{J_s}{2}\,\text{sgn}(x),\qquad\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{H},\qquad\text{in general}\qquad \mathbf{H} = \tfrac12\,\mathbf{J}_s\times\hat n,
> $$
> for uniform $\mathbf{J}_s = J_s\hat z$ on the plane $x = 0$; $\hat n$ the unit normal from the sheet *toward the field point*. Check: for $x>0$, $\hat n = \hat x$ and $\tfrac12J_s\hat z\times\hat x = \tfrac12J_s\hat y$ ✓. The magnitude is independent of distance (an infinite sheet has no scale to fall off with), and **tangential $\mathbf{H}$ jumps by $J_s$** across the sheet: $\hat n\times(\mathbf{H}_1-\mathbf{H}_2) = \mathbf{J}_s$, the [[concepts/boundary-conditions|boundary condition]] of [[3-maxwell-and-waves/16-charge-conservation-displacement-current-and-maxwells-equations|Lecture 16]] — twin of $D_n$ jumping by $\rho_s$.
>
> Slab:
> $$
> \mathbf{H} = \begin{cases}\hat y\,J_0x, & |x|<W/2,\\[4pt] \hat y\,J_0\dfrac W2\,\text{sgn}(x), & |x|\ge W/2.\end{cases}
> $$
> For $\mathbf{J} = \hat zJ_0$ in $\lvert x\rvert<W/2$, zero outside. A ramp of slope $J_0$ inside — $(\nabla\times\mathbf{H})_z = dH_y/dx = J_0$ ✓ — saturating at $\pm J_0W/2$ outside, **continuous** at the faces (no *surface* current there). From outside, the slab looks like a sheet with $J_s = J_0W$.

**Physics.** As [[1-electrostatics/03-gauss-law-at-work|Lecture 3]] did with Gauss's law and symmetric charges ([[concepts/symmetric-charge-distributions|Symmetric charge distributions]]), [[concepts/amperes-law|Ampère's law]] on symmetric *currents* gives every answer as the Unit 1 answer with "normal" replaced by "tangential". The charged slab ($D_x = \rho_0x$ inside, $\pm\rho_0W/2$ outside) has the identical plot with $D_x\to H_y$, $\rho_0\to J_0$; the charged sheet's $\tfrac12\rho_s\hat n$ becomes $\tfrac12\mathbf{J}_s\times\hat n$. The whole electrostatic ↔ magnetostatic translation is the [[2-magnetostatics/index#the-dictionary-electrostatics--magnetostatics|dictionary]]. Rolled into a cylinder, the sheet becomes the solenoid ([[concepts/solenoids-and-toroids|Solenoids and toroids]]); set oscillating, it radiates plane waves whose $\mathbf{H} = \tfrac12\mathbf{J}_s\times\hat n$ is this static result delayed ([[concepts/current-sheet-radiation|Radiation from a current sheet]]).

> [!derivation]- The sheet by Ampère's law (course notes, Lecture 13, Example 1)
> Uniform $\mathbf{J}_s = J_s\hat z$ on the plane $x = 0$, infinite in $y$ and $z$. Symmetry fixes the *direction* first: the sheet is a stack of filaments along $z$; pair a filament at $+y'$ with its mirror at $-y'$, and at a point on the $x$ axis their $\hat x$ parts cancel while the $\hat y$ parts add. So $\mathbf{B} = \hat yB_y(x)$, odd in $x$, independent of $y$ and $z$. Now the rectangle in the figure: long sides of length $L$ parallel to $\hat y$ at $x = \mp W/2$, short sides crossing the sheet. On the short sides $\mathbf{B}\perp d\mathbf{l}$; on each long side the field is parallel to the direction of travel (both reverse with $x$), so each contributes $+B_yL$:
> $$
> \oint_C\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 2B_yL = \mu_0I_C = \mu_0J_sL\quad\Rightarrow\quad B_y = \frac{\mu_0J_s}{2}\ (x>0).
> $$
> The arbitrary $L$ cancels, as it must.

> [!derivation]- The slab (course notes, Example 2)
> $\mathbf{J} = \hat zJ_0$ for $\lvert x\rvert<W/2$, zero outside. Same symmetry, same rectangle, now of half-width $x$ centred on the slab: enclosed current $J_0\cdot2x\cdot L$ for $\lvert x\rvert<W/2$, $J_0WL$ beyond; $2H_yL = I_{\text{enc}}$ gives the ramp and the plateaus of the key box.

**Examples.** The slides put the sheet on $y = 0$ with current along $z$ and get $\mathbf{H} = -\tfrac12J_{s0}\hat x\,\text{sgn}(y)$ — same formula $\tfrac12\mathbf{J}_s\times\hat n$, different axes. [[problems/current-slab-and-sheet-by-amperes-law|Worked problem — A current slab and a current sheet, by Ampère's law]] superposes a slab and a sheet, as the Exam 1 slab problem did with charge: the fields add region by region, and a sheet can cancel a slab's field on one side. Two parallel sheets with opposite $\mathbf{J}_s$ (the shorted parallel-plate line of [[concepts/transmission-line-parameters|Transmission-line parameters 𝓛, 𝓒, 𝓖]]) give $H = J_s$ between them and zero outside — the sheet rule twice.

> [!trap]
> - The $\text{sgn}$ is the most-reversed sign in this unit; evaluate $\mathbf{J}_s\times\hat n$ on one side explicitly rather than remembering a picture.
> - $\hat n$ points from the sheet *toward the field point*, so it flips across the sheet; that flip is the whole $x$-dependence.
> - Slab faces carry no surface current, so $H_y$ is continuous there; only a true sheet makes $H_t$ jump.
> - A current *along* $\hat y$ on the plane $x = 0$ makes $\mathbf{H}$ along $\mp\hat z$, not $\hat y$: the field is tangential to the sheet and perpendicular to the current.

> [!exam] On exams
> Sheet and slab are two of the three "find $\mathbf{H}$ by Ampère's law" set pieces; a superposition (slab plus sheet, two sheets) is the natural exam problem. Know $\tfrac12\mathbf{J}_s\times\hat n$ cold, direction from the cross product, and that $H_t$ jumps by $J_s$ while $B_n$ is continuous. Sketch $H_y(x)$ — the ramp-and-plateau plot — and label the plateau values.
