> [!key] Definition
> For $A\cos(\omega t\mp\beta z)$, the **dispersion relation** links $\omega$ [rad/s] and the wave-number $\beta$ [rad/m] as the governing equations demand. It fixes
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \lambda = \frac{2\pi}{\beta},\qquad T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \frac1f,\\[4pt]
> v_p = \frac{\omega}{\beta} = \frac\lambda T = \lambda f,\qquad v_g = \frac{d\omega}{d\beta}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> The notes' three examples:
> | wave | dispersion relation | phase velocity $\omega/\beta$ |
> |---|---|---|
> | TEM wave in a perfect dielectric | $\beta = \omega\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}$ | $1/\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}$ at every $\omega$ |
> | sound in a monatomic gas | $\beta = \omega\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{\frac53k_BT}}$ | $\sqrt{5k_BT/3m}$ at every $\omega$ |
> | TEM wave in a collisionless plasma | $\beta = \dfrac1c\sqrt{\omega^2 - \omega_p^2}$ | $\dfrac{c}{\sqrt{1 - \omega_p^2/\omega^2}}$, faster than $c$ |
>
> $T$: temperature; $m$: atomic mass (row 2), electron mass (row 3); $k_B = 1.381\times10^{-23}$ J/K. **Plasma frequency** $\omega_p = \sqrt{Ne^2/(m\epsilon_0)}$, $N$ electrons/m³ of charge $-e$; $f_p\approx8.98\sqrt{N}$ Hz. Rows 1–2 **non-dispersive** ($v_p$ independent of $\omega$: pulses keep their shape, [[concepts/wave-equation|Wave equation]]); row 3 **dispersive**.

**Physics.** Any 1D wave has solutions $A\cos(\omega t\mp\beta z)$ ([[concepts/monochromatic-waves|Monochromatic waves: ω, β, λ, v_p]]); its physics is $\beta(\omega)$. Light in a dielectric: a straight line through the origin, one speed for all. Sound in an ideal gas: also straight; $\tfrac53 = \gamma$ is the adiabatic index (compressions too fast for heat to flow), monatomic only. Plasma: collisionless free electrons carry a current a quarter period behind $\mathbf{E}$, lowering the permittivity to $\epsilon_0(1 - \omega_p^2/\omega^2)$ and bending the line into $\omega^2 = \omega_p^2 + c^2\beta^2$. On an $\omega$–$\beta$ diagram $v_p$ is the chord slope, $v_g$ the tangent slope; in the plasma $v_p>c$, $v_g = c\sqrt{1 - \omega_p^2/\omega^2}<c$, $v_pv_g = c^2$. $v_p>c$ breaks nothing: a pure cosine carries no message; signals and energy go at the [[concepts/group-velocity|group velocity]] (non-dispersive: $v_g = v_p$; no paper on hand asks for $v_g$). Below $\omega_p$, $\beta^2<0$: no travelling wave; the plasma turns the wave back (shortwave hops around the Earth).

A TEM wave in vacuum or in a dielectric is a straight line through the origin, so ω/β is the same at every frequency: no dispersion, and pulses keep their shape. The collisionless plasma is the hyperbola ω² = ω<sub>p</sub>² + c²β². Below ω<sub>p</sub> (shaded) it has no real β, so no wave travels; above, it bends toward the light line. At any point the phase velocity ω/β is the slope of the chord from the origin, and for the plasma that chord is always steeper than the light line (v<sub>p</sub> &gt; c). The group velocity dω/dβ, the speed of a signal's envelope (Lecture 24), is the slope of the tangent, always less steep (v<sub>g</sub> &lt; c). At P, ω = 1.5ω<sub>p</sub>: v<sub>p</sub> = 1.34c and v<sub>g</sub> = 0.75c, with v<sub>p</sub>v<sub>g</sub> = c².</figcaption></figure>

> [!derivation]- Where the plasma relation comes from (this site's derivation, using Lecture 11)
> [[concepts/conductivity-and-susceptibility-models|Lecture 11]]'s collisionless Drude equation $m\,dv_x/dt = -eE_x$, $J_x = -Nev_x$:
> $$
> \frac{\partial J_x}{\partial t} = \frac{Ne^2}{m}E_x .
> $$
> Into $\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\mu_0\,\partial_t\big(\mathbf{J} + \epsilon_0\,\partial_t\mathbf{E}\big)$; a transverse wave has $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E} = 0$, so the left side is $-\nabla^2\mathbf{E}$:
> $$
> \frac{\partial^2E_x}{\partial z^2} = \mu_0\frac{Ne^2}{m}E_x + \mu_0\epsilon_0\frac{\partial^2E_x}{\partial t^2}.
> $$
> $E_x = E_0\cos(\omega t - \beta z)$:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> -\beta^2 = \mu_0\frac{Ne^2}{m} - \frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\ \Longrightarrow\ \beta^2 = \frac{\omega^2 - \omega_p^2}{c^2},\\[4pt]
> \omega_p^2 = \frac{Ne^2}{m\epsilon_0}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> Equivalently, $\sigma(\omega) = Ne^2/\big(m(\nu + j\omega)\big)$ at $\nu = 0$, $-jNe^2/(m\omega)$: imaginary, hence lossless ([[concepts/time-average-power|Time averages and average power]]); in phasor form $\sigma + j\omega\epsilon_0 = j\omega\epsilon_0(1 - \omega_p^2/\omega^2)$ gives the same line ([[concepts/propagation-constant|Propagation constant]]).

**Examples.**
- **Gas.** This site's $v_p$ checks: helium 1019 m/s, argon 323 m/s at 300 K; 1007 and 319 m/s at 20 °C (tabulated). Diatomic air, $\gamma = \tfrac75$: 343 m/s at 20 °C (375 with $\tfrac53$). 440 Hz: $\lambda = 2.32$ m (He), 0.73 m (Ar); $v_p\propto1/\sqrt m$, ratio $\sqrt{39.95/4.003} = 3.16$.
- **Plasma** (ionosphere, 50–1000 km): daytime $N\sim10^{12}$ m⁻³ gives $f_p\approx9$ MHz.
  - $\omega>\omega_p$: 10 MHz, $f_p = 8.98$ MHz: $\beta = 0.0922$ rad/m, $\lambda = 68.1$ m (30.0 m in vacuum), $v_p = 6.81\times10^8$ m/s $= 2.27c$; $v_g = 0.440c$.
  - $\omega\gg\omega_p$: 100 MHz: $v_p = 1.004c$; FM and satellite links pass.
  - $\omega<\omega_p$: no travelling wave; waves are turned back.
- 200 MHz, non-magnetic, $\epsilon_r = 4$: $\beta = 2\pi f\sqrt{\epsilon_r}/c = 8\pi/3\approx8.38$ rad/m, $\lambda = 0.75$ m, $v_p = 1.5\times10^8$ m/s; from $\cos(4\pi\times10^8t - \tfrac{8\pi}{3}y)$ alone, $\mu_r\epsilon_r = (c/v_p)^2 = 4$.

> [!trap] Speeds and symbols
> - $\omega/\beta$ is the **phase** velocity, not a universal "propagation velocity": in a plasma energy travels at $v_g<c<v_p$. The notes' "propagation velocity" is the signal speed only without dispersion.
> - Below $\omega_p$: no travelling wave, not "a slower wave".
> - $\tfrac53$: monatomic gases only; air needs $\tfrac75$. Acoustic $T$ is a temperature, not the period.
> - A measured $v_p$ gives $\mu_r\epsilon_r$; "non-magnetic" (or $\eta$) splits it.

> [!exam] On exams
> Read $\omega$ and $\beta$ off an expression, then $\lambda$, $v_p$, $\epsilon_r$ ([[concepts/monochromatic-waves|Monochromatic waves: ω, β, λ, v_p]]); plasma and sound appear as concept checks (no $v_g$ asked on the papers on hand). In lossy media $\beta$ is no longer $\propto\omega$ ([[concepts/propagation-constant|Propagation constant]]).
