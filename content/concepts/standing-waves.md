> [!key] Standing-wave phasors (perfect mirror at z = 0, wave incident from z < 0)
> Region 1 ($z<0$) is a perfect dielectric with $\eta_1$ and $\beta_1$; a perfect conductor fills $z>0$, so $\Gamma = -1$ and $\tau = 0$ ([[concepts/reflection-and-transmission|Reflection and transmission coefficients]]).
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \tilde{\mathbf{E}}_i = \hat x\,E_0e^{-j\beta_1z},\quad \tilde{\mathbf{E}}_r = -\hat x\,E_0e^{j\beta_1z},\\[6pt]
> \tilde{\mathbf{H}}_i = \hat y\,\frac{E_0}{\eta_1}e^{-j\beta_1z},\quad \tilde{\mathbf{H}}_r = \hat y\,\frac{E_0}{\eta_1}e^{j\beta_1z},\\[8pt]
> \tilde{\mathbf{E}} = -j\hat x\,2E_0\sin\beta_1z,\\[6pt]
> \tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \hat y\,\frac{2E_0}{\eta_1}\cos\beta_1z .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> With $E_0$ real, multiplying by $e^{j\omega t}$ and taking the real part gives
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \mathbf{E}(z,t) = \hat x\,2E_0\sin(\beta_1z)\sin(\omega t),\\[4pt]
> \mathbf{H}(z,t) = \hat y\,\frac{2E_0}{\eta_1}\cos(\beta_1z)\cos(\omega t).
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> The sums use $e^{-j\theta}-e^{j\theta} = -2j\sin\theta$ and $e^{-j\theta}+e^{j\theta} = 2\cos\theta$.

> [!key] Where the fields vanish (z ≤ 0, m = 0, 1, 2, …)
> - $\mathbf{E}$ nodes at $z = -m\lambda/2$, the mirror included. $\mathbf{E}$ antinodes (amplitude $2E_0$) at $z = -(2m+1)\lambda/4$.
> - $\mathbf{H}$ nodes at $z = -(2m+1)\lambda/4$. $\mathbf{H}$ antinodes (amplitude $2E_0/\eta_1$) at $z = -m\lambda/2$, the mirror included.
> - Adjacent nodes of one field are $\lambda/2$ apart. An $\mathbf{E}$ node and the nearest $\mathbf{H}$ node are $\lambda/4$ apart.
> - In time, $\mathbf{E}\propto\sin\omega t$ and $\mathbf{H}\propto\cos\omega t$: a quarter period apart (the $-j$ in $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$).

**Physics.** A d'Alembert wave $f(t\mp z/v)$ ([[concepts/plane-waves|Plane waves]]) moves its shape. Here each field is a function of $z$ times a function of $t$: it oscillates in place, with an amplitude set by position. Read backward, $2\sin\beta z\sin\omega t = \cos(\omega t-\beta z) - \cos(\omega t+\beta z)$: two equal waves travelling in opposite directions. The notes' circuit picture: an $\mathbf{E}$ node acts as a **short** ($v = 0$) and an $\mathbf{H}$ node as an **open** ($i = 0$); the mirror is a short, holding $E_x = 0$ while a current flows on it. [[4-transmission-lines/index|Unit 4]] uses the same language for lines. Notation: the notes write $E_o$; the site writes $E_0$.

**No net power; the energy sloshes.** The notes' check, from the phasors:
$$
\begin{gathered}
\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^*\}\\[4pt]
= \hat z\,\frac{2E_0^2}{\eta_1}\sin\beta_1z\cos\beta_1z\,\text{Re}\{-j\} = 0 .
\end{gathered}
$$
The two travelling waves carry equal power in opposite directions. The instantaneous flux is not zero ([[concepts/time-average-power|Time averages and average power]] works the same pair):

> [!key] Instantaneous flux and energy density
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \mathbf{S} = \hat z\,\frac{E_0^2}{\eta_1}\sin(2\beta_1z)\sin(2\omega t),\\[6pt]
> w = 2\epsilon_1E_0^2\big[\sin^2\beta_1z\,\sin^2\omega t\\
> +\cos^2\beta_1z\,\cos^2\omega t\big],\\[6pt]
> \langle w\rangle = \epsilon_1E_0^2\ \ \text{at every } z .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> - At $\omega t = 0$ the energy is all magnetic, at the $\mathbf{H}$ antinodes. At $\omega t = \pi/2$ it is all electric, at the $\mathbf{E}$ antinodes. In between, $\mathbf{S}$ carries it across.
> - $\mathbf{S} = 0$ at every multiple of $\lambda/4$ at all times. Each $\lambda/4$ cell keeps its energy, $\epsilon_1E_0^2\lambda/4$ per m² of cross-section.
> - $w$ vanishes only at an $\mathbf{E}$ node when $\cos\omega t = 0$, and at an $\mathbf{H}$ node when $\sin\omega t = 0$.

**Partial reflection (this site's addition).** For $\lvert\Gamma\rvert<1$, region 1 holds $\tilde E_x = E_0e^{-j\beta_1z}\big(1+\Gamma e^{j2\beta_1z}\big)$. Write $\Gamma = \lvert\Gamma\rvert e^{j\theta}$:
$$
\begin{gathered}
\tilde E_x = (1-\lvert\Gamma\rvert)E_0e^{-j\beta_1z}\\[4pt]
+\ 2\lvert\Gamma\rvert E_0\,e^{j\theta/2}\cos\!\big(\beta_1z+\tfrac\theta2\big).
\end{gathered}
$$
A travelling wave plus a standing wave. $\lvert\tilde E_x\rvert$ ripples between $(1+\lvert\Gamma\rvert)E_0$ and $(1-\lvert\Gamma\rvert)E_0$ with period $\lambda/2$, with no true nulls, and $\langle S\rangle = (1-\lvert\Gamma\rvert^2)E_0^2/2\eta_1$ ([[concepts/reflection-and-transmission|power balance]]). The notes treat only $\Gamma = -1$; Lecture 36 returns to such patterns on lines.

> [!key] Surface current on a perfect mirror
> $\mathbf{E}(0,t) = 0$, as the tangential-$\mathbf{E}$ condition requires: all fields vanish inside a perfect conductor. Tangential $\mathbf{H}$ does not vanish: $\mathbf{H}(0,t) = \hat y\,(2E_0/\eta_1)\cos\omega t$. With $\hat n = -\hat z$ from the conductor into region 1 ([[concepts/boundary-conditions|Boundary conditions at an interface]]):
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \mathbf{J}_s = \hat n\times\mathbf{H}_1 = -\hat z\times\mathbf{H}(0,t)\\[4pt]
> = \hat x\,\frac{2E_0}{\eta_1}\cos\omega t\ \ \text{A/m}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> It flows along the incident $\mathbf{E}$. Its phasor $\hat x\,2E_0/\eta_1$ is the perfect-conductor case of [[concepts/reflection-and-transmission|Reflection and transmission coefficients]].

The notes' reading: $\mathbf{J}_s$ idealizes volume currents in a layer a few skin depths thick; real mirrors are good, not perfect, conductors. Radiation from $\mathbf{J}_s$ produces the reflected wave and cancels the incident wave in $z>0$. Check with the [[concepts/current-sheet-radiation|sheet]] in a uniform medium, $\mathbf{E}^\pm = -\tfrac{\eta_1}{2}\mathbf{J}_s(t\mp z/v)$ (this site's check): it radiates $-\hat x\,E_0\cos(\omega t-\beta_1z)$ into $z>0$, minus the incident wave, and $-\hat x\,E_0\cos(\omega t+\beta_1z)$ into $z<0$, the reflected wave.

**A good conductor: where the surface current comes from.** The notes return to the transmitted fields of [[concepts/reflection-and-transmission|Reflection and transmission coefficients]], with region 1 vacuum and $\mu_2 = \mu_0$:
$$
\begin{gathered}
\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_t = \hat x\,\tau E_0e^{-\gamma_2z},\qquad \tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t = \hat y\,\frac{\tau E_0}{\eta_2}e^{-\gamma_2z},\\[4pt]
\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_t = \sigma_2\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_t,\qquad \tilde{\mathbf{B}}_t = \mu_2\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_t .
\end{gathered}
$$
In a good conductor $\eta_2\approx\sqrt{j\omega\mu_2/\sigma_2}$ and $\gamma_2\approx\sqrt{j\omega\mu_2\sigma_2} = (1+j)/\delta$ ([[concepts/skin-depth|Skin depth]]), with $\lvert\eta_2\rvert\ll\eta_0$.

> [!key] Effective surface current (notes' box)
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \tau = \frac{2\eta_2}{\eta_0+\eta_2}\approx\frac{2\eta_2}{\eta_0},\\[6pt]
> \sigma_2\tau\approx\frac{2\sigma_2\eta_2}{\eta_0} = \frac{2\gamma_2}{\eta_0},\\[8pt]
> \int_0^\infty\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_t\,dz = \hat x\,\frac{\sigma_2\tau E_0}{\gamma_2} \approx \hat x\,\frac{2E_0}{\eta_0}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> The depth integral of the volume current is the perfect mirror's $\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_s$, whatever $\sigma_2$ (to order $\lvert\eta_2\rvert/\eta_0$). The key step is $\sigma\eta = \sqrt{j\omega\mu\sigma} = \gamma$. A larger $\sigma_2$ squeezes the same total current into a thinner layer; as $\sigma_2\to\infty$, $\delta\to0$ and the layer becomes the sheet above.

> [!key] Surface resistance (notes' box)
> Take $\tilde{\mathbf{J}}(z) = \tilde{\mathbf{J}}_s\gamma e^{-\gamma z}$ with $\gamma = \alpha(1+j)$, so $\int_0^\infty\tilde{\mathbf{J}}\,dz = \tilde{\mathbf{J}}_s$. The Joule heat per unit volume is $\langle\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle = \tfrac12\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_s\gamma\rvert^2e^{-2\alpha z}/\sigma$. Per unit area:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \int_0^\infty\langle\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle\,dz = \tfrac12R_s\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_s\rvert^2,\\[6pt]
> R_s\equiv\frac\alpha\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\pi f\mu}{\sigma}} = \frac{1}{\sigma\delta}\ \ [\Omega].
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> $R_s$, the **surface resistance**, equals $\text{Re}\{\eta_2\}$. It models wall losses in waveguides and cavities (ECE 450) and lossy transmission lines (Lecture 39).

**Examples.**
- 1.5 GHz in air ($\lambda = 20$ cm): $\mathbf{E}$ nodes at 0, 10, 20 cm from the mirror, $\mathbf{H}$ nodes at 5, 15, 25 cm.
- Vacuum onto $\epsilon_r = 9$ ($\Gamma = -1/2$): $\lvert\tilde E\rvert$ between $0.5E_0$ and $1.5E_0$, minimum at the interface.
- [[problems/a-circular-wave-meets-a-mirror|Worked problem — A circular wave meets a mirror]]: a circular wave on a mirror. The two components' standing waves are 90° apart in time, so $\mathbf{S} = 0$ at every instant and $w = 2\epsilon_0E_0^2$ is constant.

> [!example] Example — a copper mirror at 10 GHz (this site's numbers)
> $\sigma = 5.8\times10^7$ S/m gives $\delta = 0.661\ \mu$m and $R_s = 26.1$ mΩ. The mirror current $2E_0/\eta_0$ dissipates $\tfrac12R_s(2E_0/\eta_0)^2 = (4R_s/\eta_0)\langle S_i\rangle$. The absorbed fraction $4R_s/\eta_0 = 2.77\times10^{-4}$ matches $1-\lvert\Gamma\rvert^2$ from the exact $\Gamma = (\eta_2-\eta_0)/(\eta_2+\eta_0)$: the mirror reflects 99.97 %.

> [!trap] Standing waves
> - $\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\rvert = 2E_0\lvert\sin\beta_1z\rvert$ repeats every $\lambda/2$, not every $\lambda$.
> - Fixed-node sinusoids drawn at several instants fit $\mathbf{E}$ or $\mathbf{H}$ alike; only the node or antinode at the mirror tells them apart.
> - In a lossless travelling wave $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}$ are in phase; here they are 90° apart. Only the *average* flux vanishes; $\mathbf{S}$ and $w$ do not.
> - Distances count from the mirror. A mirror at $z = d$ puts $z - d$ in place of $z$, with $E_0$ the incident phasor at the mirror (otherwise a factor $e^{-j\beta_1d}$).
> - With $\Gamma = -1$ the reflected $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}$ carries $-\Gamma = +1$: it keeps the incident sign.

> [!exam] On exams
> Conceptual only on the Exam 3 papers on hand. SP17 Exam 3 #1b–c: does a given $\sin\cdot\sin$, $\cos\cdot\cos$ pair satisfy Maxwell's equations, and do standing waves carry net power? Check both curl equations: $\beta = \omega\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}$ and the amplitude ratio $\eta$; then $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = 0$. SP19 Exam 3 #3c: what does a PEC reflection leave in front of the mirror? $\lvert\Gamma\rvert = 1$ against $\lvert\Gamma\rvert<1$. FA23 Exam 3 #1(c)–(d): the energy density of a standing wave, and a plot of fixed nodes: instantaneous against average, $\mathbf{E}$ against $\mathbf{H}$.
