> [!key] Ampère's law (static)
> $$
> \oint_C\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \mu_0I_C\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \oint_C\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = I_C = \int_S\mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{S},\qquad \mathbf{H}\equiv\frac{\mathbf{B}}{\mu_0}\ [\text{A/m}],
> $$
> $I_C$ ($= I_{\text{enc}}$) is the net current through **any** surface $S$ bounded by the closed directed path $C$, positive in the right-hand direction from the sense of $C$ (thumb along $d\mathbf{l}$, fingers through $S$; $d\mathbf{S}$ by the Stokes convention of [[1-electrostatics/06-circulation-and-boundary-conditions|Lecture 6]]). Currents not *linked* by $C$ contribute nothing, however close. $\mathbf{H}$ is introduced "for convenience": the field whose circulation is the current, in A/m; in free space just $\mathbf{B}/\mu_0$, in a medium $\mathbf{B} = \mu\mathbf{H}$. The circulation $\oint\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$ is the **magnetomotive force**, MMF, in amperes — twin of the EMF $\oint\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$ in volts; in statics the first is the linked current and the second is zero. Time-varying fields need the extra term $\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J} + \partial\mathbf{D}/\partial t$ ([[concepts/displacement-current|Displacement current]]).

**Physics.** Around a circle of radius $r$ centred on a long wire, $d\mathbf{l} = r\,d\phi\,\hat\phi$ and $\oint\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \dfrac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}\cdot2\pi r = \mu_0I$, **for any radius** — and the law holds for *every* closed path and every steady current distribution. It is Gauss's law with a loop. Both are exact for every surface/loop, and both are *useful* only when symmetry makes the field constant along the loop so it factors out: $H\cdot(\text{loop length}) = I_{\text{enc}}$. The three symmetries are the cylindrical ones (circle: $H_\phi\cdot2\pi r$), the planar ones (rectangle straddling a sheet or slab: $2H\cdot L$) and the solenoid (rectangle with one leg inside: $H\cdot L$) — [[concepts/current-sheets-and-slabs|Current sheets and slabs]], [[concepts/solenoids-and-toroids|Solenoids and toroids]].

> [!intuition] Gauss ↔ Ampère
> Gauss: flux of $\mathbf{D}$ out of a closed *surface* = charge inside. Ampère: circulation of $\mathbf{H}$ around a closed *loop* = current through. Same recipe (the slides put $\oint\mathbf{D}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = Q_{\text{enc}}$ atop the coax challenge). Pick the surface or loop matching the symmetry so the field leaves the integral as a constant; $D\cdot(\text{area}) = Q_{\text{enc}}$ becomes $H\cdot(\text{length}) = I_{\text{enc}}$ — "$B\cdot2\pi r$" is the magnetic "$D\cdot4\pi r^2$".

**Differential form — the laws of magnetostatics.** Stokes, $\oint_C\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \int_S(\nabla\times\mathbf{H})\cdot d\mathbf{S}$, equals $\int_S\mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$ for every $S$, so the integrands agree:

$$
\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J},\qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0 .
$$

Electrostatics: $\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = 0$, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{D} = \rho$. The magnetic field's source is in its **curl**, with no divergence; the electric field's is in its **divergence**, with no curl. $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$ is the law of [[1-electrostatics/03-gauss-law-at-work#6-the-magnetic-counterpart--bds--0|Lecture 3 §6]] ([[concepts/magnetic-flux|Magnetic flux Ψ]]) — no magnetic charge, closed lines — and holds for time-varying fields too. ("$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{H} = 0$", as a slide lists it, is equivalent only where $\mu$ is uniform.) The divergence of $\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}$ forces $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$, which is why the static law fails when charge accumulates and Maxwell had to add $\partial\mathbf{D}/\partial t$.

Check on the straight wire: off the axis, $(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})_z = \dfrac1r\dfrac{d}{dr}(rB_\phi) = \dfrac1r\dfrac{d}{dr}\Big(\dfrac{\mu_0I}{2\pi}\Big) = 0$ and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = \dfrac1r\partial_\phi B_\phi = 0$. The curl sits on the wire, where $\mathbf{J}$ is a δ-function ([[concepts/current-distributions|Current distributions: J, J_s, I]]).

**When "static" is good enough.** These laws hold *quasi-statically* for a time-varying $\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r},t)$ over a region of size $L$ if the current changes on a time scale $\tau\gg L/c$, equivalently $L\ll\lambda = c/f$. Then $\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r},t) = \mu_0\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{r},t)$ follows the instantaneous current, with a slowly varying $\mathbf{E}$ from Faraday's law ([[concepts/faradays-law|Faraday's law]]). This **magneto-quasi-static** (MQS) regime is where inductors live, as capacitors live in the quasi-static regime of [[1-electrostatics/10-capacitance-and-conductance|Lecture 10]].

**Example — the coaxial cable (slide challenge, Lecture 12).** A solid core of radius $a$ carries $I$ uniformly ($J = I/\pi a^2$); the thin shell at $r = b$ returns it. Where is $\mathbf{H}$ constant? By symmetry $\mathbf{H} = H_\phi(r)\hat\phi$, so $H_\phi\cdot2\pi r = I_{\text{enc}}(r)$:

$$
H_\phi = \begin{cases}\dfrac{Ir}{2\pi a^2}, & r<a\quad(I_{\text{enc}} = I\,r^2/a^2)\\[8pt] \dfrac{I}{2\pi r}, & a<r<b\\[8pt] 0, & r>b\quad(I_{\text{enc}} = I - I).\end{cases}
$$

Answer **(c)**, $r>b$: the only constant $H$ is zero. Each region translates a Lecture 3 result: linear growth like $D$ inside a uniformly charged cylinder, $1/r$ like $D$ outside a line charge, zero like $D$ outside a coax with $\pm$ charges.

**Other examples.** Straight wire: $H_\phi = I/(2\pi r)$ for any radius. Sheet $\mathbf{J}_s$: $\mathbf{H} = \tfrac12\mathbf{J}_s\times\hat n$. Slab: ramp $J_0x$ then $\pm J_0W/2$. Solenoid: $nI$ inside, zero outside. Across any current sheet the tangential $\mathbf{H}$ jumps by $J_s$ — the boundary condition $\hat n\times(\mathbf{H}_1-\mathbf{H}_2) = \mathbf{J}_s$ is Ampère's law on a vanishingly thin loop ([[concepts/boundary-conditions|Boundary conditions at an interface]]).

> [!trap] Counting the enclosed current
> - $I_{\text{enc}}$ is a *signed* sum. A clockwise contour linking $I_1$ out of the page and $I_2$ into the page has $I_{\text{enc}} = I_2 - I_1$ (into the page is the right-hand direction for clockwise); counter-clockwise, $I_1 - I_2$.
> - Only current *through* the surface counts: a wire passing the loop without threading it contributes nothing, though its field is present all along the path.
> - The sense of $\hat\phi$ follows the current, not the axes: a current in $-\hat x$ makes $\mathbf{H}$ circulate in $-\hat\phi$ about the $+x$ axis.
> - The surface may be any surface on $C$ — flat disk or bowl — and the answer must not depend on the choice; in statics it never does, and the two-surface paradox of [[3-maxwell-and-waves/16-charge-conservation-displacement-current-and-maxwells-equations|Lecture 16]] is what happens when it does.
> - Ampère's law gives the *field* only after symmetry has fixed its direction and dependence; without symmetry, [[concepts/biot-savart-law|Biot–Savart law]]. Cancel the arbitrary path length $L$ at the end; if it survives, the loop was wrong.

> [!exam] On exams
> The magnetic twin of Exam 1 problem 2: "a current distribution with planar/cylindrical symmetry — find $\mathbf{H}$ everywhere". State the symmetry ($\mathbf{H} = H_\phi(r)\hat\phi$ or $H_y(x)\hat y$), draw the loop, count $I_{\text{enc}}$ region by region with its sign, give units (A/m for $\mathbf{H}$, T for $\mathbf{B}$). A coax with unequal currents or a hollow core is the usual variant.
