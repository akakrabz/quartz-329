> [!key] Period, wavelength and phase velocity
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> A\cos\Big[\omega\Big(t\mp\frac zv\Big)\Big] = A\cos(\omega t\mp\beta z),\\[4pt]
> \beta\equiv\frac{\omega}{v} = \omega\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}\ \ [\text{rad/m}].
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \text{fixed } z:\quad T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \frac1f\quad\text{(period, s)},\\[4pt]
> \text{fixed } t:\quad \lambda = \frac{2\pi}{\beta}\quad\text{(wavelength, m)},\\[4pt]
> v_p = \frac{\omega}{\beta} = \frac{\lambda}{T} = \lambda f\quad\text{(phase velocity, m/s)}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> Constant phase $\omega t - \beta z = \phi_0$ sits at $z = (\omega t - \phi_0)/\beta$: speed $\omega/\beta$, one wavelength per period. Perfect dielectric: $v_p = 1/\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}$ at every frequency. Notes: $v$; here $v_p$, since other speeds appear ([[concepts/dispersion-relation|Dispersion relation and phase velocity]], [[concepts/group-velocity|Group velocity]]).

**Physics.** [[concepts/plane-waves|d'Alembert waves]] $f(t\mp z/v)$ are, by Fourier analysis, sums of cosines, so one frequency at a time suffices. A source at one frequency in a linear time-invariant medium produces that frequency everywhere ([[concepts/current-sheet-radiation|the notes' cosine sheet]]): a **monochromatic** wave, also *co-sinusoidal* (exams) or *time-harmonic* (notes). $\beta$ is the notes' **wave-number** (twin of the **wave-frequency** $\omega$), Rao's *phase constant*, physics' $k$. The wave repeats each $2\pi$ of $\omega t$ and of $\beta z$: $\beta$ is a *spatial* frequency, as in image processing. A probe at fixed $z$ records a cosine of period $T$; a snapshot at fixed $t$ shows a cosine of wavelength $\lambda$; the crests advance $\lambda$ per $T$. Written with complex amplitudes it becomes the phasor $E_0e^{\mp j\beta z}$ ([[concepts/phasors|Phasors]]).

**Examples.**

> [!example] Example — everything from one expression (this site's numbers)
> Lossless, non-magnetic: $\mathbf{E} = 12\cos\big(4\pi\times10^8\,t - \tfrac{8\pi}{3}\,y\big)\,\hat z$ V/m.
> - $f = 200$ MHz, $T = 5$ ns; $\beta = 8\pi/3\approx8.38$ rad/m, $\lambda = 0.75$ m.
> - Opposite signs: toward $+y$ ([[concepts/plane-waves|reading a wave]]); $v_p = \omega/\beta = \lambda f = 1.5\times10^8$ m/s $= c/2$.
> - $\sqrt{\mu_r\epsilon_r} = c/v_p = 2$; only "non-magnetic" gives $\epsilon_r = 4$, $\eta = \eta_0/\sqrt{\epsilon_r} = 60\pi\approx188\ \Omega$.
> - Probes 25 cm apart along $y$: downstream lags $\beta\,\Delta y = 2\pi/3$ (120°): $\Delta y/v_p = 1.67$ ns $= T/3$.
> - Partner field: $\mathbf{H} = 63.7\cos\big(4\pi\times10^8t - \tfrac{8\pi}{3}y\big)\,\hat x$ mA/m; power $0.382$ W/m² ([[concepts/time-average-power|Time averages and average power]]).

- Notes' sheet $\hat x\,2\cos\omega t$ A/m in vacuum: $\cos(\omega t\mp\beta z)$ with $\beta = \omega/c$, $\lambda = 2\pi/\beta$; the delay $z/c$ became the phase $\beta z$.

**The spectrum (notes p. 4).** The chart's frequency bar is schematic; compute $f = c/\lambda$:

| band | wavelength on the chart | $f = c/\lambda$ |
|---|---|---|
| radio | $10^3$ m | 300 kHz |
| microwave | $10^{-2}$ m | 30 GHz |
| infrared | $10^{-5}$ m | 30 THz |
| visible | $0.5\times10^{-6}$ m | 600 THz |
| ultraviolet | $10^{-8}$ m | $3\times10^{16}$ Hz |
| X-ray | $10^{-10}$ m | $3\times10^{18}$ Hz |
| gamma ray | $10^{-12}$ m | $3\times10^{20}$ Hz |

Memorize: 1 MHz ↔ 300 m, 100 MHz ↔ 3 m, 1 GHz ↔ 30 cm, 10 GHz ↔ 3 cm; visible 400 nm (750 THz, violet) to 700 nm (430 THz, red). Radio and visible pass the atmosphere.

> [!trap]
> - $\omega$ is in rad/s, $f$ in Hz: $T = 2\pi/\omega = 1/f$. A coefficient $4\pi\times10^8$ of $t$ is 200 MHz, not 400.
> - $\lambda = 2\pi/\beta$ in the *medium*; the free-space $c/f$ is longer by $\sqrt{\mu_r\epsilon_r}$.
> - $v_p$ gives only the product $\mu_r\epsilon_r$; "non-magnetic" or $\eta$ splits it.
> - Opposite signs of the $t$ and $z$ terms: travel toward $+z$ ([[concepts/plane-waves|Plane waves]]).

> [!exam] On exams
> **Numbers off an expression** (SP17 #3c, Su20 HE3 #2a, #2f, Su20 FE #3a): $\omega$ from the $t$ coefficient, $\beta$ from the position coefficient, then $f$, $T$, $\lambda$, $v_p$, $\epsilon_r$ (non-magnetic), $\eta$, direction. Lossy media add $\alpha$ ([[concepts/propagation-constant|Propagation constant]]). No-calculator: $c = 3\times10^8$ m/s, $\eta_0 = 120\pi\ \Omega$.
