> [!key] Definition
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \mathbf{M} = N\mathbf{m}\qquad\Big[\frac{1}{\text{m}^3}\cdot\text{A}\cdot\text{m}^2 = \text{A/m}\Big],\\[4pt]
> \mathbf{J}_M = \nabla\times\mathbf{M}\quad[\text{A/m}^2],\qquad\qquad \mathbf{J}_{sM} = \mathbf{M}\times\hat n\quad[\text{A/m}],\qquad\qquad \mathbf{H}\equiv\frac{\mathbf{B}}{\mu_0}-\mathbf{M}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> $N$ atoms per unit volume, $\mathbf{m}$ the *average* moment of one atomic current loop; $\hat n$ the **outward** normal of the body, and at an interface ($\hat n$ from 2 into 1) $\mathbf{J}_{sM} = \hat n\times(\mathbf{M}_1-\mathbf{M}_2)$. Same unit as $\mathbf{H}$ (the slides' ink note) and as a surface current; twin $\mathbf{P} = N\mathbf{p}$ [C/m²], a surface-charge unit. **The surface term is this site's addition** (notes and slides: volume term only).

**Physics: why matter needs new bookkeeping.** The equations of [[3-maxwell-and-waves/16-charge-conservation-displacement-current-and-maxwells-equations|Lecture 16]], with $\mathbf{D} = \epsilon_0\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{H}$ written out:

$$
\begin{gathered}
\nabla\cdot(\epsilon_0\mathbf{E}) = \rho,\qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0,\\[4pt]
\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t},\qquad \nabla\times\frac{\mathbf{B}}{\mu_0} = \mathbf{J}+\frac{\partial(\epsilon_0\mathbf{E})}{\partial t}.
\end{gathered}
$$

These **microscopic** equations need every charge in $\rho$ and $\mathbf{J}$ — hopeless in matter, where a cubic millimetre of copper holds about $2.5\times10^{21}$ electrons, nearly all bound. As for dielectrics ([[concepts/polarization|Polarization P and bound charge]]), average over many atoms and split sources into **free** (you control them) and **bound** (the material supplies them):

**Sources in matter, averaged:**

$$
\rho = \rho_f - \nabla\cdot\mathbf{P},\qquad \mathbf{J} = \mathbf{J}_f + \frac{\partial\mathbf{P}}{\partial t} + \nabla\times\mathbf{M}.
$$

|---|---|---|---|
| $\rho_f$, $\mathbf{J}_f$ | free | placed charges; drifting carriers ($\sigma\mathbf{E}$) | Lectures 2, 8, 12 |
| $-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{P}$ | bound charge | uncancelled dipole ends | [[1-electrostatics/08-conductors-dielectrics-and-polarization\|Lecture 8]] |
| $\partial\mathbf{P}/\partial t$ | polarization current | bound charges moving as dipoles stretch | [[1-electrostatics/11-lorentz-drude-models-for-conductivity-and-susceptibility#5-polarization-current-bound-charges-that-carry-an-ac-current\|Lecture 11]] |
| $\nabla\times\mathbf{M}$ | magnetization current | uncancelled atomic loops | Lecture 17 |

$\mathbf{P}$, $\mathbf{M}$: electric, magnetic dipole moment per unit volume. Bound sources conserve charge by themselves: $\partial_t(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{P}) + \nabla\cdot(\partial_t\mathbf{P}) = 0$, $\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{M}) = 0$. Moving $\nabla\times\mathbf{M}$ to the left of Ampère–Maxwell and $\partial_t\mathbf{P}$ into $\partial_t\mathbf{D}$ gives $\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}_f+\partial\mathbf{D}/\partial t$ ([[concepts/permeability|Permeability, susceptibility, and B = μH]] has the steps).

**Atomic magnetic dipoles.** An orbiting electron is a loop current $I = e/T$, flowing **opposite** to its velocity; from far away it is a magnetic dipole ([[concepts/magnetic-dipole|The current loop and the magnetic dipole]], [[2-magnetostatics/13-current-sheets-solenoids-and-the-vector-potential#6-the-current-loop-and-the-magnetic-dipole|Lecture 13 §6]]) with **moment $\mathbf{m}$, and a field exerts a torque on it**:

$$
\mathbf{m} = I\mathbf{A} = IA\,\hat n\quad[\text{A}\cdot\text{m}^2],\qquad\qquad \mathbf{T} = \mathbf{m}\times\mathbf{B}\quad[\text{N}\cdot\text{m}],
$$

$\hat n$ by the right-hand rule. Circle: $m = I\pi a^2$ (slides: $\mathbf{m} = I(\pi a^2)\hat a_z$ in the $xy$-plane). Uniform $\mathbf{B}$: zero net force, $\oint I\,d\mathbf{l}\times\mathbf{B} = I(\oint d\mathbf{l})\times\mathbf{B} = 0$, but a torque $mB\sin\theta$ turns $\mathbf{m}$ toward $\mathbf{B}$ like a compass needle (slide: the couple $I\,d\mathbf{l}\times\mathbf{B}_0$ on a tilted orbit). Twin: $\mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{E}$.

> [!derivation]- The torque on a square loop, side by side
> Square loop, side $\ell$, $\mathbf{m} = I\ell^2(0,\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$, tilted by $\theta$ about $x$ from $\mathbf{B} = B\hat z$. Two sides run along $\pm\hat x$ at $\mp\tfrac\ell2\hat u$, $\hat u = (0,\cos\theta,-\sin\theta)$ (so $\hat x\times\hat u = \mathbf{m}/m$):
> $$
> \mathbf{F}_{+x} = I\ell\,\hat x\times B\hat z = -I\ell B\,\hat y,\qquad \mathbf{F}_{-x} = +I\ell B\,\hat y .
> $$
> The sides along $\pm\hat u$ feel $\pm I\ell B\cos\theta\,\hat x$ on one line through the centre: no torque. Net force zero; torque about the centre:
> $$
> \Big(-\tfrac\ell2\hat u\Big)\times(-I\ell B\hat y) + \Big(\tfrac\ell2\hat u\Big)\times(I\ell B\hat y) = I\ell^2B\,(\hat u\times\hat y) = I\ell^2B\sin\theta\,\hat x = \mathbf{m}\times\mathbf{B}.
> $$
> Tiling a flat loop with squares (shared edges cancel, as below) extends it to any planar loop in uniform $\mathbf{B}$.

**Sources of atomic loops** (notes): **orbital** motion (slides' **L**); electron **spin** (slides' **S**), quantized, with no working classical picture; charge motion **inside protons and neutrons**, far weaker. Paired electrons cancel; only unpaired ones leave a permanent moment. With no field, thermal motion scatters the moments to zero sum; a field torques them toward alignment (slides), and $\mathbf{M} = N\mathbf{m}$ is the result.

**The magnetization current.** The notes' argument for a curl: bound charge is conserved, $\partial\rho_b/\partial t + \nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}_b = 0$; with $\rho_b = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf{P}$,

$$
\nabla\cdot\Big(\mathbf{J}_b - \frac{\partial\mathbf{P}}{\partial t}\Big) = 0 .
$$

So bound current beyond $\partial\mathbf{P}/\partial t$ is divergence-free, hence a curl (as $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$, [[concepts/vector-potential|Vector potential]]) — the **bound current density**

$$
\mathbf{J}_b = \frac{\partial\mathbf{P}}{\partial t} + \nabla\times\mathbf{M}.
$$

$\partial\mathbf{P}/\partial t$ is *not sufficient* (notes): orbits and spins are closed loops that never pile up charge, invisible to continuity. $\mathbf{J}_M = \nabla\times\mathbf{M}$ (slides' symbol) is the **magnetization current density**.

Conservation only *allows* the term (a gradient could be added to $\mathbf{M}$, as to $\mathbf{A}$); $\mathbf{M} = N\mathbf{m}$ pins it down, and the picture shows its curl is the leftover loop current.

**Loops cancel inside, survive at the edges.** Cells of side $s$, loop current $I$: $m = Is^2$, $M = m/s^3 = I/s$. Interior edges cancel in pairs; boundary edges leave $I$ per length $s$, a surface current $I/s = M$ [A/m]. If $\mathbf{M}$ varies, shared edges stop cancelling: $\mathbf{M} = M_z(x)\hat z$ leaves $-\partial M_z/\partial x$ along $\hat y$, exactly $(\nabla\times\mathbf{M})_y$. The surface term is the twin of $\rho_{sb} = \mathbf{P}\cdot\hat n$ ([[1-electrostatics/08-conductors-dielectrics-and-polarization#3-dielectrics-bound-charge-and-polarization|Lecture 8 §3]]): across the layer where $\mathbf{M}$ falls to zero, $\nabla$ acts as $\hat n$ on the jump (outside minus inside), $\hat n\times(0-\mathbf{M}) = \mathbf{M}\times\hat n$, as $-\hat n\cdot(0-\mathbf{P}) = \mathbf{P}\cdot\hat n$.

**A magnetized rod is a solenoid.** $\mathbf{M} = M\hat z$ (figure, right): side $\mathbf{M}\times\hat r = M\hat\phi$, a winding with $nI\to M$; ends $\mathbf{M}\times(\pm\hat z) = 0$. Inside a long rod $\mathbf{B}\approx\mu_0\mathbf{M}$ (no free current; [[concepts/permeability|Permeability, susceptibility, and B = μH]] derives the $\mu_0\mathbf{M}$). A $\mu_0M\approx1.3$ T magnet acts like 1000 turns/m carrying 1000 A ($M\approx1.0\times10^{6}$ A/m). Unlike a dielectric's bound charge, which *opposes* $\mathbf{E}$, bound currents *reinforce* $\mathbf{B}$ in para- and ferromagnets.

**Linear media.** $\mathbf{M} = \chi_m\mathbf{H}$, so $\mathbf{B} = \mu\mathbf{H}$ and $\mathbf{M} = (\mu_r-1)\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{B}/\mu_0-\mathbf{H}$ ([[concepts/permeability|Permeability, susceptibility, and B = μH]]). Get $\mathbf{H}$ from free currents, then $\mathbf{B}$, then $\mathbf{M}$; free plus magnetization currents in the vacuum formulas must give back $\mathbf{B}$.

**The dictionary: polarization ↔ magnetization.** The slides' first annotation is "M → P"; the deck pairs the two throughout. Extending the [[2-magnetostatics/index#the-dictionary-electrostatics--magnetostatics|Unit 2 dictionary]]:

> [!intuition] The four places where the analogy is *not* a straight translation
> 1. **$\epsilon_0$ in $\mathbf{P} = \epsilon_0\chi_e\mathbf{E}$, none in $\mathbf{M} = \chi_m\mathbf{H}$**: units.
> 2. **$-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{P}$ but $+\nabla\times\mathbf{M}$**: converging $\mathbf{P}$ crowds $+$ ends together; loop currents just add.
> 3. **$\mathbf{D}$ adds $\mathbf{P}$, $\mathbf{H}$ subtracts $\mathbf{M}$**: $\mathbf{B}$ (felt by charges) is the twin of $\mathbf{E}$; a dielectric weakens $\mathbf{E}$, a para- or ferromagnet strengthens $\mathbf{B}$.
> 4. **$\chi_m$ can be negative, $\chi_e$ cannot**: electric dipoles are induced along the field, orbital moments against it (Lenz); only permanent moments make $\chi_m>0$.

**Examples.**

> [!example] This site's numbers — the size of an atomic moment
> Bohr hydrogen: $a_0 = 5.29\times10^{-11}$ m, $v = 2.19\times10^{6}$ m/s: $I = ev/(2\pi a_0) \approx 1.06$ mA through about $10^{-20}$ m², $m = I\pi a_0^2 = eva_0/2 \approx 9.28\times10^{-24}$ A·m² — the **Bohr magneton** $\mu_B = 9.27\times10^{-24}$ A·m² to rounding, the unit of atomic and spin moments; $\mathbf{m}$ antiparallel to the angular momentum.

- *Upper limit, saturated iron:* $N\approx8.5\times10^{28}$ atoms/m³ at about $2.2\,\mu_B$ each, all aligned: $M\approx1.7\times10^{6}$ A/m, $\mu_0M\approx2.2$ T — why iron cores saturate near 2 T.
- *Graded:* $\mathbf{M} = M_0(x/d)\hat z$ ⇒ uniform $\nabla\times\mathbf{M} = -(M_0/d)\hat y$.

> [!example] Slides (solved in class) — a magnetic slab between two current sheets
> Slab $\mu_r = 100$ between sheets $-0.1\hat y$ A/m on top and $+0.1\hat y$ A/m below ($x$ right, $z$ up, $y$ into the page). Find $\mathbf{H}$, $\mathbf{B}$, $\mathbf{M}$; compare $\mu_r = 1$.
> - $\mathbf{H}$, each sheet $\tfrac12\mathbf{J}_s\times\hat n$ ($\hat n$ toward the point): top $\tfrac12(-0.1\hat y)\times(-\hat z) = +0.05\hat x$, bottom $\tfrac12(0.1\hat y)\times\hat z = +0.05\hat x$; $\mathbf{H} = 0.1\hat x$ A/m $= J_s\hat x$ between, $0$ outside.
> - No free current on the slab ("no $J_s$ on slab"), so $H_t$ continuous: $\mathbf{H}_{\text{slab}} = 0.1\hat x$ A/m.
> - $\mathbf{B}_{\text{slab}} = \mu_0\mu_r\mathbf{H} = 100\mu_0(0.1)\hat x = 1.26\times10^{-5}\hat x$ T (12.6 μT); $\mathbf{M} = \chi_m\mathbf{H} = 99\times0.1\hat x = 9.9\hat x$ A/m; $\mu_0(\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{M}) = \mu_0(10)\hat x$ ✓.
> - $\mu_r = 1$: same $\mathbf{H}$, $\mathbf{B} = \mu_0(0.1)\hat x = 1.26\times10^{-7}\hat x$ T (100 times smaller), $\mathbf{M} = 0$.
>
> *Bound-current check (site):* $\nabla\times\mathbf{M} = 0$; top $\mathbf{M}\times\hat z = 9.9\hat x\times\hat z = -9.9\hat y$ A/m, bottom $+9.9\hat y$ A/m, each along its free sheet. Free plus bound, $10$ A/m per face, in the *vacuum* formula: $\mathbf{B} = \mu_0(10\text{ A/m})\hat x = 100\mu_0\mathbf{H}$ in the slab, $\mu_0(0.1)\hat x$ in any air gap, zero outside.

> [!trap]
> - $\mathbf{M}\neq\mu_0\chi_m\mathbf{H}$ (tesla): $\mathbf{M}$ and $\mathbf{H}$ share A/m.
> - $\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{B}/\mu_0-\mathbf{H}$; not $\mathbf{B}/\mu_0$, nor $\mathbf{B}/\mu-\mathbf{H}$ (identically zero).
> - No minus sign: $\mathbf{J}_M = +\nabla\times\mathbf{M}$ (contrast $\rho_b = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf{P}$); at an interface each face uses its own outward normal, $\mathbf{M}_2\times\hat n+\mathbf{M}_1\times(-\hat n) = \hat n\times(\mathbf{M}_1-\mathbf{M}_2)$.
> - $\mathbf{M}$ follows the total local $\mathbf{H}$; $\chi_m\mathbf{H}_{\text{applied}}$ only where the material leaves $\mathbf{H}$ unchanged (long rod along the field, slab with $\mathbf{H}$ along its faces).

> [!exam] On exams
> The slab-and-sheets problem with the $\mu_r = 1$ comparison; $\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{B}/\mu_0-\mathbf{M}$ and $\mathbf{J}_M = \nabla\times\mathbf{M}$ from a given $\mathbf{M}$; "no $\mu_0$" in $\mathbf{M} = \chi_m\mathbf{H}$; the polarization ↔ magnetization dictionary entries (which field adds, which subtracts, which sign).
