> [!key] Polarization
> $$
> \mathbf{P} \equiv N\,\mathbf{p} = \text{dipole moment per unit volume}\qquad[\text{C·m/m}^3 = \text{C/m}^2],
> $$
> $N$ = dipoles per unit volume, $\mathbf{p} = q\mathbf{d}$ each (charge times displacement from $-$ to $+$). $\mathbf{P}$ has the units of surface charge density, no accident: on any surface of a polarized body, outward normal $\hat{n}$, the bound surface charge is
> $$
> \rho_{sb} = \mathbf{P}\cdot\hat{n},
> $$
> and a non-uniform $\mathbf{P}$ leaves a bound **volume** charge $\rho_b = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf{P}$ (more dipole "heads" arrive than "tails" leave). Bound charge is real and makes real fields; you just did not place it and cannot move it. A neutral dielectric's total bound charge is zero. $\mathbf{D}\equiv\epsilon_0\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{P}$ absorbs it ([[concepts/electric-flux-density|Electric flux density D]]).

**Physics.** An insulator's electrons are bound to their atoms; an applied field only *displaces* them slightly from the nuclei, making each atom a small **dipole** $\mathbf{p} = q\mathbf{d}$, aligned with $\mathbf{E}$. The material is **polarized**.

Inside, each dipole's $+$ end sits next to its neighbour's $-$ end and they cancel on average. At the faces the cancellation fails: uncompensated $+$ on the face the dipoles point toward, $-$ on the other. These **bound surface charges** $\pm\rho_{sb}$ oppose the applied field, but, tethered, they cannot keep moving until it vanishes (unlike a [[concepts/conductors|conductor's]] free charge): the interior field is **reduced, not eliminated**. Only where $\mathbf{P}$ *ends* (a surface) or *changes* (a gradient) is charge left over.

> [!derivation]- The averaged field of a lattice of dipoles
> Lattice with spacings $\Delta x,\Delta y,\Delta z$, each cell one proton–electron pair displaced by $d$ along $\hat{z}$ ($\mathbf{p} = ed\,\hat{z}$, $N = 1/(\Delta x\Delta y\Delta z)$). Along $z$, protons form sheets $+e/(\Delta x\Delta y)$ and electrons sheets $-e/(\Delta x\Delta y)$, each $+$ sheet $d$ above its $-$ partner. Within a pair: $\mathbf{E}_1 = -\hat{z}\,\frac{e}{\epsilon_0\Delta x\Delta y}$ (opposite to $\mathbf{p}$); between pairs $\mathbf{E}_2 = 0$. Pairs fill a fraction $d/\Delta z$ of the volume, so the averaged bound-charge field is
> $$
> \mathbf{E}_p = \mathbf{E}_1\frac{d}{\Delta z} + \mathbf{E}_2\frac{\Delta z - d}{\Delta z} = -\hat{z}\frac{e\,d}{\epsilon_0\,\Delta x\Delta y\Delta z} = -\frac{N\mathbf{p}}{\epsilon_0} = -\frac{\mathbf{P}}{\epsilon_0}.
> $$
> Inside an infinite slab polarized *normal to its faces*, $\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{E}_0 + \mathbf{E}_p = \mathbf{E}_0 - \mathbf{P}/\epsilon_0$, $\mathbf{E}_0$ being the free charges' field: weaker ($\mathbf{P}\parallel\mathbf{E}_0$), not zero. (Polarized *along* its faces: no bound surface charge, $\mathbf{E}_p = 0$, $\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{E}_0$ inside.)

The bound field is $\mathbf{E}_p = -\mathbf{P}/\epsilon_0$ in an infinite slab polarized normal to its faces ($\mathbf{D}$ the same inside and out), $-\mathbf{P}/3\epsilon_0$ in a sphere, and zero in a slab polarized along its faces ($\mathbf{E}$ unchanged, $\mathbf{D}$ jumps): "$\mathbf{D}$ unchanged" holds for one geometry, not in general. The general facts are $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{D} = \rho_f$ and continuity of $D_n$ across interfaces without free charge.

**Linear media.** $\mathbf{P} = \epsilon_0\chi_e\mathbf{E}$ gives $\mathbf{D} = \epsilon\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{P} = (\epsilon_r-1)\epsilon_0\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{D}-\epsilon_0\mathbf{E}$ ([[concepts/permittivity|Permittivity, susceptibility, and D = εE]]). Get $\mathbf{D}$ from free charge, then $\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{D}/\epsilon$, then $\mathbf{P}$ ([[concepts/static-fields-in-dielectrics|Static fields in dielectrics]]). Across an interface the pillbox gives $\hat{n}\cdot(\mathbf{P}_1-\mathbf{P}_2) = -\rho_{sb}$: bound surface charge is how much normal $\mathbf{P}$ *decreases* from medium 2 into medium 1 (along $\hat{n}$).

**Magnetic twin.** [[concepts/magnetization|Magnetization M and magnetization current]] ([[3-maxwell-and-waves/17-magnetization-and-maxwells-equations-in-matter|Lecture 17]]), $\mathbf{M} = N\mathbf{m}$ [A/m], with bound *currents* $\nabla\times\mathbf{M}$ and $\mathbf{M}\times\hat n$ for $-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{P}$ and $\mathbf{P}\cdot\hat n$, and $\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{B}/\mu_0-\mathbf{M}$ for $\mathbf{D} = \epsilon_0\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{P}$. Not quite literal: $\mathbf{M} = \chi_m\mathbf{H}$ has no $\mu_0$, the bound current no minus sign, $\mathbf{H}$ subtracts $\mathbf{M}$ where $\mathbf{D}$ adds $\mathbf{P}$, and $\chi_m$ can be negative.

**Examples.** Worked in full on [[concepts/static-fields-in-dielectrics|Static fields in dielectrics]] and [[concepts/permittivity|Permittivity, susceptibility, and D = εE]]; the bound-charge results: slab in $18\hat{x}$ V/m with $3\hat{x}$ inside — $\mathbf{P} = 15\epsilon_0\hat{x}$, faces $\pm15\epsilon_0$ C/m², whose sheet field $-15$ V/m accounts for $18-3$. Dielectric interface with $P_{1z} = -10\epsilon_0$ above and $P_{2z} = -16\epsilon_0$ below: $\rho_{sb} = P_{2n}-P_{1n} = -6\epsilon_0$ C/m² ([[problems/fields-across-a-dielectric-interface|Worked problem — Fields across a dielectric interface]]). Graded $\epsilon(z)$ between plates: $P_z = \epsilon_0z/2$ ⇒ uniform $\rho_b = -\epsilon_0/2$ C/m³ with no free charge at all. Metal sphere $Q$ in a dielectric shell: $\rho_b = 0$, bound totals $\mp(1-1/\epsilon_r)Q$ on the inner and outer surfaces. Slab between sheets $\pm\rho_{s0}$ with susceptibility $\chi_e$: $\rho_{sb} = \chi_e\rho_{s0}/(1+\chi_e)$.

> [!trap]
> - $\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{D} - \epsilon\mathbf{E}$ is identically zero; it is $\mathbf{D} - \epsilon_0\mathbf{E}$.
> - $\mathbf{P} = \chi_e\mathbf{E}$ is missing an $\epsilon_0$ (units: C/m² ≠ V/m).
> - $\mathbf{P}$ responds to the total field, not the applied field alone.
> - Between two dielectrics, $\hat{n}\cdot(\mathbf{P}_1-\mathbf{P}_2) = -\rho_{sb}$ (minus sign; compare $\hat{n}\cdot(\mathbf{D}_1-\mathbf{D}_2) = +\rho_s$).
> - A uniform dielectric with no free charge in its bulk has $\rho_b = 0$; free charge embedded in one drags along $\rho_b = -(\chi_e/\epsilon_r)\rho$.

> [!exam] On exams
> FA26 3(c): $\mathbf{P}$ on both sides of a dielectric interface — $\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{D} - \epsilon_0\mathbf{E} = (\epsilon_r-1)\epsilon_0\mathbf{E}$, each side with its own $\epsilon_r$. Bound charge on a face: $\rho_{sb} = \mathbf{P}\cdot\hat{n}$, $\hat{n}$ out of the dielectric.
