> [!key] Curl
> $$
> (\nabla\times\mathbf{v})\cdot\hat{n} = \lim_{\Delta S\to 0}\frac{\oint_C \mathbf{v}\cdot d\mathbf{l}}{\Delta S}
> \qquad(\text{$C$ bounds $\Delta S$, right-hand rule between $d\mathbf{l}$ and $\hat{n}$})
> $$
> In Cartesian components,
> $$
> \nabla\times\mathbf{v} =
> \begin{vmatrix}\hat{x}&\hat{y}&\hat{z}\\[2pt] \dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}&\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}&\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}\\[6pt] v_x&v_y&v_z\end{vmatrix}
> = \hat{x}\Big(\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial v_y}{\partial z}\Big)
> + \hat{y}\Big(\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial x}\Big)
> + \hat{z}\Big(\frac{\partial v_y}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y}\Big).
> $$
> A **vector** field. Cylindrical and spherical forms on the [[0-toolkit/02-vector-calculus-cheatsheet|cheat sheet]].

**Physics.** The **circulation** around a closed curve $C$ is $\oint_C\mathbf{v}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$. Shrink the loop, divide by its area; the limit depends on orientation, so it is a vector, defined component by component. The $\hat{z}$-component from a small $xy$ rectangle 1–2–3–4: the $v_y$ legs give $[v_y(\text{right}) - v_y(\text{left})]\Delta y \approx \frac{\partial v_y}{\partial x}\Delta x\Delta y$, the $v_x$ legs $-\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y}\Delta x\Delta y$. Nonzero curl means the line integral from 1 to 3 differs along 1→2→3 and 1→4→3 — *path-dependent*. Tiling a surface with such loops gives [[concepts/stokes-theorem|Stokes' theorem]].

$\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t$ (Faraday) and $\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}+\partial\mathbf{D}/\partial t$ (Ampère–Maxwell): time-varying magnetic fields and currents make fields circulate ([[concepts/maxwells-equations|Maxwell's equations]]). In **statics** $\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = 0$: every Coulomb field is curl-free, so by superposition every electrostatic field is → a potential exists ([[concepts/conservative-field|Conservative (curl-free) fields and the potential]]).

> [!intuition] "Variation across the flow"
> For a unidirectional field, curl is nonzero exactly when it changes *across its own direction*: $+y$ arrows growing with $x$ — a paddlewheel spins because its right side is pushed harder. A uniform stream has no curl; a straight shear flow does, though nothing goes "around". Curved flow adds geometry: rigid rotation ($\mathbf{v}\propto r\hat{\phi}$) has curl though the speed is equal on both sides of a small wheel, while the free vortex $\mathbf{v}\propto\hat{\phi}/r$ — the field around a wire — has *zero* curl except on its axis. Spin axis (right-hand rule) = direction of $\nabla\times\mathbf{v}$. Side-by-side panels with the divergence picture: [[concepts/divergence|Divergence]].

**Identities** for any smooth fields:

$$
\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{A}) = 0,\qquad
\nabla\times(\nabla f) = 0,\qquad
\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf{A} = \nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}) - \nabla^2\mathbf{A},\qquad \nabla^2\mathbf{A}\equiv\hat{x}\nabla^2A_x+\hat{y}\nabla^2A_y+\hat{z}\nabla^2A_z .
$$

(The identity holds in any coordinates; the component-wise Laplacian on the right is valid for *Cartesian* components only.) The first gives charge conservation from Ampère–Maxwell ([[concepts/continuity-equation|Continuity equation (charge conservation)]]); the second is why $\mathbf{E} = -\nabla V$ is automatically curl-free.

**Examples.**
- Slides, "a whirlpool of strength 5": $\mathbf{v} = -3y\,\hat{x} + 2x\,\hat{y}$. Only the $\hat{z}$-component can be nonzero: $\nabla\times\mathbf{v} = \hat{z}\left(\dfrac{\partial(2x)}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial(-3y)}{\partial y}\right) = \hat{z}\,(2+3) = 5\hat{z}$, uniform. (Compare $\mathbf{v} = 3x\hat{x}+2y\hat{y}$: a "faucet of strength 5", zero curl.)
- $\mathbf{E} = E_0(y^2\hat{x}+2xy\hat{y}+3z^2\hat{z})$: $x$- and $y$-components vanish by inspection (no $z$-dependence in $E_x,E_y$; $E_z$ depends only on $z$); $\hat{z}$ component $\partial_x(2xy)-\partial_y(y^2) = 2y-2y = 0$. Curl-free ⇒ a potential $V = -E_0(xy^2+z^3)$ exists.
- Any radial field $g(r)\,\mathbf{r}$ is curl-free ([[concepts/conservative-field|Conservative (curl-free) fields and the potential]]).

> [!trap] Determinant sign errors — even solution keys make them
> - The middle term carries a **minus** (top-row expansion), and the $\hat{z}$ term is $\partial_x v_y - \partial_y v_x$, in that order. The backwards bracket turns up even in official keys, unpunished only when the two partials are equal.
> - Expand mechanically, then *check one component with the "across the flow" picture*.

> [!exam] On exams
> FA26 problem 1(a): $\nabla\times\mathbf{E}$ of a polynomial field (zero) — the determinant expanded correctly, every time. "Curl-free" says nothing about whether $\rho$ is zero.
