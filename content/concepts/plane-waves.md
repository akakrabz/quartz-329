> [!key] Uniform plane TEM wave, and the rule that pairs E with H
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \mathbf{E} = \hat x\,f\Big(t\mp\frac zv\Big),\qquad \mathbf{H} = \pm\hat y\,\frac{f(t\mp z/v)}{\eta},\qquad v = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}},\quad \eta = \sqrt{\frac\mu\epsilon}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> A field that depends on position only through $t - \hat u\cdot\mathbf{r}/v$, with $\mathbf{E}\perp\hat u$ and
> $$
> \mathbf{H} = \frac1\eta\,\hat u\times\mathbf{E},\qquad \mathbf{E} = \eta\,\mathbf{H}\times\hat u,\qquad \mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}\parallel\hat u .
> $$
> The notes: divide $\mathbf{E}$ by $\eta$ and rotate 90° so that $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}$ points along the travel ([[concepts/intrinsic-impedance|Intrinsic impedance]]). $\mathbf{E}$'s direction is the **polarization** ("$x$-polarized"). *TEM*: $\mathbf{E},\mathbf{H}\perp$ the travel — for fields depending on $z$ alone, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E} = \nabla\cdot\mathbf{H} = 0$ forbids a $z$-component ($\partial E_z/\partial z\neq0$). *Travel along $\mathbf{S}\equiv\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}$*, the [[concepts/poynting-vector|Poynting vector]], now the definition: $\hat x\times(\pm\hat y) = \pm\hat z$. *Uniform plane*: the same field over each **plane of constant phase** $t - z/v = t_0$, i.e. $z = v(t - t_0)$, perpendicular to $\mathbf{S}$, moving at the **phase velocity** $v_p = v$ ($c$ in free space). An antenna's spherical waves are TEM but not uniform (ECE 450).

**Physics.** The simplest source-free Maxwell solution: the notes try $\mathbf{E} = \hat x\,E_x(z,t)$, the slides start from an infinite current sheet; both reduce the curl equations to two component equations solved by d'Alembert's $E_x = Af(t - z/v) + Bg(t + z/v)$, $H_y = (Af - Bg)/\eta$ ([[concepts/wave-equation|Wave equation]] has the reduction). Nothing along the travel: $\hat z\,f(t\mp z/v)$ solves the 1D wave equation but has $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E} = \mp f'/v\neq0$. A sheet launches one wave per face, $\mathbf{E} = -\tfrac\eta2\mathbf{J}_s$, $\mathbf{H} = \tfrac12\mathbf{J}_s\times\hat n$ ([[concepts/current-sheet-radiation|Radiation from a current sheet]]).

**Reading a wave.** A field depending on position and time only through $t - \hat u\cdot\mathbf{r}/v$ travels along $+\hat u$ at speed $v$. Two ways to read an argument:

- Factor out the coefficient of $t$: $at - bz = a\,(t - z/(a/b))$ ($a, b>0$), so $f(at - bz)$ travels toward $+z$ at $a/b$, and $f(at + bz)$ toward $-z$ at $a/b$.
- Compare the signs of the $t$ term and the position term: opposite → toward $+$ that axis; same → toward $-$. Term order and overall sign do not matter: $z - vt = -v(t - z/v)$, so a function of $z - vt$ travels toward $+z$.

**Shift identities** (record at one place ↔ snapshot at one instant; slide 25).

$$
\begin{gathered}
\text{toward}\ {+z}:\quad E(z,t) = E\Big(0,\ t-\frac zv\Big) = E(z - vt,\ 0),\\[4pt]
\text{toward}\ {-z}:\quad E(z,t) = E\Big(0,\ t+\frac zv\Big) = E(z + vt,\ 0).
\end{gathered}
$$
A probe at $z$ sees what the probe at $0$ saw $z/v$ earlier ($+z$ travel) or will see $z/v$ later ($-z$); the snapshot at $t$ is the $t = 0$ snapshot slid $vt$ along the travel. **The mirror:** at $t = 0$ a $+z$ wave has $E(z,0) = E(0, -z/v)$, the record reversed — the front reaches a probe first (left of the record) but leads on the right in space. A $-z$ wave, $E(z,0) = E(0, z/v)$, is not mirrored; symmetric pulses hide this.

**World lines (slide 11).** Each corner of a profile $F(z - vt)$ rides a line $z = z_0 + vt$ in the $z$–$t$ plane (ink: "+ slope" for a $+z$ wave), reaching $z = 0$ at $t = -z_0/v$. For a $-z$ wave the lines slope the other way and the record keeps the profile's order. Every snapshot/record question in the unit ([[problems/a-pulse-on-the-move|Worked problem — A pulse on the move]], SP18 Exam 2 #4) is this.

**Monochromatic plane waves.** $A\cos(\omega t\mp\beta z)$: every parameter comes from the **phase** $\phi = \omega t\mp\beta z$.

| quantity | definition | unit |
|---|---|---|
| phase | $\phi = \omega t\mp\beta z$ | rad |
| angular frequency | $\omega = \partial\phi/\partial t$ | rad/s |
| frequency | $f = \omega/2\pi$ | Hz = 1/s |
| phase constant | $\beta = \lvert\partial\phi/\partial z\rvert$ | rad/m |
| wavelength | $\lambda = 2\pi/\beta$ | m |
| phase velocity | $v_p = \omega/\beta = \lambda f$ ($= c$ in free space) | m/s |
| intrinsic impedance | $\eta_0 = \lvert\mathbf{E}\rvert/\lvert\mathbf{H}\rvert$ | Ω |

$\phi$ = const gives $z = (\omega t - \text{const})/\beta$, moving at $\omega/\beta$; $\beta = \omega/v = \omega\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}$; $\lambda$ is the distance over which the phase changes by $2\pi$ at fixed $t$. At 100 MHz in free space, $\beta = 2\pi f/c = 2.10$ rad/m, $\lambda = 3.00$ m. [[concepts/phasors|Phasors]] ([[3-maxwell-and-waves/21-monochromatic-waves-and-phasor-notation|Lecture 21]]): with $\mathbf{E} = \text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}e^{j\omega t}\}$,
$$
\begin{gathered}
\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(\mathbf{r}) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}_0\,e^{-j\beta\hat u\cdot\mathbf{r}},\qquad \tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \frac1\eta\,\hat u\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}},\\[4pt]
\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^*\} = \frac{\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_0\rvert^2}{2\eta}\,\hat u .
\end{gathered}
$$
$e^{-j\beta\hat u\cdot\mathbf{r}}$: the delay $\hat u\cdot\mathbf{r}/v$ as a phase lag; in $e^{j(ax + by + cz)}$, $(a, b, c) = -\beta\hat u$. Complex components of $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_0$ give [[concepts/wave-polarization|polarization]]; $\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_0\rvert^2$ adds their powers. Opposite waves, $E_x = Af + Bg$, make [[concepts/standing-waves|standing waves]].

> - $g = u(t + x/50)$: same signs, toward $-x$ at **50 m/s**.

> [!trap]
> - "$x$-polarized" names $\mathbf{E}$, not the travel.
> - $-z$ reverses $\mathbf{H}$: use $\hat u\times\mathbf{E}/\eta$; relabel axes cyclically only ($x\to y\to z\to x$).
> - At $t = 0$ a $+z$ snapshot is the record mirrored ($-z$: not). Every probe reading zero: suspect the sign of $z/v$.
> - $E/H = \pm\eta$ for one wave only.
> - The coefficient of $z$ in $t - z/v$ is the slowness, not the speed; below $c$ means matter, not loss.
> - $e^{+j\beta z}$ goes toward $-z$: restore $e^{j\omega t}$ first. A sine's phasor has $-j$.
