description: "δ = 1/α, where the field falls to 1/e; in a good conductor δ = 1/√(πfμσ) = λ/2π, η = (1+j)/(σδ), H lags 45°. Seawater, submarines, metals."

> [!key] Definition
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \delta\equiv\frac1\alpha\ [\text{m}]:\\[4pt]
> \lvert\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\rvert\propto e^{-z/\delta},\qquad \langle S\rangle\propto e^{-2z/\delta}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> The notes: *penetration depth* in general, *skin depth* when small. **Imperfect dielectric** ($\sigma/\omega\epsilon\ll1$): $\delta\to\dfrac2\sigma\sqrt{\dfrac\epsilon\mu}$, frequency independent. **Good conductor** ($\sigma/\omega\epsilon\gg1$): $\delta\approx\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\omega\mu\sigma}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\pi f\mu\sigma}}$, growing as frequency falls.

> [!key] A plane wave in a good conductor
> $\sigma\gg\omega\epsilon$, $j\omega\epsilon$ dropped (derivation, other regimes: [[concepts/loss-tangent|Loss tangent]]):
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \delta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi f\mu\sigma}} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\omega\mu\sigma}},\\[4pt]
> \gamma = \frac{1+j}{\delta}\quad(\alpha = \beta = 1/\delta),\\[4pt]
> \eta = \frac{1+j}{\sigma\delta} = \sqrt{\frac{\omega\mu}{\sigma}}\;e^{j\pi/4},\\[4pt]
> \lambda = 2\pi\delta,\qquad v_p = \frac\omega\beta = \omega\delta .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = \hat xE_0e^{-z/\delta}e^{-jz/\delta}$, $E_0$ real:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \mathbf{E} = \hat x\,E_0e^{-z/\delta}\cos\Big(\omega t - \frac z\delta\Big),\\[4pt]
> \mathbf{H} = \hat y\,\frac{\sigma\delta E_0}{\sqrt2}\,e^{-z/\delta}\cos\Big(\omega t - \frac z\delta - \frac\pi4\Big),
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> and $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \hat z\,\dfrac{\sigma\delta E_0^2}{4}\,e^{-2z/\delta}$. Check: $\sqrt{\omega\mu/2\sigma} = \tfrac1\sigma\sqrt{\omega\mu\sigma/2} = 1/(\sigma\delta)$; the power is $\lvert E_0\rvert^2\cos\tau/(2\lvert\eta\rvert)$, $\cos\tau = 1/\sqrt2$, $\lvert\eta\rvert = \sqrt2/(\sigma\delta)$ ([[concepts/time-average-power|Time averages and average power]]).

**Physics.** Currents $\sigma\mathbf{E}$ turn the wave's energy into Joule heat; eddy currents oppose the changing $\mathbf{B}$ (Lenz, [[concepts/faradays-law|Faraday's law]]), so each layer shields those below. In a good conductor:

1. **Dead within a wavelength:** $\alpha = \beta = 1/\delta$, so the field falls by $e^{-2\pi}\approx1/535$, 54.6 dB, per wavelength; 86 % of the power is absorbed within $\delta$ ($1 - e^{-2}$), 99.75 % within $3\delta$.
2. **Slow and dispersive:** $v_p = \omega\delta = \sqrt{2\omega/\mu\sigma}\propto\sqrt f$. Seawater at 1 kHz: $5\times10^{4}$ m/s (Example 3) against $c/9 = 3.3\times10^{7}$ m/s lossless. Copper at 1 MHz: $\lambda = 0.42$ mm, $v_p = 415$ m/s.
3. **H lags E by 45°**, an eighth of a period, and is large: $1/\lvert\eta\rvert$ is 22.5 (A/m) per (V/m) in seawater at 1 kHz, $1/377$ in air. Electric over magnetic time-average energy density: $\epsilon\lvert\eta\rvert^2/\mu = 1/\sqrt{1+p^2}\approx\omega\epsilon/\sigma$, $1.1\times10^{-6}$ for seawater at 1 kHz (1 if lossless): almost all magnetic.
4. **Perfect conductor as the limit:** $\delta\to0$, $\eta\to0$, no field inside; the $\delta$-thick current layer becomes a surface current carrying the jump in $\mathbf{H}$ ([[concepts/boundary-conditions|Boundary conditions at an interface]]; derived on [[concepts/standing-waves|Standing waves]]).

$\delta\propto1/\sqrt{f\mu\sigma}$: four times the frequency halves it; $\mu_r\gg1$ shrinks it by $1/\sqrt{\mu_r}$.

> [!intuition] Why the good-conductor η looks like a resistance
> $\text{Re}\{\eta\} = 1/(\sigma\delta)$ is the edge-to-edge DC resistance of a square slab one skin depth thick ($R = l/(\sigma A)$, $A = l\delta$): the **surface resistance** $R_s$ of [[concepts/standing-waves|Standing waves]]. The induced current is a resistive sheet absorbing $\tfrac12\text{Re}\{\eta\}\lvert\tilde H_0\rvert^2 = \lvert\tilde H_0\rvert^2/(2\sigma\delta)$ per area, its $\tfrac12RI^2$.

**The attenuation saturates.** Above $10f_c$, $\alpha = (\sigma/2)\sqrt{\mu/\epsilon}$ stops growing with frequency (per wavelength it even falls); below $f_c/10$, $\alpha = \sqrt{\pi f\mu\sigma}$ grows as $\sqrt f$: lower frequencies always penetrate further. Seawater's plateau: 84 Np/m, $\delta = 1.2$ cm. (Rao Problem 5.26: good-conductor $\alpha$ = plateau value times $\sqrt{2\omega\epsilon/\sigma}$.)

|---|---|---|---|
| 60 Hz | 8.53 mm | 11.0 mm | 2.86 µΩ |
| 1 kHz | 2.09 mm | 2.69 mm | 11.7 µΩ |
| 1 MHz | 66.1 µm | 85.1 µm | 0.369 mΩ |
| 100 MHz | 6.61 µm | 8.51 µm | 3.69 mΩ |
| 1 GHz | 2.09 µm | 2.69 µm | 11.7 mΩ |

- Closed form: copper $0.0661/\sqrt f$ m (Rao's $0.066/\sqrt f$), aluminium $0.0851/\sqrt f$ m ($\sqrt{5.8/3.5} = 1.29$ times copper's), seawater $252/\sqrt f$ m (32.5 m at 60 Hz; table below) while $\sigma/\omega\epsilon>10$, for seawater below about 90 MHz (at 100 MHz, $\sigma/\omega\epsilon<10$, the formula's 2.5 cm is 5 % short of 2.7 cm exact); copper $\lvert\eta\rvert = \sqrt{\omega\mu_0/\sigma} = 3.69\times10^{-7}\sqrt f\ \Omega$ (Rao).

> [!example] Example 1 (notes) — a 1 GHz wave in an imperfect dielectric
> $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = \hat y\,2e^{-\alpha z}e^{-j\beta z}$ V/m toward $+z$; $\epsilon = 4\epsilon_0$, $\mu = \mu_0$, $\sigma = 10^{-3}$ S/m, $\omega = 2\pi\times10^{9}$ rad/s. Find $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}$ and $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle$ at $z = 0$ and 10 m.
>
> **Classify.** $\sigma/\omega\epsilon = 10^{-3}\cdot36\pi\times10^{9}/(2\pi\times10^{9}\cdot4) = 4.5\times10^{-3}\ll1$, so $\tau\approx\sigma/2\omega\epsilon = 2.25\times10^{-3}$ rad $= 0.13^\circ$, $\lvert\eta\rvert\approx\sqrt{\mu_0/4\epsilon_0} = \eta_0/2\approx60\pi = 188\ \Omega$, $\alpha\approx(\sigma/2)\lvert\eta\rvert = 30\pi\times10^{-3} = 0.094$ Np/m, $\beta\approx\omega\sqrt{\mu_0\cdot4\epsilon_0} = 41.9$ rad/m, $\lambda = 15$ cm.
>
> **Magnetic field.** Divide by $\eta$; $\hat z\times\hat y = -\hat x$ puts $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}$ along $+\hat z$:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \tilde{\mathbf{H}} = -\hat x\,\frac{2}{\eta}\,e^{-\alpha z}e^{-j\beta z}\\[4pt]
> = -\hat x\,\frac{2}{\lvert\eta\rvert}\,e^{-\alpha z}e^{-j\beta z}e^{-j\tau}\ \ \text{A/m}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> Time domain ($E_0$ real):
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \mathbf{E} = \hat y\,2e^{-\alpha z}\cos(\omega t - \beta z)\ \text{V/m},\\[4pt]
> \mathbf{H} = -\hat x\,\frac{2}{\lvert\eta\rvert}e^{-\alpha z}\cos(\omega t - \beta z - \tau)\ \text{A/m},
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> $2/\lvert\eta\rvert = 10.6$ mA/m; $\mathbf{H}$ lags 0.13°.
>
> **Power.**
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^*\}\\[4pt]
> = \tfrac12\text{Re}\Big\{\hat y\times(-\hat x)\,\frac{4}{\lvert\eta\rvert}e^{-2\alpha z}e^{+j\tau}\Big\}\\[4pt]
> = \hat z\,\frac{2}{\lvert\eta\rvert}\cos\tau\,e^{-2\alpha z}\ \ \text{W/m}^2 .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> $z = 0$: $2/(60\pi) = 1/(30\pi) = 10.6$ mW/m². $z = 10$ m: $e^{-2\times0.094\times10} = e^{-0.6\pi} = 0.15$, so $0.15/(30\pi)\approx1.6$ mW/m², 15 % left; $8.686\,\alpha = 0.82$ dB/m, 8.2 dB over 10 m; $\delta = 1/\alpha = 10.6$ m. Energy check: $\tfrac12\sigma\lvert E_0\rvert^2 = 2\alpha\langle S_z\rangle$ ([[concepts/time-average-power|Time averages and average power]]).

> [!example] Example 2 (notes) — the same wave at 1 kHz in seawater
> Example 1 with $\omega = 2\pi\times10^{3}$ rad/s, $\sigma = 4$ S/m. The notes keep $\epsilon = 4\epsilon_0$ ($\sigma/\omega\epsilon = 1.8\times10^{7}$; seawater's $81\epsilon_0$: $8.9\times10^{5}$): a **good conductor** either way, $\epsilon$ drops out:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \tau = \frac\pi4,\\[4pt]
> \lvert\eta\rvert = \sqrt{\frac{\omega\mu_0}{\sigma}} = \sqrt{\frac{2\pi\times10^{3}\cdot4\pi\times10^{-7}}{4}}\\[4pt]
> = \frac{\pi\sqrt2}{100} = 0.0444\ \Omega,\\[4pt]
> \alpha = \sqrt{\pi f\mu_0\sigma} = \frac{\pi}{25} = 0.126\ \text{Np/m}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = -\hat x\,(2/\lvert\eta\rvert)\,e^{-j\pi/4}e^{-\alpha z}e^{-j\beta z}$: 45 A/m, 4,240 times Example 1's for the same 2 V/m, so
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \hat z\,\frac{2}{\lvert\eta\rvert}\cos\frac\pi4\,e^{-2\alpha z}\\[4pt]
> = \hat z\,\frac{100}{\pi}\,e^{-2\alpha z}\ \text{W/m}^2,
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> 31.8 W/m² at $z = 0$ (3,000 times Example 1's 10.6 mW/m²), $31.8\times e^{-0.8\pi} = 31.8\times0.081 = 2.6$ W/m² at 10 m: 8 % left, 1.09 dB/m.

> [!example] Example 3 (notes) — the penetration depth of seawater at 1 kHz
> $\sigma = 4$ S/m, $\epsilon = 81\epsilon_0$, $\mu_0$, 1 kHz: $\sigma/\omega\epsilon = 72\times10^{9}/(81\times10^{3}) = 8.9\times10^{5}\gg1$, so
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \delta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi\cdot10^{3}\cdot4\pi\times10^{-7}\cdot4}}\\[4pt]
> = \frac{1}{\sqrt{16\pi^2\times10^{-4}}} = \frac{100}{4\pi} = \frac{25}{\pi} = 7.96\ \text{m}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> In the water $\lambda = 2\pi\delta = 50$ m, $v_p = \lambda f = 5\times10^{4}$ m/s.

The price (the notes) is **bandwidth**: a carrier at $f$ carries a small fraction of $f$, so deep links are slow: VLF (3–30 kHz) tens of metres at roughly 300 bit/s, ELF (US Navy 76 Hz, shut down 2004; Russia 82 Hz) hundreds of metres at a few characters per minute (Wikipedia; cf. the table's 19 m and 330 m). The second price is the **antenna**: against $\lambda_0$ of 3,900 km and 12.5 km any buildable antenna radiates poorly: tens of kilometres of wire, huge power, one-way. (Rao: in the water $\lambda = 2\pi\delta$, 50 m at 1 kHz; immersed antennas can be short.)

**Skin depth in metals.** Metals are good conductors at every course frequency.

- **Skin effect in wires.** Current crowds into a layer about $\delta$ thick: a wire of radius $a\gg\delta$ acts as a tube of wall $\delta$, resistance up about $a/2\delta$ (7.6 for 1 mm radius copper at 1 MHz). At 60 Hz $\delta = 8.5$ mm exceeds house wiring, which conducts throughout.
- **Shielding.** Absorption through thickness $d$: $e^{-d/\delta}$, $8.686\,d/\delta$ dB, plus reflection ([[concepts/reflection-and-transmission|Reflection and transmission coefficients]]). 1 mm of aluminium is $0.09\,\delta$ at 60 Hz (nearly transparent) but $11.8\,\delta$ (102 dB) at 1 MHz: power-frequency magnetic fields are hard to shield, radio easy. At a microwave oven's 2.45 GHz aluminium's $\delta = 1.7$ µm: a sub-millimetre wall is hundreds of skin depths.
- **Tiny $\lvert\eta\rvert$.** Copper's 11.7 mΩ at 1 GHz, $3\times10^{-5}$ of $\eta_0$, is a near short: near-total reflection ([[concepts/reflection-and-transmission|Reflection and transmission coefficients]]).

> [!trap]
> - $\delta$ is for the **field**: the power is down to $1/e$ at $\delta/2$, to 14 % at $\delta$.
> - $1/\sqrt{\pi f\mu\sigma}$ needs $\sigma/\omega\epsilon\gg1$ (seawater at 1 GHz, 0.89: 1.3 cm exact, 0.8 cm by formula); in an imperfect dielectric $1/\alpha\approx(2/\sigma)\sqrt{\epsilon/\mu}$ does not shrink as $1/\sqrt f$.
> - $\pi f$ with $f$, or $\sqrt{2/\omega\mu\sigma}$ with $\omega$; mixing them costs $\sqrt{2\pi}$.
> - **"Copper is a good conductor at all frequencies":** constant $\sigma$ puts copper's $\sigma/\omega\epsilon_0 = 1$ at $f_c = 1.0\times10^{18}$ Hz (X-rays); $\sigma/\omega\epsilon\to0$ as $\omega\to\infty$, so every medium ends up a dielectric. Constant $\sigma$ fails earlier: the Drude $\sigma_{\text{DC}}/(1 + j\omega/\nu)$ ([[concepts/conductivity-and-susceptibility-models|Lorentz–Drude models of σ and χe]]; $\nu\approx4\times10^{13}$ s⁻¹ for copper) changes in the infrared; X-rays pass through foil. Safe: every radio and microwave frequency.

> [!exam] On exams
> **Good-conductor concepts** (SP17 #1a, #2(i); SP18 #1(i)): $\alpha$ vs $\beta$, 45° phase, decay within a wavelength, dispersive $v_p$, decaying power flow, "good at all frequencies". **Classify, then compute** (Summer 2018, 2019 HE3 #3b: seawater at 1 kHz; recipe on [[concepts/loss-tangent|Loss tangent]]). **Power against depth in water** (FA23 #6): e.g. $e^{-0.2z}e^{-j0.2z}$ ($\alpha = \beta$: good conductor); $8.686\,\alpha$ dB/m. Calculator-free: $\pi f\mu_0\sigma = 4\pi^2\times10^{-7}f\sigma$, so $\delta$ needs $f\sigma\times10^{-7}$ to be a perfect square ($f\sigma = 4\times10^{3}$ for seawater).
