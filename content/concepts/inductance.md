> [!key] Inductance
> $$
> \Psi = LI,\qquad L = \frac{\Psi}{I}\ \ge0\quad[\text{H} = \text{Wb/A}].
> $$
> $$
> \mathcal{E} = -L\frac{dI}{dt}\ (\text{rise along }I),\qquad V = L\frac{dI}{dt}\ (\text{drop along }I).
> $$
> $\Psi$: flux through one turn from the circuit's own $I$. $L$ depends on geometry and permeability only — twin of $C = Q/V$. For $N$ turns each linking the same single-turn flux $\Psi$, the $N$ emfs add: $\mathcal{E} = -N\,d\Psi/dt\equiv-L\,dI/dt$, so $L = N\Psi/I$ (other books: flux *linkage* $N\Psi$ over $I$). *Mutual* inductance, $M = \Psi_{\text{coil 1 due to }I_2}/I_2$, also in henries, is the transformer's parameter; the course names it and moves on (ECE 330 takes it up). Per unit length: $\mathcal{L} = L/\ell$ [H/m] ([[concepts/transmission-line-parameters|Transmission-line parameters 𝓛, 𝓒, 𝓖]]).

**Physics.** $\mathbf{B}\propto I$ (Biot–Savart is linear), so a coil's flux through its own surface is $\propto I$: the magnetic twin of [[concepts/capacitance|Capacitance]], where linear Coulomb made $Q\propto V$. When $I$ changes, Faraday's law on the coil's own loop gives a **self-emf**

$$
\mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Psi}{dt} = -L\frac{dI}{dt},
$$

a voltage *rise* around the coil in the direction of the current (the **back-emf**, as opposed to emfs from externally produced flux), opposing any change in $I$ (Lenz). Circuit courses state it from the other side, **the inductor as a circuit element**:

$$
V = L\frac{dI}{dt}
$$

is the voltage **drop** across the inductor in the direction of $I$ (passive sign convention); $-L\,dI/dt$ is the equal and opposite *rise* Faraday supplies. For a resistive coil with no other source, KVL reads $RI = -L\,dI/dt$: rise matches drop. (A typed slide has the wrong sign; the circuit symbol's $+$/$-$ is right — [[0-toolkit/04-errata-in-the-course-materials|errata page]].)

**The RL decay.** $RI = -L\,dI/dt$ gives $I(t) = I(0)e^{-t/\tau}$, $\tau = L/R$; the self-emf obeys the same equation, $R\mathcal{E} = -L\,d\mathcal{E}/dt$.

> [!example] Example 1 (course notes, Lecture 15) — a coil discharging into itself
> An $N$-turn coil, $R = 1\ \Omega$, $L = 1\ \mu$H, carries 3 A at $t = 0$ and is then shorted. $RI = -L\,dI/dt \Rightarrow I(t) = 3e^{-10^6t}$ A: gone in microseconds; $N$ never enters once $L$ is given.

> [!intuition] Capacitor ↔ inductor
> $\tau = RC$ for the leaky capacitor ([[1-electrostatics/10-capacitance-and-conductance#6-conductance-the-leaky-capacitor|Lecture 10 §6]]) ↔ $\tau = L/R$. A large $C$ holds its voltage while slowly discharging — a slowly varying **voltage source**; a large $L$ holds its *current* — a slowly varying **current source**, maintaining whatever rise $-L\,dI/dt$ the circuit requires. (Interrupt a big inductor's current abruptly and $dI/dt\to\infty$: the spark across the switch is the inductor insisting.)

**When $I = \mathcal{E}/R$ was allowed.** In general a resistive loop's current solves an ODE built from its total emf. [[concepts/faradays-law|Lecture 14]]'s algebraic $I = \mathcal{E}/R$ is the special case where the self-emf $-L\,dI/dt$ is negligible next to the external emf — true for those small loops and modest rates (60 Hz generator: $L\,dI/dt\sim$ millivolts against 118 V). A lumped $L$ also needs the coil small compared with $\lambda = c/f$ (the MQS condition of [[concepts/amperes-law|Ampère's law]]).

> [!recipe] Inductance, five steps (the Ampère chain)
> Assume $I$ → $\mathbf{H}$ by [[concepts/amperes-law|Ampère's law]] (or Biot–Savart) → $\mathbf{B} = \mu\mathbf{H}$ → $\Psi = \int_S\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$ through the surface bounded by the current path (times $N$ for $N$ turns) → $L = N\Psi/I$. The assumed $I$ cancels, as $Q$ did for capacitance.

**Long solenoid.** $n$ turns per metre, cross-section $A$, length $\ell\gg$ radius (minor edge effects), $N = n\ell$ turns. From [[concepts/solenoids-and-toroids|Solenoids and toroids]], $\mathbf{B} = \mu_0nI\hat z$ inside, so each turn links $\Psi = \mu_0nIA$ and

$$
L = \frac{N\Psi}{I} = \frac{n\ell\cdot\mu_0nIA}{I} = n^2\mu_0A\ell = N^2\mu_0\frac{A}{\ell}.
$$

Quadratic in winding density: doubling the turns doubles the field *and* the number of turns linking it. ($n$ is per metre; a slide swaps $N$ and $n$.)

**Results.**

| structure | $L$ | with |
|---|---|---|
| long solenoid, $n$ turns/m, area $A$, length $\ell$, $N = n\ell$ | $n^2\mu_0A\ell = N^2\mu_0A/\ell$ | $B = \mu_0nI$ inside |
| shorted coax, radii $a<b$, length $\ell$ | $\dfrac{\mu\ell}{2\pi}\ln\dfrac ba$, $\ \mathcal{L} = \dfrac{\mu}{2\pi}\ln\dfrac ba$ | $B_\phi = \mu I/2\pi r$; flux through the $r$–$z$ rectangle; external inductance only |
| shorted parallel plates, width $W$, gap $d$ | $\mathcal{L} = \mu\,d/W$ | $H = I/W$ inside |
| toroid, $N$ turns, core area $A$, mean radius $r$ | $\mu N^2A/(2\pi r)$ | $H = NI/2\pi r$ in the core (site's own) |

Coax and plates are derived on [[concepts/transmission-line-parameters|Transmission-line parameters 𝓛, 𝓒, 𝓖]], where $\mathcal{L}\mathcal{C} = \mu\epsilon$ follows.

**With a magnetic core, $L\propto\mu$** ([[3-maxwell-and-waves/17-magnetization-and-maxwells-equations-in-matter|Lecture 17]]). The free current fixes $\mathbf{H}$, so the flux $\int\mu\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$ scales with $\mu$: a cored solenoid has $L = n^2\mu A\ell$, $\mu_r$ times the air value (12.6 μH → 1.26 mH for $n = 1000$/m, $A = 1$ cm², $\ell = 10$ cm, $\mu_r = 100$). Such measurements are the notes' evidence that $\mathbf{M}\propto\mathbf{H}$ in most materials. Iron's $L$ depends on the current, collapsing toward the air value at saturation.

> [!trap]
> - $N$ enters twice (field $\propto nI$, linkage $N\Psi$), hence $N^2$; not a third time.
> - Coax flux surface: the rectangle between the conductors, not a circle.
> - $-L\,dI/dt$ is a rise, $L\,dI/dt$ a drop (passive convention), both along $I$; hence the shorted coil's minus sign.
> - $\mathbf{B} = \mu\mathbf{H}$ with the *medium's* $\mu$; $\mathbf{H}$ itself does not change with the core.

> [!exam] On exams
> "Find the inductance (per unit length) of …" is the magnetic twin of Exam 1 problem 4, graded on the same chain: $\mathbf{H}$ from Ampère with the right loop, $\mathbf{B} = \mu\mathbf{H}$ with the *medium's* $\mu$, flux over the surface bounded by the current path (for a coax, the $r$–$z$ rectangle, not a circle), units H or H/m, and $N$ counted once in the field and again in the turns linked. Sign questions want $V = L\,dI/dt$ as a drop in the direction of $I$; RL questions want $\tau = L/R$ and the exponential.
