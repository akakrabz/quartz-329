> [!key] Stokes' theorem
> $$
> \oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \int_S (\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\cdot d\mathbf{S}\qquad (C = \partial S,\ \text{right-hand rule})
> $$
> Circulation around the boundary = total curl through the surface, for any surface bounded by $C$. Right-hand rule: fingers curl along the travel ($d\mathbf{l}$), thumb along $d\mathbf{S}$.

**Physics.** Tile $S$ with tiny loops: each interior edge is traversed both ways and cancels; only the boundary $C$ survives. Each tiny loop's circulation is (curl)·(area) — the *definition* of [[concepts/curl|curl]] as circulation per unit area. It is the surface rung of the ladder that starts with the fundamental theorem of calculus and the gradient theorem and ends with the [[concepts/divergence-theorem|Divergence theorem]] (the ladder is spelled out there).

**What it is for.**
- Converts the curl equations between forms: $\oint_C\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_S\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$ for every $S$ ⟺ $\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t$; likewise Ampère ([[concepts/maxwells-equations|Maxwell's equations]]).
- Proves the equivalences for [[concepts/conservative-field|conservative fields]]: $\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = 0$ everywhere ⟹ $\oint_C\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 0$ for every loop ⟹ path independence ⟹ a potential. The difference between two paths from $a$ to $b$ is the flux of the curl through the surface they enclose.
- Interprets an EMF: $\oint_C\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$ around a loop equals the total curl threading it ([[concepts/electromotive-force|Electromotive force (emf) and path-dependent voltage]]).

**Examples.**

> [!example] A curl trap, checked with Stokes
> $\mathbf{E} = E_0(2xy\hat{x}+\kappa x^2\hat{y})$ has $\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = 2(\kappa-1)E_0\,x\,\hat{z}$. Around the unit square in the $xy$-plane (counter-clockwise), Stokes predicts $\oint\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 2(\kappa-1)E_0x\,dx\,dy = (\kappa-1)E_0$; direct integration of the four legs gives the same. For $\kappa = 1$ the field is conservative and the loop integral vanishes.

Lecture 5's two-path example ($\mathbf{E} = y\hat{x} - x\hat{y}$, paths differing by 2 = flux of $\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -2\hat{z}$ through the unit square, clockwise) is worked on [[concepts/conservative-field|Conservative (curl-free) fields and the potential]].

> [!trap]
> - Orientation: reverse the direction of travel around $C$ and $d\mathbf{S}$ flips with it; the two sides change sign together.
> - Any surface spanning $C$ works — pick the flat one.

> [!exam] On exams
> A loop integral asked for a field whose curl you know: compute (curl)·(area) instead of four legs — or, if the curl is zero, answer 0 at once (FA26 problem 1d by path independence).
