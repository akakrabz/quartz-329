> [!key] The three classes
> | | diamagnetic | paramagnetic | ferromagnetic |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | $\chi_m$ | about $-10^{-5}$ | about $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-3}$ | $10^2$ to $10^6$, not a constant |
> | $\mu_r$ | slightly below 1 | slightly above 1 | $\gg1$ |
> | mechanism | orbital moments *induced* (Lenz) | *permanent* moments partly aligned | permanent moments aligned *spontaneously*, in domains |
> | $\mathbf{M}$ at $\mathbf{H} = 0$? | no | no | yes — permanent magnets |
> | examples (slides) | copper, bismuth, water, silver | aluminum, platinum, liquid oxygen, palladium | iron, nickel, cobalt, alloys |
>
> $\mathbf{M} = \chi_m\mathbf{H}$, $\mu_r = 1+\chi_m$ ([[concepts/permeability|Permeability, susceptibility, and B = μH]]). Dia- and paramagnets are within a part in a thousand of vacuum: $\mu = \mu_0$ for most media later. Mnemonic: **Para**magnetic ↔ **Pos**itive.

**Physics.** **Diamagnetism** is in every atom: a switched-on field induces an emf around each orbit ([[concepts/faradays-law|Faraday's law]], [[2-magnetostatics/14-faradays-law-and-induced-emf|Lecture 14]]), and by Lenz the induced moment opposes it (the notes' "electron clouds spin up" is this orbital change, not spin). Water, wood, glass, copper, silver, bismuth show only this: weakly pushed out of strong fields.

**Paramagnetism**: unpaired spins in unfilled inner shells (Pauli pairs those in filled ones) align against thermal agitation — lopsidedly: at 1 T, $\mu_BB\approx9\times10^{-24}$ J is about $1/450$ of room-temperature $k_BT\approx4\times10^{-21}$ J, so only a small excess aligns. Weak attraction to magnets.

**Ferromagnetism** is quantum: conduction electrons, constrained by exclusion, pass a spin orientation from atom to atom ("exchange"), so spins align spontaneously into saturated domains pointing different ways ($\mathbf{M}\approx0$ unmagnetized). Applied $\mathbf{H}$ grows aligned domains: modest $\mathbf{H}$, enormous $\mathbf{M}$, and $\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{H}+\mu_0\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{H})$ depends on history.

Now lower H. B does not retrace its path: at H = 0 there is still a remanent flux density B<sub>r</sub> — the sample has become a permanent magnet — and a reverse field −H<sub>c</sub> (the coercivity) is needed to bring B to zero. That memory is **hysteresis**: back at $H = 0$ after the virgin curve to saturation, $B$ keeps the **remanence** $B_r$; the **coercivity** $-H_c$ zeroes it. $\mathbf{B}\neq0$ at $\mathbf{H} = 0$ is a **permanent magnet** (notes, p. 12). Loop area = heat per m³ per cycle: *soft* (small $H_c$: transformer steel, ferrites) for AC, *hard* (large $B_r$, $H_c$) for magnets. Saturation: iron, $N\approx8.5\times10^{28}$ atoms/m³ at about $2.2\,\mu_B$ each, all aligned, gives $\mu_0M\approx2.2$ T ([[concepts/magnetization|Magnetization M and magnetization current]]) — why iron cores saturate near 2 T, and why a linear "$\mu_r = 5000$" coil field of 12.6 T is impossible ([[concepts/permeability|Permeability, susceptibility, and B = μH]]).

Notes: a first-principles $\chi_m$ needs quantum mechanics; like $\chi_e$ ([[concepts/conductivity-and-susceptibility-models|Lorentz–Drude models of σ and χe]], [[1-electrostatics/11-lorentz-drude-models-for-conductivity-and-susceptibility|Lecture 11]]) it depends on frequency, with loss and resonances that matter for magnetic imaging.

- A $\mu_0M\approx1.3$ T permanent magnet is equivalent to a solenoid of 1000 turns/m carrying 1000 A ([[concepts/magnetization|Magnetization M and magnetization current]]).
- Iron pole pieces: with $\mu_r = 5000$, $B$-lines leave the iron almost normally (85° inside → 0.13° outside; [[concepts/boundary-conditions|Boundary conditions at an interface]]) — motors and loudspeakers steer flux with iron.

> [!trap] Classification slips
> - Domains: ferromagnets only; paramagnet atoms align individually, slightly.
> - Every atom is diamagnetic; para/ferro means permanent moments win (notes, footnote 4).
> - A ferromagnet's "$\mu_r = 5000$" is a small-field slope; near saturation $B$ grows only as $\mu_0H$.
> - $\chi_e>0$ always (Unit 1); $\chi_m$ can be negative.

> [!exam] On exams
> Classify from $\chi_m$ or $\mu_r$ (sign and size); say which class has domains, hysteresis, $\chi_m<0$, $\mathbf{M}$ at $\mathbf{H} = 0$; read remanence and coercivity off a loop; know that tabulated $\mu_r$ is a small-field value and that iron saturates near 2 T.
