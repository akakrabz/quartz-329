> [!key] The phasor of a sinusoid
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> a(t) = A\cos(\omega t + \theta) = \text{Re}\{\tilde A\,e^{j\omega t}\},\\[4pt]
> \tilde A = A\,e^{j\theta}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> $\lvert\tilde A\rvert = A$ (**peak**, not rms), $\angle\tilde A = \theta$, cosine reference, no $t$. Euler: $\tilde Ae^{j\omega t} = A\cos(\omega t + \theta) + jA\sin(\omega t + \theta)$. Vector phasors $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = \tilde E_x\hat x + \tilde E_y\hat y + \tilde E_z\hat z$ (complex components, real unit vectors):
> $$
> \mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r}, t) = \text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(\mathbf{r})\,e^{j\omega t}\}.
> $$
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \frac{da}{dt}\ \leftrightarrow\ j\omega\tilde A,\qquad \int a\,dt\ \leftrightarrow\ \frac{\tilde A}{j\omega},\\[4pt]
> a(t-\tau)\ \leftrightarrow\ \tilde Ae^{-j\omega\tau}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
>
> **Plane-wave phasors, and the Ẽ ↔ H̃ rule (one wave, lossless medium):**
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \text{toward }{+z}:\ \ \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(z) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}_0\,e^{-j\beta z},\\[4pt]
> \text{toward }{-z}:\ \ \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(z) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}_0\,e^{+j\beta z},\\[4pt]
> \text{toward }\hat u:\ \ \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(\mathbf{r}) = \tilde{\mathbf{E}}_0\,e^{-j\beta\,\hat u\cdot\mathbf{r}},\quad \tilde{\mathbf{E}}_0\perp\hat u .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \frac1\eta\,\hat u\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}},\qquad \tilde{\mathbf{E}} = \eta\,\tilde{\mathbf{H}}\times\hat u,\\[4pt]
> \tilde{\mathbf{S}}\equiv\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^* = \frac{\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\rvert^2}{\eta}\,\hat u .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> Uniform $\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\rvert$; phase *falls* along the travel. $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_0$ may be complex ([[concepts/wave-polarization|circular polarization]]). The real-field rule $\mathbf{H} = \tfrac1\eta\hat u\times\mathbf{E}$ (divide by $\eta$, rotate so $\tilde{\mathbf{S}}$ points along the travel) carries over since real $\eta$, $\hat u$ commute with Re; complex factors ride along.

**Physics.** A linear system driven at $\omega$ settles at $\omega$: amplitude plus phase, one complex number ($e^{j\omega t}$ unwritten). $\tilde Ae^{j\omega t}$ is an arrow of length $A$ turning counterclockwise; $a(t)$ is its real-axis shadow, $\tilde A$ the arrow at $t = 0$. $\times(-j)$ turns it $-90^\circ$, so $\sin(\omega t + \theta) = \cos(\omega t + \theta - \pi/2)$ has phasor $-jAe^{j\theta}$, a quarter-period lag: $\text{Re}\{-je^{j\omega t}\} = \text{Re}\{-j\cos\omega t + \sin\omega t\} = \sin\omega t$. The rules are linear ($a + b\to\tilde A + \tilde B$, $ka\to k\tilde A$, $k$ real) and unique (compare $t = 0$, $\omega t = \pi/2$), so sinusoid equations become phasor equations; Re commutes with $d/dt$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dt}\text{Re}\{\tilde Ae^{j\omega t}\} &= \text{Re}\Big\{\tilde A\,\frac{d}{dt}e^{j\omega t}\Big\}\\
&= \text{Re}\big\{(j\omega\tilde A)\,e^{j\omega t}\big\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$j\omega$: stretch, turn a quarter *forward* (derivatives peak a quarter period early); $\tfrac{d}{dt}\cos\omega t = -\omega\sin\omega t = \text{Re}\{j\omega e^{j\omega t}\}$. A delay is a phase lag $\omega\tau$: toward $+z$, $\mathbf{E}(z, t) = \mathbf{E}(0, t - z/v_p)$, factor $e^{-j\omega z/v_p} = e^{-j\beta z}$; toward $-z$, $e^{+j\beta z}$. So came ECE 210's $\tilde I = j\omega C\tilde V$ and, from $j\omega m\tilde{\mathbf{v}} = q\tilde{\mathbf{E}} - m\nu\tilde{\mathbf{v}}$, the Drude $\sigma(\omega) = Nq^2/\big(m(\nu + j\omega)\big)$ ([[concepts/conductivity-and-susceptibility-models|Lorentz–Drude models of σ and χe]]). **No phasor for products:** $\cos^2\omega t = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12\cos2\omega t$, so $\text{Re}\{\tilde A\tilde Be^{j\omega t}\}\neq a(t)b(t)$; power needs $\langle ab\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde A\tilde B^*\}$ ([[concepts/time-average-power|Time averages and average power]]). One frequency only; a pulse is a Fourier sum of phasor problems.

**The complex-number moves this needs.**

- $x + jy = re^{j\phi}$, $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$, $\phi$ in the point's own quadrant: $-4 + 3j$ is at $143.1^\circ$, not $\tan^{-1}(3/(-4)) = -36.9^\circ$.
- $j = e^{j\pi/2}$ turns $+90^\circ$, $-j = 1/j = e^{-j\pi/2}$ turns $-90^\circ$, $-1 = e^{j\pi}$ half a turn.
- $(re^{j\phi})^* = re^{-j\phi}$, $zz^* = \lvert z\rvert^2$, $\text{Re}\{z\} = \tfrac12(z + z^*)$, $(r_1e^{j\phi_1})(r_2e^{j\phi_2}) = r_1r_2e^{j(\phi_1 + \phi_2)}$.

**Time function → phasor.**

| time function | phasor |
|---|---|
| $A\cos\omega t$ | $A$ |
| $A\sin\omega t$ | $-jA$ |
| $-A\cos\omega t$ | $-A$ ($e^{j\pi} = -1$) |
| $-A\sin\omega t$ | $+jA$ ($(-1)(-j) = j$) |
| $A\cos(\omega t + \theta)$ | $Ae^{j\theta}$ |
| $A\sin(\omega t + \theta)$ | $-jAe^{j\theta} = Ae^{j(\theta - \pi/2)}$ |
| $A\cos(\omega t - \beta z)$ | $Ae^{-j\beta z}$ |
| $A\sin(\omega t + \beta y)$ | $-jAe^{j\beta y}$ |

**Back to the time domain:** $a(t) = \lvert\tilde A\rvert\cos(\omega t + \angle\tilde A)$; multiply by $e^{j\omega t}$ *before* taking Re. $(4 - 3j)\,e^{-j\beta z}\hat x$ V/m, $4 - 3j = 5e^{-j36.9^\circ}$: $5\cos(\omega t - \beta z - 36.9^\circ)\,\hat x$ V/m $= 4\cos(\omega t - \beta z) + 3\sin(\omega t - \beta z)$. The notes' example, $e^{\mp j\beta z}$ being the phase at each position:
$$
\begin{gathered}
\mathbf{E} = E_0\cos(\omega t\mp\beta z)\,\hat x\ \ [\text{V/m}]\\[2pt]
\updownarrow\\[2pt]
\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = E_0e^{\mp j\beta z}\,\hat x\ \ [\text{V/m}],
\end{gathered}
$$

**The direction-of-travel trap.** $e^{+j\beta z}e^{j\omega t} = e^{j(\omega t + \beta z)}$: same signs, toward $-z$. **A minus sign in the exponent means travel toward plus**, toward where the phase lags.

**Any direction, and the exam template.** Wave vector $\beta\hat u$: factor $e^{-j(\beta_xx + \beta_yy + \beta_zz)}$ (Rao §8.1). The exams' template $(A\hat x + B\hat y + C\hat z)\,e^{j(ax + by + cz)}$ has a plus sign:
$$
(a, b, c) = -\beta\,\hat u,\qquad a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = \beta^2:
$$
- Toward $-\hat z$, 450 MHz, non-magnetic, $\epsilon_r = 4$: $\beta = 2\pi f\sqrt{\epsilon_r}/c = 6\pi$ rad/m, $e^{+j6\pi z}$, $(a, b, c) = (0, 0, +6\pi)$ rad/m.
- Toward $(\hat x + \hat y)/\sqrt2$, $\beta = 2\pi$ rad/m: $e^{-j\sqrt2\pi(x + y)}$, $a = b = -\sqrt2\pi\approx-4.44$ rad/m, $c = 0$; amplitude $\perp\hat u$: $\hat z$, $\hat x - \hat y$ or a complex mix.

**Why the conjugate in $\tilde{\mathbf{S}}$:** it cancels the shared $e^{-j\beta\hat u\cdot\mathbf{r}}$. With $\mathbf{a}\times(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}) = \mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{c}) - \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})$, $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\cdot\hat u = 0$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^* &= \frac1\eta\,\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\big(\hat u\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^*\big)\\
&= \frac1\eta\Big[\hat u\,\big(\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^*\big) - \tilde{\mathbf{E}}^*\big(\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\cdot\hat u\big)\Big] = \frac{\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\rvert^2}{\eta}\,\hat u .
\end{aligned}
$$
Without it, $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = (\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{E}}/\eta)\,\hat u$ carries $e^{-2j\beta z}$, flipping sign every quarter wavelength.

> [!derivation]- Why "divide by η" works, from Faraday's law in phasor form
> The planar curl equations ([[concepts/wave-equation|Wave equation]]) with $\partial/\partial t\to j\omega$:
> $$
> \frac{d\tilde E_x}{dz} = -j\omega\mu\,\tilde H_y,\qquad -\frac{d\tilde H_y}{dz} = j\omega\epsilon\,\tilde E_x .
> $$
> $\tilde E_x = E_0e^{\mp j\beta z}$ gives $d/dz\to\mp j\beta$:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \tilde H_y = \pm\frac{\beta}{\omega\mu}\,\tilde E_x = \pm\frac{\tilde E_x}{\eta},\\[4pt]
> \text{since}\quad \frac{\beta}{\omega\mu} = \frac{\omega\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}}{\omega\mu} = \sqrt{\frac\epsilon\mu} = \frac1\eta .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> $\pm$ is the direction; the second equation holds as $\beta/\eta = \omega\epsilon$. With $\sigma\neq0$ complex $\eta$ shifts $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}$ by $-\angle\eta$ ([[concepts/phasor-maxwell-equations|Maxwell's equations in phasor form]], [[concepts/intrinsic-impedance|Intrinsic impedance]]).

> [!recipe] From a field to its partner, in phasors
> 1. Convert term by term; group into waves by exponent; read each $\hat u$.
> 2. Per wave: $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \tfrac1\eta\hat u\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$ or $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = \eta\tilde{\mathbf{H}}\times\hat u$.
> 3. Check $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^*\parallel+\hat u$, $\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\rvert/\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{H}}\rvert = \eta$.
> 4. Time domain: $\times e^{j\omega t}$, take Re.

**Examples.**
- $3\sin(\omega t - 30^\circ) = 3\cos(\omega t - 120^\circ)$: $3e^{-j120^\circ} = -1.5 - 2.60j$.
- $6\cos\omega t + 6\sin\omega t$: $6 - 6j = 6\sqrt2\,e^{-j45^\circ}$, i.e. $8.49\cos(\omega t - 45^\circ)$.
- $-0.4\sin(\omega t + 2\pi z - \pi/4)\,\hat y$ has phasor $0.4e^{j\pi/4}e^{j2\pi z}\,\hat y$; with $\eta = 40\pi\ \Omega$ the whole wave carries $5\pi\approx15.7$ W/m².
- The 200 MHz wave $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = 12e^{-j(8\pi/3)y}\,\hat z$ V/m toward $+\hat y$ ([[concepts/monochromatic-waves|Monochromatic waves: ω, β, λ, v_p]]): $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \tfrac{1}{60\pi}\,\hat y\times\hat z\;12e^{-j(8\pi/3)y} = \tfrac{1}{5\pi}e^{-j(8\pi/3)y}\,\hat x\approx63.7\,e^{-j(8\pi/3)y}\,\hat x$ mA/m ($\hat z\times\hat x = +\hat y$ ✓); $\mathbf{H} = 63.7\cos\big(4\pi\times10^8t - \tfrac{8\pi}{3}y\big)\,\hat x$ mA/m.

> [!example] Example 2 (notes) — two waves at once, one rule each
> $\mathbf{H} = \hat xH^+\cos(\omega t - \beta z) + \hat yH^-\sin(\omega t + \beta z)$, real $H^\pm$:
> $$
> \tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \hat xH^+e^{-j\beta z} - j\,\hat yH^-e^{j\beta z}.
> $$
> Each wave **with its own** $\hat u$:
> - $+z$: $\eta H^+e^{-j\beta z}\,(\hat x\times\hat z) = -\hat y\,\eta H^+e^{-j\beta z}$;
> - $-z$: $\eta(-jH^-e^{j\beta z})\,\big(\hat y\times(-\hat z)\big) = \eta(-jH^-e^{j\beta z})(-\hat x) = +j\,\hat x\,\eta H^-e^{j\beta z}$.
>
> $$
> \tilde{\mathbf{E}} = -\hat y\,\eta H^+e^{-j\beta z} + j\,\hat x\,\eta H^-e^{j\beta z},
> $$
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \mathbf{E} = &-\hat y\,\eta H^+\cos(\omega t - \beta z)\\
> &- \hat x\,\eta H^-\sin(\omega t + \beta z),
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> as $\text{Re}\{je^{j(\omega t + \beta z)}\} = -\sin(\omega t + \beta z)$. The notes invite a hunt for a sign error; there is none (checked numerically). One $\hat u$ for both waves flips a term. Its power, $\tfrac\eta2[(H^+)^2 - (H^-)^2]\hat z$: [[concepts/time-average-power|Time averages and average power]].

> [!example] Example 3 (notes) — a sinusoidal current sheet, in phasors
> The sheet's delay $d/v$ ($d$: distance; $\hat n$: from sheet to field point; [[concepts/current-sheet-radiation|Radiation from a current sheet]]) becomes $e^{-j\beta d}$. For $\mathbf{J}_s(t) = \text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_se^{j\omega t}\}$ in a lossless medium,
> $$
> \tilde{\mathbf{E}} = -\frac\eta2\,\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_s\,e^{-j\beta d},\qquad \tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \frac12\,\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_s\times\hat n\;e^{-j\beta d}.
> $$
> Sheet on $z = 0$: $d = \lvert z\rvert$, $\hat n = \pm\hat z$ for $z\gtrless0$; $\tilde{\mathbf{S}}\parallel+\hat n$. The notes' $\mathbf{J}_s = \hat x\,2\cos(\omega t)$ A/m in vacuum: $\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_s = 2\hat x$ A/m, $\beta = \omega/c$, $\eta = \eta_0\approx120\pi\ \Omega$; $E_x = -\eta\cos(\omega t\mp\beta z)$, $H_y = \mp\cos(\omega t\mp\beta z)$ become
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \tilde{\mathbf{E}}^\pm = -\eta\,e^{\mp j\beta z}\,\hat x\ \ \text{V/m},\\[4pt]
> \tilde{\mathbf{H}}^\pm = \mp e^{\mp j\beta z}\,\hat y\ \ \text{A/m}\qquad(z\gtrless0).
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> Checks: $\tfrac12(2\hat x)\times(\pm\hat z) = \mp\hat y$ ✓; $(-\eta\hat x)\times(-\hat y) = +\eta\hat z$ above, $(-\eta\hat x)\times(+\hat y) = -\eta\hat z$ below ✓. **A sine instead** (this site's): $\mathbf{J}_s = \hat x\,2\sin(\omega t)$ A/m, $\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_s = -2j\hat x$; $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^\pm = j\eta e^{\mp j\beta z}\hat x$, $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^\pm = \pm je^{\mp j\beta z}\hat y$, i.e. $E_x = -\eta\sin(\omega t\mp\beta z)$, $H_y = \mp\sin(\omega t\mp\beta z)$. Same averages.

> [!trap]
> - The $-j$ of a sine; the quadrant of $x + jy$ (complex-number moves above).
> - $e^{-j\beta z}$: $+z$; $e^{+j\beta z}$: $-z$ (unsure? restore $e^{j\omega t}$). The template's $(a, b, c)$ points *against* the travel.
> - A front factor ($-j$, $e^{j\pi/3}$, $4 - 3j$) is a phase, not a direction.
> - No $t$ in a phasor, nothing complex in time: never "$\mathbf{E}(z,t) = 5e^{-j\beta z}\hat x$"; write $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(z) = 5e^{-j\beta z}\hat x$ or $5\cos(\omega t - \beta z)\,\hat x$.
> - $\text{Re}\{\tilde A\}\cos\omega t$ is not $\text{Re}\{\tilde Ae^{j\omega t}\}$: it loses the sine part.
> - The $\tfrac12$ and the conjugate in $\langle ab\rangle$; rms phasors drop the $\tfrac12$.
