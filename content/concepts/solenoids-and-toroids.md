> [!key] Field of an infinite solenoid
> $$
> \mathbf{B} = \hat z\,\mu_0nI,\qquad \mathbf{H} = \hat z\,nI\quad\text{inside};\qquad \mathbf{B} = 0\quad\text{outside}.
> $$
> $n$ turns per metre, each carrying $I$ counter-clockwise viewed from the top ($+z$). Uniform inside, independent of the coil's radius; by the sheet rule, tangential $\mathbf{H}$ jumps by $J_s = nI$ across the winding. With a core, $\mathbf{H} = nI\hat z$ still and $\mathbf{B} = \mu nI\hat z$ ([[concepts/permeability|Permeability, susceptibility, and B = μH]]).

**Physics.** From a little distance a tight helix is a cylindrical **current sheet** $\mathbf{J}_s = nI\,\hat\phi$ ([[concepts/current-distributions|Current distributions: J, J_s, I]]) — the plane sheet of [[concepts/current-sheets-and-slabs|Current sheets and slabs]] rolled up. By symmetry the field is axial and depends only on $r$, and [[concepts/amperes-law|Ampère's law]] with two rectangles of height $L$ does the rest:

- $C_1$, both long legs *outside*, links $nL$ turns one way and $nL$ the other: $\oint_{C_1}\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 0$. So the exterior field is uniform, and for an isolated infinite coil it is zero (a finite loop's field falls off, so the field at infinity, hence everywhere outside, is zero).
- $C_2$, one leg inside and one outside, links $nL$ turns all the same way: $B_zL = \mu_0nLI$.

The solenoid traps its flux: the field lines that run up the inside must close, and for the infinite coil they close at infinity. Doubling $n$ doubles $B$; the coil's radius never enters, which is why $L = n^2\mu_0A\ell$ depends on the cross-section only through the flux $\mu_0nIA$ per turn ([[concepts/inductance|Inductance]]).

**Two footnotes from the notes.** Ampère's law alone cannot exclude a uniform exterior field from *other* sources; one would add to the interior field too, unrelated to $I$. A finite solenoid has $\mathbf{H}\approx\hat znI$ at its centre, dropping to $\tfrac12nI$ at each end, with lines fringing out and looping back outside (they cannot end). For a coil much longer than its radius the infinite result is excellent at the centre. (A slide asserts the exterior field is zero without the argument — [[0-toolkit/04-errata-in-the-course-materials|errata]].)

**Toroid** (site's own; the course uses it in homework and practice, not in lecture). Wind $N$ turns on a ring of mean radius $r$: the winding is now a *closed* current sheet and the field is confined to the core, circling the axis. A circle of radius $r$ inside the core links all $N$ turns once:

$$
H_\phi\cdot2\pi r = NI\quad\Rightarrow\quad \mathbf{H} = \hat\phi\,\frac{NI}{2\pi r}\quad\text{inside the core},\qquad \mathbf{H} = 0\quad\text{outside}
$$

(a circle inside the hole links no current; one outside the ring links $N$ turns in and $N$ out). For a thin ring, $N/(2\pi r) = n$ and the result is the solenoid's $nI$ — a solenoid bent into a circle, with no ends to fringe. The flux through one turn of cross-section $A$ is $\mu NIA/(2\pi r)$, so $L = \mu N^2A/(2\pi r)$; a two-layer core carries two $\mu$'s in series along $\hat\phi$ with the same $\mathbf{H}$ ([[practice/15-inductance-and-magnetic-energy#159-toroid-with-a-two-layer-core|15.9]]).

**Examples.** $n = 1000$ turns/m, $I = 2$ A: $H = 2000$ A/m, $B = \mu_0nI = 2.51$ mT in air; $\mu_r = 100$ core: 251 mT ([[concepts/magnetic-energy|Magnetic energy]], [[concepts/permeability|Permeability, susceptibility, and B = μH]]). Solenoid inside solenoid: fields add inside the inner one, only the outer one's field between the windings — the superposition set piece. A solenoid's ramping current is the standard source of a time-independent exterior $\mathbf{E}$ with zero local $\mathbf{B}$ in the voltmeter paradox ([[concepts/motional-emf|Motional emf and the voltmeter paradox]]).

> [!trap]
> - $n$ is turns per metre, $N = n\ell$ the total; a slide swaps them in the inductance ($L = n^2\mu_0A\ell = N^2\mu_0A/\ell$).
> - The direction: curl the right hand along the current in the winding; the thumb gives $\mathbf{B}$ inside. Counter-clockwise seen from $+z$ gives $+\hat z$.
> - "Zero outside" is for the *infinite* isolated coil; a finite coil's return flux is outside, weak but not zero.
> - A toroid's $H$ falls as $1/r$ across the core; "uniform" is a thin-ring approximation.

> [!exam] On exams
> The third Ampère set piece: state $\mathbf{H} = H_z(r)\hat z$, draw $C_2$ with one leg inside, cancel $L$, give $nI$ in A/m. Superposition variants (coaxial solenoids, a solenoid in a uniform external field) are common; so is the chain to $L = N^2\mu_0A/\ell$ and $W = \tfrac12\mu_0H^2\cdot A\ell$. For a toroid use a circle inside the core and count $N$, not $n$.
