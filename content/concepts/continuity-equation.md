> [!key] Continuity equation
> $$
> \oint_S\mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = -\frac{dQ_V}{dt} = -\frac{d}{dt}\int_V\rho\,dV\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = -\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t},
> $$
> $d\mathbf{S}$ outward; the second form by the [[concepts/divergence-theorem|divergence theorem]] and "true for every $V$". Net current **out** = rate of **decrease** of enclosed charge; where current lines diverge, charge is being depleted. It is charge conservation, nothing more and nothing less.

**Physics.** Charge is made or removed only in $\pm$ pairs: photoionization (neutral atom → free ion + free electron) and recombination; pair creation and electron–positron annihilation at higher energy. So a change in $Q_V = \int_V\rho\,dV$ in a fixed volume must be carried across the bounding surface $S$ by a current — the bucket picture of [[3-maxwell-and-waves/16-charge-conservation-displacement-current-and-maxwells-equations|Lecture 16]].

The notes' derivation: two open surfaces $S_1$ (below) and $S_2$ (above) on the same rim enclose $V$, both with $d\mathbf{S}$ "upward". $I_1 = \int_{S_1}\mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{S}_1$ enters through the bottom, $I_2 = \int_{S_2}\mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{S}_2$ leaves through the top, so $dQ_V/dt = I_1 - I_2$. Flipping the sign on $S_1$ to make the normal **outward** on $S = S_1\cup S_2$ gives the integral form above.

The equation is not independent of [[concepts/maxwells-equations|Maxwell's equations]]: the divergence of $\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}+\partial\mathbf{D}/\partial t$ is $0 = \nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}+\partial_t\nabla\cdot\mathbf{D} = \nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}+\partial\rho/\partial t$ by Gauss's law. Run backwards, that is Maxwell's argument: the static law $\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}$ would require $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$ always, contradicting conservation wherever charge accumulates (a charging capacitor, a draining sphere, any antenna), so a term whose divergence is $\partial\rho/\partial t$ *must* be added — the [[concepts/displacement-current|Displacement current]]. Steady currents are the special case $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$: current lines close on themselves, the same current flows through every cross-section of a wire, and the Coulomb-gauge solution of $\nabla^2\mathbf{A} = -\mu_0\mathbf{J}$ ([[concepts/vector-potential|Vector potential]], [[2-magnetostatics/13-current-sheets-solenoids-and-the-vector-potential|Lecture 13]]) is consistent.

**Already at work before Lecture 16.** [[1-electrostatics/08-conductors-dielectrics-and-polarization|Lecture 8]]: with $\mathbf{J} = \sigma\mathbf{E}$ and Gauss, $\partial\rho/\partial t = -(\sigma/\epsilon)\rho$ — interior charge decays as $e^{-t/\tau}$ with the relaxation time $\tau = \epsilon/\sigma$. [[1-electrostatics/10-capacitance-and-conductance|Lecture 10]]: the lossy capacitor's $I = C\,dV/dt + GV$ is continuity for one plate's charge — current in minus leakage out equals $dQ/dt$; and the coax conductance's leakage current is the same through every coaxial cylinder, $J_r\cdot2\pi rL$ with $J_r\propto1/r$, because $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$ in the steady state.

**Examples.**

> [!example] Slides — rate of decrease of charge in a unit cube
> $\mathbf{J} = (x, y, z)$ A/m² in the cube $0<x,y,z<1$ m. $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 1+1+1 = 3$ A/m³, so $-dQ/dt = \int_V\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}\,dV = 3$ A: the charge inside *decreases* at 3 C/s. Face by face: $J_x = 1$ on $x = 1$, $0$ on $x = 0$, so 1 A leaves through the $x$ faces; likewise $y$, $z$ — 3 A out ✓. The underlined word is *decrease*; the sign is the point.

- Numbers for a draining sphere and the surfaces around it: [[problems/mmf-around-a-draining-charge|Worked problem — MMF around a draining point charge]].

> [!trap]
> - The sign: outward flux is a *decrease*. Problems that underline "rate of decrease" want $+\oint\mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$.
> - Partial derivative in the point form; the total $dQ/dt$ is fine for a fixed volume.
> - $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$ is a *statics* statement, not a law.

> [!exam] On exams
> Given $\mathbf{J}$, the rate of change of charge in a region: integrate $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}$ (or the flux through the faces), then say *decrease* or *increase*. Concept question: why $\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}$ alone contradicts charge conservation (divergence of a curl is zero).
