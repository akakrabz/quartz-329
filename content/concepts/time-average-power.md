> [!key] The averages you need
> For period $T = 2\pi/\omega$, over any whole period,
> $$
> \langle f\rangle\equiv\frac1T\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T}f(t)\,dt,
> $$
> and $\cos^2(\omega t + \phi) = \tfrac12\big[1 + \cos(2\omega t + 2\phi)\big]$: a constant plus a $2\omega$ ripple that averages to zero. So
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \langle\cos^2(\omega t + \phi)\rangle = \langle\sin^2(\omega t + \phi)\rangle = \tfrac12,\\[4pt]
> \langle\cos(\omega t + \phi)\rangle = 0,\qquad \langle\sin\omega t\cos\omega t\rangle = 0,
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> for every phase, including $\phi = \mp\beta z$: $\tfrac12$ at every point.
>
> **Time averages of power densities, from phasors:**
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \langle\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^*\}\ \ [\text{W/m}^2],\\[4pt]
> \langle\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{J}}\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^*\}\ \ [\text{W/m}^3],\\[6pt]
> \tilde{\mathbf{S}}\equiv\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^*\ \ (\text{complex Poynting vector}),\\[4pt]
> \langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{S}}\}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> Componentwise; sheets: $\langle\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_s\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^*\}$ W/m². **One lossless plane wave**:
> $$
> \langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \frac{\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_0\rvert^2}{2\eta}\,\hat u = \frac{\eta\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_0\rvert^2}{2}\,\hat u,
> $$
> $\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_0\rvert^2 = \lvert\tilde E_{0x}\rvert^2 + \lvert\tilde E_{0y}\rvert^2 + \lvert\tilde E_{0z}\rvert^2$, whatever the phases.

**Physics.** A cosine wave's $\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}$ pulses at $2\omega$ between 0 and its peak; what a detector or a power budget sees is the average. Two routes give it. *Cosines* ([[3-maxwell-and-waves/20-poynting-theorem-and-monochromatic-waves#7-time-averages|Lecture 20 §7]]): square, use $\langle\cos^2\rangle = \tfrac12$. *Phasors* ([[3-maxwell-and-waves/21-monochromatic-waves-and-phasor-notation#7-time-averages-straight-from-phasors|Lecture 21 §7]]): there is no phasor for a product, since $\cos^2\omega t = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12\cos2\omega t$ has parts at 0 and $2\omega$, but the average of a product is $\tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde A\tilde B^*\} = \tfrac12AB\cos(\theta_a - \theta_b)$: in phase $\tfrac12AB$, quadrature 0, antiphase $-\tfrac12AB$; $\langle a^2\rangle = \tfrac12\lvert\tilde A\rvert^2$. A common phase cancels, which is why the conjugate removes the shared $e^{-j\beta z}$ of $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}$ ([[concepts/phasors|Phasors]]).

> [!derivation]- The product rule ⟨a b⟩ = ½ Re{Ã B̃*}
> With $\text{Re}\{z\} = \tfrac12(z + z^*)$:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> a\,b &= \tfrac14\big(\tilde Ae^{j\omega t} + \tilde A^*e^{-j\omega t}\big)\big(\tilde Be^{j\omega t} + \tilde B^*e^{-j\omega t}\big)\\[4pt]
> &= \frac{\tilde A\tilde B^* + \tilde A^*\tilde B}{4} + \frac{\tilde A\tilde Be^{j2\omega t} + \tilde A^*\tilde B^*e^{-j2\omega t}}{4}\\[4pt]
> &= \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde A\tilde B^*\} + \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde A\tilde B\,e^{j2\omega t}\}.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> The $2\omega$ term averages out:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \langle a\,b\rangle &= \frac1T\int_0^Ta(t)\,b(t)\,dt\\
> &= \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde A\tilde B^*\} = \tfrac12AB\cos(\theta_a - \theta_b).
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Notes: same proof for $\langle v(t)i(t)\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{VI^*\}$.

**Why stored energy drops out.** Periodic steady state: $\langle\partial w/\partial t\rangle = [w(t_0 + T) - w(t_0)]/T = 0$, leaving from [[concepts/poynting-theorem|Poynting's theorem]] the **time-averaged Poynting theorem**:

$$
\begin{gathered}
\nabla\cdot\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = -\langle\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle,\\[4pt]
\oint_A\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle\cdot d\mathbf{A} = -\int_V\langle\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle\,dV .
\end{gathered}
$$
Average outflow = power injected minus power dissipated inside. Slab $\lvert z\rvert<d$ about the notes' sheet (Examples below): $\eta_0/2 + \eta_0/2 = \eta_0$ for every $d$.

**A single plane wave**, $\mathbf{E} = \hat x\,E_0\cos(\omega t - \beta z)$, $\mathbf{H} = \hat y\,(E_0/\eta)\cos(\omega t - \beta z)$ ([[concepts/intrinsic-impedance|Intrinsic impedance]]):

$$
\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \hat z\,\frac{E_0^2}{2\eta} = \hat z\,\tfrac12\eta H_0^2 = \hat z\,\tfrac12E_0H_0 .
$$

Since $\eta^2 = \mu/\epsilon$, $\tfrac12\mu(E/\eta)^2 = \tfrac12\epsilon E^2$: electric and magnetic energies are equal at every instant, $w = \epsilon E^2$, $\langle w\rangle = \tfrac12\epsilon E_0^2$, and $\langle S\rangle = v\langle w\rangle$, like $\mathbf{J} = \rho\mathbf{u}$. Notes' example: $\langle w\rangle = \eta_0/(2c) = 6.3\times10^{-7}$ J/m³ per side of the sheet below.

**Time-average power in a lossy medium.** For $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = \hat xE_0e^{-\alpha z}e^{-j\beta z}$, $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \hat y\,(E_0/\eta)\,e^{-\alpha z}e^{-j\beta z}$, $E_0$ possibly complex:

$$
\begin{gathered}
\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^*\}\\[4pt]
= \hat z\,\frac{\lvert E_0\rvert^2}{2\lvert\eta\rvert}\,\cos\tau\;e^{-2\alpha z}\quad[\text{W/m}^2].
\end{gathered}
$$

- **Power** reaches $1/e$ at $z = \delta/2$, the field at $z = \delta$.
- In **decibels** both fall at the same rate: $10\log_{10}e^{2\alpha z} = 20\log_{10}e^{\alpha z} = 8.686\,\alpha z$ dB (1 Np = 8.686 dB).
- $\cos\tau$ is a power factor: 1 lossless, $1/\sqrt2$ in a good conductor.

**Where the lost power goes: heat.** The averaged theorem with $\mathbf{J} = \sigma\mathbf{E}$ and $z$ dependence only:

$$
\begin{gathered}
-\frac{d\langle S_z\rangle}{dz} = 2\alpha\,\frac{\lvert E_0\rvert^2\cos\tau}{2\lvert\eta\rvert}\,e^{-2\alpha z}\\[4pt]
= \langle\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle = \tfrac12\sigma\lvert E_0\rvert^2e^{-2\alpha z}\\[4pt]
\Longrightarrow\quad \alpha = \frac{\sigma\lvert\eta\rvert}{2\cos\tau}.
\end{gathered}
$$

Exact for any medium ($\text{Re}\{\gamma/\eta\} = \sigma$ in disguise); with $\cos\tau\approx1$, $\lvert\eta\rvert\approx\sqrt{\mu/\epsilon}$ it is the imperfect-dielectric $\alpha = (\sigma/2)\sqrt{\mu/\epsilon}$ of [[concepts/loss-tangent|Loss tangent]], so that formula is energy conservation. The notes' 1 GHz example ([[concepts/skin-depth|Skin depth]]) at $z = 0$: $\tfrac12\sigma\lvert E_0\rvert^2 = \tfrac12\times10^{-3}\times4 = 2.0$ mW/m³ $= 2\alpha\langle S_z\rangle = 2\times0.094\times10.6$ mW/m² ✓.

**Joule heating.** With $\tilde{\mathbf{J}} = \sigma\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$, $\langle\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\sigma\}\,\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\rvert^2$: $\tfrac12\sigma E_0^2$ for real $\sigma$. Only current in phase with $\mathbf{E}$ absorbs. A collisionless plasma's imaginary $\sigma$ ([[concepts/dispersion-relation|Dispersion relation and phase velocity]]): current 90° off, energy returned each quarter period, lossless. Collisions: $\text{Re}\{\sigma_{\text{DC}}/(1 + j\omega/\nu)\} = \sigma_{\text{DC}}/(1 + \omega^2/\nu^2)$, damping ([[concepts/conductivity-and-susceptibility-models|Lorentz–Drude models of σ and χe]], [[concepts/propagation-constant|Propagation constant]]).

**Examples.**

> [!example] Energy balance of the notes' sheet (Lecture 20 by cos², Lecture 21 by phasors)
> For $\mathbf{J}_s = \hat x\,2\cos\omega t$ A/m in vacuum ([[concepts/current-sheet-radiation|Radiation from a current sheet]]):
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \langle\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}\rangle = \pm\hat z\,\frac{\eta_0}{2}\approx\pm\hat z\,188\ \text{W/m}^2,\\[4pt]
> \langle-\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle = \eta_0\approx377\ \text{W/m}^2 ,
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> upper sign for $z>0$: injected = $\eta_0/2 + \eta_0/2$ (the notes' $60\pi$ and $120\pi$). For $\hat x\,J_0\cos\omega t$ in impedance $\eta$: $\eta J_0^2/8$ per side, $\langle-\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle = \eta J_0^2/4$. By phasors, $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^\pm = -\eta e^{\mp j\beta z}\hat x$, $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^\pm = \mp e^{\mp j\beta z}\hat y$:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \langle\mathbf{S}^\pm\rangle &= \tfrac12\text{Re}\big\{(-\eta e^{\mp j\beta z}\hat x)\times(\mp e^{\pm j\beta z}\hat y)\big\}\\
> &= \pm\frac\eta2\,\hat z = \pm60\pi\,\hat z\ \ \text{W/m}^2,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \langle-\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E}(0, t)\rangle &= \tfrac12\text{Re}\{-\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_s\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^*(0)\}\\
> &= \tfrac12\text{Re}\{-(2\hat x)\cdot(-\eta\hat x)\}\\
> &= \eta\approx120\pi\ \ \text{W/m}^2 .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> $\eta/2$ per side, any $z$. Sine current ($\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_s = -2j\hat x$): $(-j)(-j)^* = 1$, same.

- **The 200 MHz wave** $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = 12e^{-j(8\pi/3)y}\,\hat z$ V/m, $\eta = 60\pi\ \Omega$ ([[concepts/monochromatic-waves|Monochromatic waves: ω, β, λ, v_p]]): $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = 144/(120\pi)\,\hat y = 0.382\,\hat y$ W/m².
- **Two phases:** $3\cos(\omega t + 20^\circ)$, $2\cos(\omega t - 40^\circ)$: $\tfrac12\cdot3\cdot2\cos60^\circ = 1.5$.

> [!example] Example — J·E in sea water (the exam's question, new numbers)
> Sea water ($\sigma\approx4$ S/m), low-frequency $\mathbf{E} = \hat y\,0.5\cos\omega t$ V/m: $\mathbf{J} = \hat y\,2\cos\omega t$ A/m², $\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E} = \sigma E^2 = \cos^2\omega t$ W/m³; peak 1, average 0.5 W/m³ of heat, half a milliwatt per litre. Summer 2019 HE3 #1b.

> [!example] Example — sunlight and a radio station as fields
> - **Sunlight** above the atmosphere: about 1361 W/m². As one cosine wave, $E_0 = \sqrt{2\eta_0\langle S\rangle}\approx1.01$ kV/m, $H_0 = E_0/\eta_0\approx2.69$ A/m (rms 716 V/m, 1.90 A/m); energy density $\langle S\rangle/c\approx4.5\ \mu$J/m³.
> - **A 50 kW transmitter radiating equally in all directions** (idealized). Averaged integral form on a sphere of radius $r$, enclosing only the transmitter: $4\pi r^2\langle S\rangle = 50$ kW for every $r$, $\langle S\rangle\propto1/r^2$. Far out (many wavelengths) the wave is locally plane, $E_0 = \sqrt{2\eta_0\langle S\rangle}\propto1/r$; near the antenna, energy-storing fields break this. At 10 km: $\langle S\rangle = 3.98\times10^{-5}$ W/m², $E_0 = 0.173$ V/m; with $\eta_0 = 120\pi$, $E_0 = \sqrt{60P}/r$ (Rao's argument).

> [!example] Example — perpendicular components carry power independently
> $\mathbf{E} = \hat x\,E_1\cos(\omega t - \beta z) + \hat y\,E_2\cos(\omega t - \beta z + \phi)$, $\mathbf{H} = \hat z\times\mathbf{E}/\eta$: $S_z = E_xH_y - E_yH_x = (E_x^2 + E_y^2)/\eta$, no cross term, so $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \hat z\,(E_1^2 + E_2^2)/(2\eta)$ for any $\phi$. $E_1 = 3$, $E_2 = 4$ V/m in vacuum: $25/(2\times377) = 33$ mW/m². Exams "split, then add" for circular polarization ([[concepts/wave-polarization|Wave polarization]]).

> [!example] Example — two equal waves in opposite directions: S ≠ 0 but ⟨S⟩ = 0
> $\hat x\,E_0\cos(\omega t - \beta z)$ plus $-\hat x\,E_0\cos(\omega t + \beta z)$, each with its own $\mathbf{H}$:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \mathbf{E} = \hat x\,2E_0\sin\omega t\,\sin\beta z,\\[4pt]
> \mathbf{H} = \hat y\,\frac{2E_0}{\eta}\cos\omega t\,\cos\beta z,\\[4pt]
> \mathbf{S} = \hat z\,\frac{E_0^2}{\eta}\sin2\omega t\,\sin2\beta z .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> $\mathbf{S}$ flips sign every quarter period; $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = 0$ at every $z$ ($= (\lvert\mathbf{E}_1\rvert^2 - \lvert\mathbf{E}_2\rvert^2)/\eta$, [[concepts/poynting-vector|Poynting vector]]). Yet $\langle w\rangle = \epsilon E_0^2$ everywhere, sloshing between electric ($\mathbf{E}$ maxima) and magnetic ($\mathbf{H}$ maxima) each quarter period; where $\sin\beta z = 0$, $w = 0$ when $\cos\omega t = 0$. Each half wavelength's energy is constant ([[concepts/standing-waves|Standing waves]]).

> [!example] Example — counter-propagating waves subtract (notes' Lecture 21 Example 2, this site's extension)
> $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = -\hat y\,\eta H^+e^{-j\beta z} + j\,\hat x\,\eta H^-e^{j\beta z}$, $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^* = \hat xH^+e^{j\beta z} + j\hat yH^-e^{-j\beta z}$; $\hat x\times\hat x$, $\hat y\times\hat y$ vanish:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^* &= -\eta(H^+)^2\,(\hat y\times\hat x)\\
> &\quad + j\cdot j\,\eta(H^-)^2\,(\hat x\times\hat y)\\
> &= \eta\big[(H^+)^2 - (H^-)^2\big]\hat z,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> so $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \tfrac\eta2\big[(H^+)^2 - (H^-)^2\big]\hat z$. Parallel polarizations add imaginary cross terms, absent from $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle$.

> [!trap] Where time averages go wrong
> - **The ½ belongs to the average of a squared cosine.** DC has none; the *peak* of $\cos^2$ is 1. Peak phasors keep the ½; rms ones drop it.
> - **Average the product, not the factors:** $\langle E\rangle\langle H\rangle = 0\times0$, but $\langle EH\rangle = \tfrac12E_0H_0$ for one wave. No "phasor of the power"; only the average, from $\tilde A\tilde B^*$.
> - **Missing conjugate:** $\tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}\}$ varies as $\cos2\beta z$ for one linearly polarized wave.
> - **Per area or per volume?** $\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E}$ is W/m², $\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{E}$ W/m³. Exams call $\langle\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle$ "the average Joule heating rate" even at a source (negative).
> - **A sheet's power splits evenly** (same medium both sides): each side carries half.
> - **E and H out of phase** (lossy media, $\mathbf{H}$ lags $\mathbf{E}$): $\langle S\rangle\neq\tfrac12E_0H_0$; $\cos a\cos b = \tfrac12[\cos(a - b) + \cos(a + b)]$ gives $\langle\cos(\omega t + \phi_1)\cos(\omega t + \phi_2)\rangle = \tfrac12\cos(\phi_1 - \phi_2)$, the phasor rule ([[concepts/loss-tangent|Loss tangent]]: $\cos\tau$).
> - Instantaneous $\mathbf{S}$ of one linearly polarized wave swings 0 to $2\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle$ at $2\omega$; with out-of-phase perpendicular components it never hits 0 ([[problems/phasors-e-and-power-from-a-given-h|worked problem]], (f)). Dropping a perpendicular component: powers add.
