## 1. Decoding a damped wave in a magnetic medium (25 points)

**Asks.** $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = 20e^{-(1+j\sqrt3)y}\hat z$ V/m at $\omega = 2\pi\times10^7$ rad/s, $\mu = 2\mu_0$; $\gamma\eta = j\omega\mu$ and $\gamma/\eta = \sigma+j\omega\epsilon$ are given. Find $\gamma$, $\eta$ in polar form, $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(y)$, the loss tangent, and classify the medium on a five-step scale.

**Method.** [[concepts/propagation-constant|Propagation constant]] · [[0-toolkit/07-wave-parameters-map|Wave parameters on one page]]
1. $\gamma$ is the coefficient in the exponent: $\alpha$ its real part [Np/m], $\beta$ its imaginary part [rad/m]. Put it in polar form (the hint gives it).
2. $\eta = j\omega\mu/\gamma$: $\lvert\eta\rvert = \omega\mu/\lvert\gamma\rvert$ and $\angle\eta = 90^\circ-\angle\gamma$. Use the medium's $\mu$.
3. $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \hat u\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}}/\eta$: divide the amplitude by $\lvert\eta\rvert$, subtract $\angle\eta$ from the phase, direction $\hat y\times\hat z$.
4. Loss tangent: $\gamma/\eta = \sigma+j\omega\epsilon$, so $\sigma/\omega\epsilon$ is the ratio of its real to its imaginary part. Its angle is $\angle\gamma-\angle\eta$. Shortcut: $\sigma/\omega\epsilon = \tan(2\angle\eta)$.
5. Classify by comparing $\sigma/\omega\epsilon$ with 1: $\ll1$ imperfect dielectric, $\gg1$ good conductor, neither in between. Equivalent tests: $\alpha$ against $\beta$, $\angle\eta$ against 0° and 45°.

**Traps.** $\mu = 2\mu_0$ enters $\eta$ (and $\sigma$, $\epsilon$) but cancels in the loss tangent. $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}$ **lags**: the phase factor is $e^{-j\angle\eta}$.

**Key note.** In part (d) the written key divides by $4\pi^2$ where its own part (b) gives $\lvert\eta\rvert = 8\pi^2$. The factor cancels in the ratio and the boxed answer is right ([[0-toolkit/04-errata-in-the-course-materials#past-exam-3-solution-keys|errata]]).

## 2. A parallel-plate line (20 points)

**Asks.** Plates of width $w = 0.5$ m, separation $h = 0.01$ m, filled with $\epsilon_r = 4$ ($\eta = 60\pi\ \Omega$ given), $E_x$ and $H_y$ between them. (a) $Z_0$. (b) $\mathbf{E}(z,t)$ when the bottom plate carries $I = 2\delta(t-z/v)$ A along $+z$. (c) The time-averaged power through the cross-section for $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = 240\pi e^{-j\beta z}\hat x$ V/m.

**Method.** [[4-transmission-lines/27-guided-tem-waves-on-transmission-lines|Lecture 27]] · [[concepts/characteristic-impedance|Characteristic impedance]]
1. $V = E_xh$ and $I = H_yw$, so $Z_0 = V/I = \eta\,h/w$ (equivalently $\sqrt{\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{C}}$ with $\mathcal{L} = \mu h/w$, $\mathcal{C} = \epsilon w/h$).
2. Current to field: $J_s = I/w$ on the plate, and $H$ between the plates equals $J_s$ in size ($\hat n\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}_s$, $\hat n$ from the plate into the gap, fixes its direction). Then $\mathbf{E} = \eta\,\mathbf{H}\times\hat z$ for a wave toward $+z$. Circuit route: $V = Z_0I$, $E = V/h$.
3. Power: $\langle S\rangle = \lvert\tilde E\rvert^2/2\eta$ times the area $wh$, or $\tfrac12\lvert\tilde V\rvert^2/Z_0$. Do both as a check.

**Traps.** $h/w$, not $w/h$. The direction of $\mathbf{E}$ must make $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}$ point along the travel. The $\tfrac12$ in the average.

**Twins.** [[practice/27-transmission-line-basics#279-a-pulse-on-a-coax|27.9 A pulse on a coax]] · [[practice/27-transmission-line-basics#271-strip-line-parameters|27.1 Strip-line parameters]] · [[practice/27-transmission-line-basics#274-plane-wave-power-on-a-line|27.4 Plane-wave power on a line]] · worked problem [[problems/a-backward-pulse-on-a-parallel-plate-line|Worked problem — A backward pulse on a parallel-plate line]]

## 3. A circular wave meets a perfect conductor (15 points)

**Asks.** $\mathbf{E}_i = \cos(\omega t+\beta x)\hat y+\sin(\omega t+\beta x)\hat z$ V/m in free space. (a) Its polarization. (b) The reflected field when a perfect conductor fills the space beyond $x = 0$. (c) What the total field in free space is (travelling, standing, or a mix).

**Method.** [[concepts/wave-polarization|Wave polarization]] · [[concepts/reflection-and-transmission|Reflection and transmission coefficients]] · [[concepts/standing-waves|Standing waves]]
1. Travel direction from the sign in $(\omega t+\beta x)$. Equal amplitudes and a quarter-cycle phase: circular. Handedness: (leading) × (lagging) compared with the travel direction.
2. Perfect conductor: the tangential $\mathbf{E}$ must vanish at the wall at all times. Each component reflects with $\Gamma = -1$, and the reflected wave travels the other way: flip the sign of the position term and of each amplitude.
3. $\lvert\Gamma\rvert = 1$: incident and reflected waves have equal size, so their sum is a pure standing wave.

**Traps.** The statement labels the field $\mathbf{E}_i(z,t)$ although it depends on $x$. The reflected wave has the opposite handedness (same rotation in time, reversed travel).

**Twins.** [[practice/25-reflection-and-transmission#2511-a-circular-wave-on-a-mirror|25.11 A circular wave on a mirror]] · [[practice/26-standing-waves-and-radiation-pressure#263-travelling-standing-or-both|26.3 Travelling, standing or both]] · worked problem [[problems/a-circular-wave-meets-a-mirror|Worked problem — A circular wave meets a mirror]]

## 4. Two lines in cascade (25 points)

**Asks.** $Z_1 = 50\ \Omega$ ($v_1\approx10^8$ m/s) joined at $z = \ell/2$ to $Z_2 = 100\ \Omega$ ($v_2 = 2\times10^8$ m/s), $\ell = 40$ m, $R_g = R_L = 100\ \Omega$, $f(t) = 150u(t)$ V. (a)–(d) Voltage **and** current reflection and transmission coefficients at the junction, the load and the generator. (e) The voltage bounce diagram to 1 µs. (f) $V$ at one point and time. (g) The steady-state load voltage.

**Method.** [[4-transmission-lines/30-multi-line-circuits|Lecture 30]] · [[concepts/bounce-diagram|Bounce diagram]]
1. Junction, wave going from 1 into 2: $\Gamma_{12} = (Z_2-Z_1)/(Z_2+Z_1)$, $\tau_{12} = 1+\Gamma_{12}$. For current: $\Gamma_I = -\Gamma_V$ and $\tau_I = 1+\Gamma_I$.
2. Load: $\Gamma_L$ with $Z_2$. Generator, for the wave coming back: $\Gamma_g = (R_g-Z_1)/(R_g+Z_1)$.
3. Diagram: two delays, $T_1 = (\ell/2)/v_1$ and $T_2 = (\ell/2)/v_2$, so the lines have different slopes. Launch $\tau_g\times150$ V with $\tau_g = Z_1/(R_g+Z_1)$. At the junction each arriving wave splits into $\Gamma$ (back) and $\tau$ (through).
4. $V$ at $(z,t)$: draw the horizontal line $t$ and add the weights of every segment that has already passed $z$.
5. Steady state: DC divider, the lines are wires.

**Traps.** $\tau_I\ne\tau_V$. A matched load ends that branch of the diagram, so the only echoes are those trapped between the generator and the junction. The transmitted wave is **larger** than the incident one when $Z_2>Z_1$; power still balances.

**Check yourself.** The values on line 1 must converge to the DC divider value.

## 5. Resonances from two measurements (15 points)

> [!note] Not on FA26 Midterm 3
> This problem is Lectures 31–33, and this semester's Midterm 3 stops at Lecture 30. Skip it when you work the paper for Monday; it is kept here for later.

**Asks.** A 100 m line, $v = 2\times10^8$ m/s, with $\tilde V_{\text{in}} = 0$ and $\tilde I_L = 0$ in sinusoidal steady state. (a) $Z_{\text{in}}$ and $Z_L$. (b) The three lowest resonant frequencies.

**Method.** [[exams/e3-steady-state-lines#2-resonances-from-the-end-conditions|Steady-state lines §2]]: zero voltage is a short, zero current an open; unlike ends hold odd quarter waves, $f_n = (2n-1)v/4\ell$. Sketch $\lvert\tilde V\rvert$ and $\lvert\tilde I\rvert$ for each to make sure both end conditions hold.

**Trap.** Listing $v/2\ell$ multiples (the like-ends family).
