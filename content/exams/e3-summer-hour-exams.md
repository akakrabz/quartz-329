## 1. Build the phasor of a circular wave

**Asked in** 2017 #2a, 2018 #3a, 2019 #3a. A circular wave of given handedness, direction and frequency; $\mathbf{E}$ at the origin at $t = 0$ is given. Fill in $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = (A\hat x+B\hat y+C\hat z)e^{j(ax+by+cz)}$ and find $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle$.

**Method.** [[concepts/wave-polarization|Wave polarization]] · [[concepts/phasors|Phasors]]
1. $\beta = \omega/c = 2\pi f/c$. Only the coordinate along the travel direction appears in the exponent, with a **minus** sign for travel toward the positive axis and a plus sign toward the negative one.
2. The component along the travel direction is zero.
3. The field at $t = 0$ fixes one transverse component as a real number. The other has the same size and a factor $\pm j$.
4. Choose the sign with (leading) $\times$ (lagging) $= +\hat u$ for right circular, $-\hat u$ for left. The component carrying $+j$ leads.
5. $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle$: each component carries $\lvert E\rvert^2/2\eta_0$; the two add, twice the linear value, along $\hat u$.

**Two snapshots instead of a handedness** (2019 #3a): the field at $t = 0$ is the real part of the phasor; the field at $T/4$ is the real part of $j$ times it. Read the two components off, then name the handedness with step 4.

**Traps.** The sign of the exponent. Forgetting that handedness is defined relative to the travel direction: the same rotation in time is right-handed toward $+y$ and left-handed toward $-y$.

**Twins.** [[practice/24-group-velocity-and-polarization#249-building-a-right-circular-wave|24.9 Building a right-circular wave]] · [[practice/24-group-velocity-and-polarization#246-from-two-snapshots-to-a-phasor|24.6 From two snapshots to a phasor]] · [[practice/21-phasors-and-monochromatic-waves#219-a-phasor-from-two-snapshots|21.9 A phasor from two snapshots]]

## 2. Classify, then compute

**Asked in** 2017 #2b, 2018 #3b, 2019 #3b (and the conflict paper), 2020 #3.

**Method.** [[concepts/loss-tangent|Loss tangent]] · [[concepts/skin-depth|Skin depth]] · [[0-toolkit/09-midterm-3-summary#4-the-two-regimes-and-skin-depth-lecture-23|the regime table]]
1. **Classify.** If the medium is given, compute $\sigma/\omega\epsilon = 18\times10^9\sigma/(f\epsilon_r)$. If $\lambda$ and $\delta$ are given instead, compare them: a good conductor has $\lambda = 2\pi\delta$ exactly; $\delta\gg\lambda$ means an imperfect dielectric.
2. **Imperfect dielectric:** $v = f\lambda$, $\epsilon_r = (c/v)^2$, $\lvert\eta\rvert = \eta_0/\sqrt{\epsilon_r}$, and $\sigma$ from $\alpha = 1/\delta = \tfrac12\sigma\eta$.
3. **Good conductor:** $\delta = 1/\sqrt{\pi f\mu\sigma}$, $\lambda = 2\pi\delta$, $v_p = f\lambda = \omega\delta$. For seawater at 1 kHz the square root is exact without a calculator: $\pi f\mu_0\sigma = 16\pi^2\times10^{-4}$.
4. **Power at depth:** $e^{-2z/\delta}$ times the surface value.
5. **"Possible or not possible":** test each statement against two facts. In a conducting medium $\alpha\le\beta$ always. And $\sigma\gg\omega\epsilon$ is the same thing as $\alpha = \beta$, that is $\lambda = 2\pi\delta$.

**Traps.** Classify at the frequency given: dry earth is a good conductor at 100 Hz and an imperfect dielectric at 1 GHz. Power uses $2\alpha$.

**Twins.** [[practice/23-imperfect-dielectrics-and-good-conductors#236-wavelength-and-depth-measured-underground|23.6 Wavelength and depth measured underground]] · [[practice/23-imperfect-dielectrics-and-good-conductors#239-down-into-a-deep-lake|23.9 Down into a deep lake]] · [[practice/23-imperfect-dielectrics-and-good-conductors#2311-dry-earth-from-elf-to-vhf|23.11 Dry earth from ELF to VHF]] · [[practice/22-damped-waves-in-conducting-media#224-possible-or-not-possible|22.4 Possible or not possible]] · worked problem [[problems/a-radio-wave-into-moist-ground|A radio wave into moist ground]]

## 3. A circular wave given in time

**Asked in** 2019 #1a (and the conflict paper): $\mathbf{E} = \cos(\omega t+\beta x)\hat y+\sin(\omega t+\beta x)\hat z$ V/m in free space. The same field is the incident wave of [[exams/e3-sp19#3-a-circular-wave-meets-a-perfect-conductor-15-points|SP19 #3]].

**Method.**
1. Phasor: the cosine gives $\hat y$, the sine gives $-j\hat z$; both share $e^{+j\beta x}$.
2. Direction from the sign of $\beta x$. Then $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \hat u\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}}/\eta_0$, component by component.
3. Power of one component alone: $\lvert E_1\rvert^2/2\eta_0$ along $\hat u$. The whole wave carries twice that.
4. Handedness: which component leads, then (leading) $\times$ (lagging) against $\hat u$.

**Twins.** [[practice/20-poynting-theorem-and-power#206-split-then-add|20.6 Split, then add]] · [[practice/24-group-velocity-and-polarization#245-the-forgotten-travel-direction|24.5 The forgotten travel direction]] · worked problem [[problems/a-circular-wave-meets-a-mirror|A circular wave meets a mirror]]

### Twin: a switched-on sheet in a dielectric

> [!hard] Hard · current-sheet radiation · records and snapshots · power
> A current sheet on the plane $x = 0$ carries $\mathbf{J}_s(t) = -\hat z\,3u(t)\sin(2\pi ft)$ A/m with $f = 1.5$ MHz. All space is filled with a lossless, non-magnetic dielectric with $\epsilon_r = 4$. Use $c = 3\times10^8$ m/s and $\eta_0 = 120\pi\ \Omega$.
> (a) Find $v$, $\eta$, the period $T$ and the wavelength $\lambda$.
> (b) Find $\mathbf{E}(x,t)$ on both sides of the sheet.
> (c) Find $\mathbf{H}(x,t)$ for $x>0$ and for $x<0$.
> (d) When does the field first reach $x = 300$ m? Describe $E_z(300\ \text{m},t)$ for $0<t<3$ μs.
> (e) Describe $H_y(x, 3\ \mu\text{s})$ for $0<x<600$ m.
> (f) For $t>0$, find $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle$ on each side and $\langle\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle$ at the sheet.
>
> *Source: Summer 2017 HE3 #1 style (also 2018 #2, 2019 #2, 2020 #2), re-parameterized: a dielectric instead of free space, a sheet on $x = 0$ with the current along $-\hat z$, a new frequency, and the 2020 paper's power question added.*

> [!hint]- Hint
> $\mathbf{E} = -\tfrac\eta2\mathbf{J}_s(t-\lvert x\rvert/v)$ on both sides; $\mathbf{H} = \tfrac12\mathbf{J}_s(t-\lvert x\rvert/v)\times\hat n$ with $\hat n$ pointing away from the sheet. The step $u(t-\lvert x\rvert/v)$ keeps the field zero until the front arrives. For the snapshot, $2\pi f\cdot3\ \mu\text{s} = 9\pi$.

> [!solution]- Solution
> **(a)** $v = c/\sqrt{\epsilon_r} = 150$ m/μs, $\eta = \eta_0/\sqrt{\epsilon_r} = 60\pi\ \Omega$, $T = 1/f = \tfrac23$ μs, $\lambda = v/f = 100$ m.
>
> **(b)** $\mathbf{E} = -\tfrac\eta2\mathbf{J}_s$, delayed by $\lvert x\rvert/v$: $-\tfrac{60\pi}{2}(-3\hat z) = +90\pi\,\hat z$. On both sides,
> $$
> \mathbf{E} = \hat z\,90\pi\,u\Big(t-\frac{\lvert x\rvert}{v}\Big)\sin\Big[2\pi f\Big(t-\frac{\lvert x\rvert}{v}\Big)\Big]\ \text{V/m},\qquad 90\pi\approx283\ \text{V/m}.
> $$
> $\mathbf{E}$ opposes the current, on both sides.
>
> **(c)** For $x>0$, $\hat n = +\hat x$ and $(-\hat z)\times\hat x = -\hat y$; for $x<0$, $\hat n = -\hat x$ and $(-\hat z)\times(-\hat x) = +\hat y$:
> $$
> \mathbf{H} = \mp\hat y\,1.5\,u\Big(t-\frac{\lvert x\rvert}{v}\Big)\sin\Big[2\pi f\Big(t-\frac{\lvert x\rvert}{v}\Big)\Big]\ \text{A/m}\quad(x\gtrless0).
> $$
> Check for $x>0$: $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}\propto\hat z\times(-\hat y) = +\hat x$, away from the sheet ✓. And $E/H = 90\pi/1.5 = 60\pi = \eta$ ✓.
>
> **(d)** The front travels 300 m in $300/150 = 2$ μs. $E_z(300\ \text{m},t) = 0$ for $t<2$ μs. From $t = 2$ μs it is $90\pi\sin[2\pi f(t-2\ \mu\text{s})]$: it starts at zero, rises first, and completes $1/T = 1.5$ periods by $t = 3$ μs.
>
> **(e)** At $t = 3$ μs the front is at $x = 450$ m; beyond it the field is zero. Behind it, $2\pi f(3\ \mu\text{s}-x/v) = 9\pi-2\pi x/\lambda$ and $\sin(9\pi-\theta) = \sin\theta$:
> $$
> H_y(x, 3\ \mu\text{s}) = -1.5\sin\frac{2\pi x}{100\ \text{m}}\ \text{A/m},\qquad 0<x<450\ \text{m}.
> $$
> Four and a half wavelengths: zero at the sheet, $-1.5$ A/m at $x = 25$ m, $+1.5$ A/m at 75 m, and negative just behind the front ($-0.88$ A/m at 440 m). The part next to the front left the sheet first.
>
> **(f)** Each side: $\langle S\rangle = \tfrac12(90\pi)(1.5) = 67.5\pi\approx212$ W/m², pointing away from the sheet. At the sheet $\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E} = (-3\sin)(90\pi\sin)$, so $\langle\mathbf{J}_s\cdot\mathbf{E}\rangle = -135\pi\approx-424$ W/m². Negative: the sheet is a source, and it supplies exactly what the two sides carry away, $2\times212$ W/m².
>
> **Answer.** (a) 150 m/μs, $60\pi\ \Omega$, $\tfrac23$ μs, 100 m. (b) $\mathbf{E} = \hat z\,90\pi\,u\sin[2\pi f(t-\lvert x\rvert/v)]$ V/m on both sides. (c) $\mathbf{H} = \mp\hat y\,1.5\,u\sin[\cdot]$ A/m for $x\gtrless0$. (d) 2 μs; zero before, then a sine starting upward, 1.5 periods by 3 μs. (e) $-1.5\sin(2\pi x/100)$ A/m for $x<450$ m, zero beyond. (f) $67.5\pi\approx212$ W/m² away from the sheet on each side; $-135\pi\approx-424$ W/m².
