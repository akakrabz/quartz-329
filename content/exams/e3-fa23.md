## 1. Four concept items (11 points)

**Asks.** (a) Which statements about decomposing a polarization are true. (b) What the loss tangent represents. (c) True or false: the stored-energy density is zero where a standing wave exists. (d) A plot of sinusoids with fixed nodes drawn at several times: what could it be?

**Method.**
- (a) Any polarization is a sum of two orthogonal linear ones (that is how the phasor is written), and a linear wave is the sum of a right- and a left-circular wave of equal size: $\hat x = \tfrac12(\hat x-j\hat y)+\tfrac12(\hat x+j\hat y)$. [[concepts/wave-polarization|Wave polarization]]
- (b) Draw the three current phasors: conduction $\sigma\tilde E$ (real), displacement $j\omega\epsilon\tilde E$ (90° ahead), and their sum. Read the angle between the displacement and the total current off the triangle, and take its tangent. [[concepts/loss-tangent|Loss tangent]]
- (c) Separate the two statements people merge: $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = 0$ in a standing wave, but energy is still stored and sloshes between $w_e$ and $w_m$. [[concepts/standing-waves|Standing waves]]
- (d) Fixed nodes mean a standing wave; a travelling wave's zeros move. Then ask whether $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{H}$ differ in *shape* or only in where the nodes sit.

**Traps.** "Circle all that apply" items often have an "all of the above". In (c), zero net power flow is not zero energy.

## 2. Name the polarization (9 points)

**Asks.** $\mathbf{H} = \hat xA\cos(\omega t-kz)+\hat yB\cos(\omega t-kz+\phi)$ for three sets $(A, B, \phi)$: linear, right circular, left circular or elliptical?

**Method.** The class depends only on the amplitude ratio and the phase difference, and it is the same for $\mathbf{H}$ as for $\mathbf{E}$ ($\mathbf{H}$ is $\mathbf{E}$ turned 90° about the travel direction and divided by $\eta$).
1. $\phi = 0$ or $\pi$: the components rise and fall together, **linear**, whatever $A$ and $B$ are.
2. $A = B$ **and** $\phi = \pm\pi/2$: **circular**. Handedness: (leading component) × (lagging component) along the travel direction is right-handed.
3. Anything else: **elliptical**.

**Traps.** Unequal amplitudes do not make a wave elliptical when $\phi = 0$ or $\pi$. Equal amplitudes do not make it circular unless the phase is exactly a quarter cycle. The statement prints the amplitudes of $\mathbf{H}$ in V/m; they are A/m (it does not affect the answer).

**Twins.** [[practice/24-group-velocity-and-polarization#243-name-four-polarizations|24.3 Name four polarizations]] · [[practice/24-group-velocity-and-polarization#248-starting-from-h|24.8 Starting from H]] (a wave given through $\mathbf{H}$) · [[practice/24-group-velocity-and-polarization#244-true-or-false-on-one-wave|24.4 True or false on one wave]]

## 3. Three line sections in cascade (12 points)

**Asks.** An impulse source behind $100\ \Omega$ drives sections of $100\ \Omega$, $50\ \Omega$ and $150\ \Omega$ (1 µs each) into $150\ \Omega$: the injection coefficient, the voltage reflection coefficient at the first junction, the **current** reflection coefficient at the second, and the earliest time the second junction's echo reaches the generator.

**Method.** [[4-transmission-lines/30-multi-line-circuits|Lecture 30]]
1. $\tau_g = Z_{01}/(R_g+Z_{01})$: the first section is all the generator sees at $t = 0$.
2. At a junction, for a wave going from section $j$ into section $k$: $\Gamma_{jk} = (Z_{0k}-Z_{0j})/(Z_{0k}+Z_{0j})$. The line beyond acts as a load equal to its $Z_0$.
3. The current coefficient is $-\Gamma$ (a backward wave carries $I = -V/Z_0$).
4. Timing: add the one-way delays out to the junction and back.

**Traps.** The order of the subscripts (which side the wave comes from) sets the sign. "Minimum time" means the direct path, with no extra bounces.

**Twins.** [[practice/30-multi-line-circuits#308-three-sections-and-the-first-echoes|30.8 Three sections and the first echoes]] · [[practice/30-multi-line-circuits#301-coefficients-both-ways|30.1 Coefficients both ways]] · worked problem [[problems/echoes-trapped-on-the-second-line|Worked problem — Echoes trapped on the second line]]

## 4. Two pulses at the midpoint (18 points)

**Asks.** A source $30\delta(t)$ with unknown $R_g$ on a $50\ \Omega$, 100 m line with unknown $R_L$. At the midpoint two impulses are seen, $A\delta(t-1\ \mu\text{s})$ and $\tfrac A2\delta(t-3\ \mu\text{s})$, and nothing else. Find $R_g$, $R_L$, $A$ and, for a non-magnetic line, $\epsilon_r$.

**Method.** [[concepts/bounce-diagram|Bounce diagram]]
1. **Timing gives $v$:** the first pulse covers half the line in the first arrival time. Then the second pulse is identified by its delay (out to the load and back to the midpoint).
2. **Ratio gives $\Gamma_L$:** second weight over first weight, then $R_L = Z_0(1+\Gamma_L)/(1-\Gamma_L)$.
3. **"Nothing else" gives $\Gamma_g$:** the echo that returns to the generator is not re-launched, so the generator is matched.
4. **$A$** is the launched weight, $\tau_g$ times the source weight, with the $R_g$ just found.
5. $\epsilon_r = (c/v)^2$ for $\mu = \mu_0$.

**Traps.** The source impulse is 30, not $A$: half of it can stay across $R_g$. A missing third pulse is information.

**Twins.** [[practice/29-bounce-diagram-examples#298-one-pulse-and-nothing-else|29.8 One pulse and nothing else]] · [[practice/30-multi-line-circuits#3010-locating-a-splice-by-its-echoes|30.10 Locating a splice by its echoes]] · [[practice/27-transmission-line-basics#277-two-bench-measurements|27.7 Two bench measurements]] (the filling from a delay) · worked problem [[problems/reading-a-line-from-two-pulses|Worked problem — Reading a line from two pulses]]

## 5. A shorted line: steady state and a pulse (24 points)

**Asks.** $R_g = Z_0 = 60\ \Omega$, $v = 300$ m/µs, 300 m long, short-circuited. (a), (b) Steady-state $V(0)$ and $I(\ell)$ for a 240 V step. (c) For a 240 V pulse lasting 6 µs: sketch $V(0,t)$ and $I(0,t)$ for 11 µs, with the axis scales.

**Method.** [[4-transmission-lines/28-distributed-circuits-and-bounce-diagrams|Lecture 28]] · [[4-transmission-lines/29-bounce-diagram-examples|Lecture 29]]
1. **Steady state of a step is DC:** replace the line by two wires. The answers are a one-line circuit.
2. **Coefficients:** $\tau_g$, $\Gamma_L$ of a short, $\Gamma_g$ of a matched generator. With $\Gamma_g = 0$ there are only two waves: the incident one and one echo.
3. **At $z = 0$** the voltage is incident + echo, the echo delayed by $2T$. For the current, the echo's term is $-\Gamma_L$ times the incident current: at a short the current **doubles** where the voltage cancels.
4. **A pulse is a step minus a delayed step.** Sketch the step response, shift it by the pulse length, subtract.

**Traps.** Voltage and current echoes have opposite signs. The echo reaches $z = 0$ at $2T$, not $T$. After the pulse ends the echo is still on its way back: the voltage at $z = 0$ goes negative for a while.

**Check yourself.** Long after the pulse everything must be zero; during the overlap $V(0)$ must equal the DC value of part (a).

**Twins.** [[practice/28-bounce-diagrams#2810-a-pulse-into-a-resistive-load|28.10 A pulse into a resistive load]] · [[practice/29-bounce-diagram-examples#2910-a-ramp-into-an-open-line|29.10 A ramp into an open line]] · [[practice/28-bounce-diagrams#288-snapshots-along-a-shorted-line|28.8 Snapshots along a shorted line]]

## 6. Power against depth in water (10 points)

**Asks.** $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = \hat x\,5e^{-0.2z}e^{-j0.2z}$ V/m going down into water, with $\eta$ left as a symbol. (a) The time-averaged power density against depth. (b) The attenuation rate for power.

**Method.** [[concepts/skin-depth|Skin depth]] · [[concepts/time-average-power|Time averages and average power]]
1. Read $\alpha$ and $\beta$ off the exponent. $\alpha = \beta$ is the signature of a good conductor, so $\angle\eta = 45^\circ$.
2. $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \hat u\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}}/\eta$, then $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^*\} = \hat u\,\dfrac{\lvert\tilde E\rvert^2}{2\lvert\eta\rvert}\cos(\angle\eta)$, with $\lvert\tilde E\rvert^2\propto e^{-2\alpha z}$.
3. Power falls as $e^{-2\alpha z}$: $2\alpha$ in Np/m, or $10\log_{10}e^{2\alpha} = 8.686\,\alpha$ dB per metre.

**Traps.** $e^{-\alpha z}$ is the field; the power exponent is doubled. The $\cos\angle\eta$ factor. No calculator: leave $\log_{10}e$ unless told otherwise.

**Twins.** [[practice/23-imperfect-dielectrics-and-good-conductors#235-power-against-depth-in-seawater|23.5 Power against depth in seawater]] · [[practice/22-damped-waves-in-conducting-media#228-power-soaking-into-lossy-ground|22.8 Power soaking into lossy ground]]

## 7. Resonances and standing-wave shapes (16 points)

> [!note] Not on FA26 Midterm 3
> This problem is Lectures 31–33, and this semester's Midterm 3 stops at Lecture 30. Skip it when you work the paper for Monday; it is kept here for later.

**Asks.** A 1000 m line with $v = 10^8$ m/s in sinusoidal steady state has $\tilde I_{\text{in}} = 0$ and $\tilde V_L = 0$. (a) $Z_{\text{in}}$ and $Z_L$. (b) The two lowest resonant $\omega$, and which of twelve plotted shapes are $\tilde V(d)$ and $\tilde I(d)$. (c) At the second resonance, the shortest distance from the load where the line is a short-circuit input, in wavelengths.

**Method.** [[exams/e3-steady-state-lines#2-resonances-from-the-end-conditions|Steady-state lines §2]]
1. Zero voltage is a short, zero current an open.
2. Unlike ends hold an odd number of quarter waves: $\ell = (2n-1)\lambda/4$, then $\omega = 2\pi v/\lambda$.
3. Shapes: from the short at the load, $\lvert\tilde V\rvert\propto\lvert\sin\beta d\rvert$ (zero at $d = 0$) and $\lvert\tilde I\rvert\propto\lvert\cos\beta d\rvert$ (zero at $d = \ell$). The plots' $d$ axis runs right to left.
4. The line looks like a short at every voltage null.

**Traps.** $\omega$ is asked, not $f$. The first resonance is a quarter wave, not a half.

**Twins.** [[exams/e3-steady-state-lines#s3-resonances-from-two-measurements|S3 Resonances from two measurements]] · [[practice/31-resonances-of-line-segments#317-a-resonator-from-two-zero-readings|31.7 A resonator from two zero readings]]
