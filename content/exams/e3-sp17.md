## 3. Decoding a damped wave (27 points)

**Asks.** $\mathbf{E} = 3e^{-3x}\cos(8\pi10^6t-4x+60^\circ)\hat z$ V/m in a non-magnetic medium; $\gamma\eta = j\omega\mu$, $\gamma/\eta = \sigma+j\omega\epsilon$ and $\tan^{-1}(4/3) = 53^\circ$ are given. Find $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$; $\lvert\gamma\rvert$ and $\angle\gamma$; $\lambda$, $f$, $v_p$; $\lvert\eta\rvert$ and $\tau$; and $\mathbf{H}$ with its direction.

**Method.** [[concepts/propagation-constant|Propagation constant]] · worked problem [[problems/decoding-a-damped-wave|Worked problem — Decoding a damped wave]]
1. $\alpha$ is in the envelope $e^{-\alpha x}$ [Np/m], $\beta$ multiplies $x$ in the cosine [rad/m], $\gamma = \alpha+j\beta$. A 3-4-5 triangle gives the polar form.
2. $\omega$ is the coefficient of $t$: $f = \omega/2\pi$, $\lambda = 2\pi/\beta$, $v_p = \omega/\beta$.
3. $\eta = j\omega\mu_0/\gamma$: $\lvert\eta\rvert = \omega\mu_0/\lvert\gamma\rvert$, $\tau = \angle\eta = 90^\circ-\angle\gamma$.
4. $\mathbf{H}$: amplitude $\lvert E\rvert/\lvert\eta\rvert$, same envelope, phase **reduced** by $\tau$, direction $\hat u\times\hat E$ with $\hat u = +\hat x$.

**Traps.** Units on every blank (Np/m, rad/m, m, Hz, m/s, Ω, degrees). $\hat x\times\hat z = -\hat y$. Leave $\pi$'s as they are: no calculator.

**Twins.** [[practice/22-damped-waves-in-conducting-media#229-decoding-a-wave-toward-minus-y|22.9 Decoding a wave toward minus y]] · [[practice/22-damped-waves-in-conducting-media#223-reading-a-damped-wave|22.3 Reading a damped wave]] (warm-up) · worked problem [[problems/decoding-a-damped-wave|Worked problem — Decoding a damped wave]]

## 4. A two-component wave: phasors, power, handedness (18 points)

**Asks.** $\mathbf{E} = E_x\cos(\omega t-\beta z)\hat x+E_y\sin(\omega t-\beta z+\phi)\hat y$ in a lossless medium. (a) The phasors $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}$. (b) $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle$. (c) Which of four conditions on $E_x$, $E_y$, $\phi$ makes it left-circular.

**Method.** [[concepts/phasors|Phasors]] · [[concepts/wave-polarization|Wave polarization]]
1. Turn the sine into a cosine first: $\sin\theta = \cos(\theta-\pi/2)$, so the $\hat y$ phasor carries $e^{j(\phi-\pi/2)}$.
2. $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \hat z\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}}/\eta$, component by component: $\hat x\to\hat y$, $\hat y\to-\hat x$.
3. $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \tfrac12\text{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^*\}$: the cross terms vanish and the two components' powers add, whatever $\phi$ is.
4. Circular: equal magnitudes and a quarter-cycle difference **after** step 1. Left-handed for travel along $+z$: the $\hat y$ component leads (leading × lagging $= \hat y\times\hat x = -\hat z$). A negative amplitude is a phase of $\pi$.

**Traps.** The sine hides a $-\pi/2$: $\phi = \pi/2$ makes the wave linear, not circular. Test each option by tracking the field at $z = 0$ for $\omega t = 0$ and $\pi/2$.

**Twins.** [[practice/24-group-velocity-and-polarization#2410-a-wave-with-a-free-phase|24.10 A wave with a free phase]] · [[practice/24-group-velocity-and-polarization#245-the-forgotten-travel-direction|24.5 The forgotten travel direction]] · worked problem [[problems/phasors-e-and-power-from-a-given-h|Worked problem — Phasors, E and power from a given H]]

## 5. Bounce diagram and two snapshots (20 points)

**Asks.** $Z_0 = 50\ \Omega$, $\ell = 750$ m, $v = 250$ m/µs, $R_g = 100\ \Omega$, $R_L = 25\ \Omega$. (a) $\tau_g$, $\Gamma_g$, $\Gamma_L$. (b) The voltage bounce diagram to 10 µs for a unit step. (c) For a source that is 1 µs of a sine at $\tfrac7{12}$ MHz (peak $+1$ V, ending at $-0.5$ V): plot $V$ against $z$ at $t = 2$ µs and at $t = 4$ µs, with scales.

**Method.** [[4-transmission-lines/29-bounce-diagram-examples|Lecture 29]] · worked problem [[problems/a-ramp-pulse-meets-its-echo|Worked problem — A ramp pulse meets its echo]]
1. Coefficients as in [[exams/e3-sp18#3-voltage-and-current-bounce-diagrams-30-points|SP18 #3]]; $T = \ell/v$.
2. Step diagram: label each segment with its weight, $\tau_g$, $\tau_g\Gamma_L$, $\tau_g\Gamma_L\Gamma_g$, …
3. Snapshot of a burst: the forward wave is $\tau_gf(t-z/v)$. Its front is at $z = vt$ and its tail at $z = v(t-1\ \mu\text{s})$; the part emitted **first** is farthest along, so the waveform appears mirrored in $z$. Mark three points of $f$ (start, peak, end) and place each.
4. After the tail has passed the load, only the echo is on the line: $\Gamma_L\tau_gf$ travelling backward, measured back from the load. A negative $\Gamma_L$ flips it.

**Traps.** Mirror image in $z$. The vertical scale shrinks by $\tau_g$ and then by $\Gamma_L$. At $t = 4$ µs the incident burst has just left: do not draw it.

**Twins.** [[practice/29-bounce-diagram-examples#299-one-sine-cycle-on-a-mismatched-line|29.9 One sine cycle on a mismatched line]] · [[practice/28-bounce-diagrams#281-three-coefficients|28.1 Three coefficients]] · [[practice/28-bounce-diagrams#288-snapshots-along-a-shorted-line|28.8 Snapshots along a shorted line]] · worked problem [[problems/a-ramp-pulse-meets-its-echo|Worked problem — A ramp pulse meets its echo]]

## 6. A shorted line in series with a resistor (11 points)

> [!note] Not on FA26 Midterm 3
> This problem is Lectures 31–33, and this semester's Midterm 3 stops at Lecture 30. Skip it when you work the paper for Monday; it is kept here for later.

**Asks.** A shorted line ($v = 2\times10^8$ m/s) in series with $R = 50\ \Omega$ and a source $\tilde F = 10$ V at $\omega = \pi\times10^8$ rad/s. (a) $\lambda$. (b) The resistor current is zero: $Z(\ell)$; the shortest $\ell$ in wavelengths; whether $\tilde V(\ell/2) = 0$ is possible; and whether an inductor that restores the current must be in series or in parallel.

**Method.** [[exams/e3-steady-state-lines#3-quarter-wave-and-half-wave-sections|Steady-state lines, the recipe]]
1. $\lambda = v/f$, $f = \omega/2\pi$.
2. Zero series current: the input is an open circuit, $Z(\ell) = jZ_0\tan\beta\ell = \infty$: the first solution is a quarter wave.
3. A voltage null at $d = \ell/2$ forces $\sin(\beta\ell/2) = 0$. What does that make $\sin\beta\ell$, and therefore $Z(\ell)$? Compare with step 2.
4. Series with an open circuit is still open; a parallel element provides a path.

**Twins.** [[exams/e3-steady-state-lines#s1-an-open-stub-that-blocks-the-source|S1 An open stub that blocks the source]] · [[practice/32-stubs-and-input-impedance#327-a-shorted-line-that-stops-the-current|32.7 A shorted line that stops the current]]
