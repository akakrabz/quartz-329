## 2. Working back from a transmitted wave (20 points)

**Asks.** Free space for $y<0$, a perfect dielectric with $\mu = \mu_0$ for $y>0$. The transmitted phasor is $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_t = 10e^{-j12y}\hat z$ V/m at $\omega = 3\times10^9$ rad/s; $\eta\gamma = j\omega\mu$ is given. Find $\gamma$ in free space, $\gamma_t$, $\eta_t$, then $\Gamma$ and $\tau$, then the incident and reflected phasors.

**Method.** [[concepts/reflection-and-transmission|Reflection and transmission coefficients]]
1. Lossless regions: $\gamma = j\beta$. Free space: $\beta = \omega/c$. Region 2: read $\beta_t$ off the exponent.
2. $\eta_t = j\omega\mu/\gamma_t = \omega\mu_0/\beta_t$ (real). Free space: $\eta_0 = 120\pi\ \Omega$.
3. $\Gamma = (\eta_2-\eta_1)/(\eta_2+\eta_1)$ with region 1 the side the wave comes **from**; $\tau = 1+\Gamma$.
4. Work backward at the interface: $E_i = E_t/\tau$, $E_r = \Gamma E_i$. The incident phasor carries $e^{-j\beta_1y}$, the reflected one $e^{+j\beta_1y}$, both with region 1's $\beta$.

**Traps.** Using $\beta_t$ in region 1. Forgetting that the reflected wave travels toward $-y$. A negative $\Gamma$ (denser medium ahead) flips the reflected field's sign.

**Check yourself.** $E_i+E_r = E_t$ at $y = 0$, and the power balance $\lvert E_i\rvert^2/\eta_1 = \lvert E_r\rvert^2/\eta_1+\lvert E_t\rvert^2/\eta_2$.

## 3. Voltage and current bounce diagrams (30 points)

**Asks.** $Z_0 = 50\ \Omega$, $\ell = 15$ cm, $v_p = \tfrac23c$, $R_g = 30\ \Omega$, $R_L = 150\ \Omega$, $V_g = 10u(t)$ V. (a) The injection coefficient and both reflection coefficients. (b) Voltage and current bounce diagrams to 3 ns. (c), (d) Plots of the source-end and load-end voltages to 2 ns, with numbers on the axis.

**Method.** [[4-transmission-lines/28-distributed-circuits-and-bounce-diagrams|Lecture 28]] · [[concepts/bounce-diagram|Bounce diagram]]
1. $T = \ell/v_p$ sets the time grid. $\tau_g = Z_0/(R_g+Z_0)$, $\Gamma_g = (R_g-Z_0)/(R_g+Z_0)$, $\Gamma_L = (R_L-Z_0)/(R_L+Z_0)$.
2. Voltage diagram: launch $\tau_gV_g$, multiply by $\Gamma_L$ at the load and by $\Gamma_g$ at the source, alternately.
3. Current diagram: the first segment is $V_1^+/Z_0$; every reflection multiplies by $-\Gamma$.
4. **Voltage at an end = sum of every segment that has touched that end so far.** At the load, the incident segment and its echo appear at the same instant: the first step is $(1+\Gamma_L)$ times the launched wave. At the source, the launched wave is there from $t = 0$ and each returning wave adds itself plus its re-reflection.

**Traps.** The current coefficients have the opposite signs. At the ends two segments meet: count both.

**Key note: part (d).** The official key labels the load-end voltage with the launched value alone. Its own part (c) uses the echo of that wave, so the load-end level for $0.75<t<2$ ns should be $(1+\Gamma_L)$ times the launched wave, $\tfrac{75}{8}$ V $= 9.375$ V; a line simulation confirms it ([[0-toolkit/04-errata-in-the-course-materials#past-exam-3-solution-keys|errata]]). The rest of the key agrees with the simulation.

**Check yourself.** Both end voltages must tend to the DC divider value $V_gR_L/(R_g+R_L)$.

## 4. A line in series with a resistor (20 points)

> [!note] Not on FA26 Midterm 3
> This problem is Lectures 31–33, and this semester's Midterm 3 stops at Lecture 30. Skip it when you work the paper for Monday; it is kept here for later.

**Asks.** A 50 m segment ($v = 200$ m/µs, $Z_0 = 50\ \Omega$) in series with $R = 50\ \Omega$ and a source $2\angle0$ V. (a) With $Z_L = 0$ the resistor current is zero: find **all** possible wavelengths. (b) With $Z_L = 100\ \Omega$ at the frequency where $\ell = \lambda/4$: $Z_{\text{in}}$ and $\tilde V_{\text{in}}$.

**Method.** [[exams/e3-steady-state-lines|Steady-state lines]]
1. Zero current in a series loop: the line's input is an open circuit. A shorted line is open at its input when it is an odd number of quarter waves long: solve $\ell = (2n+1)\lambda/4$ for $\lambda$, keeping $n$.
2. Quarter-wave section: $Z_{\text{in}} = Z_0^2/Z_L$, then a voltage divider with $R$.

**Traps.** "All possible" wants the whole family, with the index. In (b) the resistor current is no longer zero.
